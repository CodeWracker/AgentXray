4. Agent_03_propose_whole_image_CLAHE
QUESTION IT TRIES TO ANSWER:
Can a single reprocessed version of the full image simultaneously show good detail in both the darker/well-penetrated proximal arm and the brighter/overexposed distal forearm-hand region, to support a more holistic read without switching between multiple crops?
WHY THIS METHOD MAY HELP:
Contrast-Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) adjusts contrast locally rather than globally, which should even out the wide exposure range seen across this single image (well-penetrated shaft vs. bright hand) better than a single global window/level.
WHAT IT EXPECTS TO REVEAL:
A more uniformly interpretable full-image view that could make the fracture line, joint contours, and hand outlines all reasonably visible at once, useful as a general-purpose companion to the original for other agents/readers.
POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS / MISLEADING EFFECTS:
CLAHE can exaggerate local noise and create artificial "texture" or halo effects around high-contrast edges (e.g., bone margins), and can make normal soft-tissue variation look more structured/abnormal than it is. It should not be used as the sole basis for identifying a finding, only as a visibility aid.
HOW TO COMPARE WITH ORIGINAL:
Place CLAHE output next to the original at matched scale; treat any finding visible only in CLAHE and not at all traceable (even faintly) in the original with extra skepticism as a possible enhancement artifact.
