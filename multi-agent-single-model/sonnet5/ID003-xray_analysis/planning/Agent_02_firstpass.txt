3. POSSIBLE ABNORMALITIES (hedged)
- Ulna, mid-shaft: a thin, obliquely oriented lucent line crosses the shaft cortex-to-cortex. There is no obvious displacement or step-off. This is most consistent with a nondisplaced (hairline) fracture, though a nutrient foramen or a summation/Mach-band artifact from overlapping soft tissue cannot be fully excluded without a second projection.
- Radius, mid-shaft (same level as the ulnar line, slightly proximal): a more convincing focal cortical discontinuity is present — the proximal fragment tapers to a sharp "beak," with a corresponding notch in the distal fragment and mild angulation/apposition at the fracture site. This pattern is more consistent with a complete, mildly angulated/displaced fracture than a subtle finding.
- Taken together, the two findings at approximately the same forearm level raise concern for a both-bone (radius + ulna) forearm fracture, with the radius component appearing more displaced/angulated than the ulna component. This should be treated as a preliminary visual impression only — true assessment of displacement, angulation, and completeness requires orthogonal (AP + lateral) views, which are not confirmed to be present here.
- No obvious abnormality is identified at the elbow joint itself (joint spaces appear preserved, no gross effusion sign clearly visible), though image quality/foreshortening limits confidence.
- Hand/finger bones: alignment cannot be reliably assessed because digits are flexed, overlapped, and motion-blurred; no fracture can be excluded or confirmed in this region from this view alone.

- The hand and wrist are markedly foreshortened due to a flexed/fisted posture, causing extensive bone overlap — individual carpal and phalangeal anatomy cannot be resolved.
- Contrast/exposure across the image is uneven: forearm bones are well penetrated, but the torso portion at the right edge is relatively overexposed (bright, low contrast), and the far-field background is very dark with sharp collimation edges, suggesting tight collimation and possibly high kVp/mAs suited to the forearm but not optimized for the (incidentally captured) trunk.
- No visible patient marker (L/R side marker) or technique/ID label in the visible frame, limiting laterality confirmation.
- Faint curvilinear, evenly spaced parallel lines are visible over the torso near the bottom-right of the image, arcing with the body contour — most consistent with a soft-tissue/skin fold or an external wrap/strap (e.g., an immobilization band), rather than a bony or pulmonary finding; discussed further as a possible artifact.
- Single projection only (as received) — no orthogonal view is included in this file, which limits fracture characterization (true displacement/angulation/rotation cannot be assessed from one plane).
