PURPOSE: This is the same forearm mid-shaft crop as the fracture-zone image but with unsharp-mask edge enhancement instead of plain contrast stretch, produced to answer: are the margins of the candidate fracture line(s) sharp/well-corticated (favoring true fracture) or soft/gradual (favoring blur, summation, or nutrient canal)?
OBSERVATIONS: A tight vertical crop showing two parallel forearm long bones (radius on the right, ulna on the left in this projection), mid-shaft, with the proximal elbow region cut off at the very top. On the right-hand bone, roughly in the upper third of the crop, there is a clear cortical discontinuity: the bone shows a step-like offset where the shaft above the break line is not perfectly co-linear with the shaft below it, and both the medial and lateral cortical margins show a sharp, angulated break with a jagged/serrated interruption rather than a smooth taper. The lucent line itself is short, irregular, and has crisp, high-contrast edges after the sharpening. Lower down and slightly to the left, on the other bone, there is a second, thinner, more obliquely oriented lucent line crossing the shaft; its margins appear thinner and more uniform, without any visible step-off or angulation of the surrounding cortex, and it runs a fairly straight, smooth oblique course across the bone width.
RELEVANT FINDINGS: The right-bone lesion has the classic appearance of an acute fracture: sharp, well-corticated but disrupted margins, an irregular/jagged fracture line, and a visible step/angulation between fragments (i.e., not just a line but a true discontinuity in bone alignment). The left-bone thinner oblique line, in contrast, has smoother, less disrupted margins and no fragment offset, which is more compatible with a nutrient canal or a nondisplaced/incomplete fracture line — the edge enhancement does not resolve this ambiguity definitively.
RELATION TO ORIGINAL IMAGE: Yes — in 00_original.png the same mid-shaft region of the forearm shows a visible angulated lucent line with slight offset at the corresponding location on the same bone, consistent with what is seen here. The second, thinner oblique line on the neighboring bone is present but much harder to appreciate in the original due to lower local contrast; it becomes clearly visible only after this enhancement.
POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: Unsharp-mask sharpening can create halo/ringing artifacts along high-contrast edges (bone-to-background and cortex-to-medulla boundaries), which can make a normal thin structure (like a nutrient canal) look crisper and more "fracture-like" than it truly is, and can also exaggerate the apparent sharpness of the true fracture margins. The apparent step/angulation on the right bone is large and consistent enough that it is unlikely to be purely a sharpening artifact, but the thin oblique line on the left bone should be interpreted cautiously since enhancement could be manufacturing apparent crispness from a low-contrast, possibly normal structure.
