COUNCIL DELIBERATION SUMMARY
===============================================================================

CONSENSUS OBSERVATIONS (multiple independent agents, same model — noted as
consistency, NOT independent cross-model validation):
- All 6 agents independently identified the image as a lateral plain-film
  radiograph spanning distal tibia/fibula, ankle mortise, and hindfoot/
  midfoot, with the forefoot partially cropped at the right edge.
- All 6 agents state that no obviously displaced fracture, dislocation, or
  gross destructive lesion is apparent, but all qualify this as low-confidence
  given contrast/noise/resolution limitations and single-view-only projection.
- All 6 agents flagged compressed mid-tone contrast and visible background
  grain/noise as limiting factors.
- 4/6 agents (02, 04, 05, 06) independently noted a background-region
  artifact (a lighter rectangular/vignette area or border distinct from a
  clean black collimated field) and flagged it as needing characterization
  before trusting any brightness-based comparison elsewhere in the image.
- 5/6 agents (02, 03, 04, 05, 06) specifically called out the overlapping
  tarsal/ankle-mortise region as the highest-value target for magnification,
  since superimposition there could hide a subtle finding.
- Agent_02 specifically flagged a transverse lucent band crossing the distal
  tibial metaphysis, most likely a normal physeal (growth plate) line, but
  requiring closer inspection to rule out an irregular/discontinuous margin.
  Agent_04 independently noted "non-uniform density along the tibial shaft"
  in the same general area, a softer echo of the same observation.
- 4/6 agents (03, 05, 06, and implicitly 01) described the calcaneus/talus
  trabecular texture as coarser or more "mottled" than the tibial shaft,
  while explicitly noting this is expected normal overlap/texture and not
  necessarily pathological.

PROPOSAL CONVERGENCE (used to set priority, not correctness):
- Local/adaptive contrast enhancement (CLAHE): proposed independently by
  ALL 6 agents. Highest priority.
- Edge enhancement / unsharp masking (+ edge map): proposed independently by
  ALL 6 agents. Highest priority.
- ROI crop/zoom/magnification of ankle mortise + hindfoot: proposed by 5/6
  agents (02, 03, 04, 05, 06). High priority.
- Histogram/intensity-range analysis + linear contrast stretch: proposed
  explicitly by Agent_05, motivated by the background-artifact question
  raised by 4/6 agents. Elevated priority because it resolves a
  cross-agent disagreement/uncertainty (artifact vs. real feature).
- Noise characterization (background baseline vs. anatomic texture),
  optionally with mild denoising for comparison: proposed by Agent_01 and
  Agent_03 (2/6). Moderate priority — useful to calibrate confidence in the
  CLAHE/edge outputs above, so it is retained despite lower agreement.
- ROI crop of the distal tibial metaphyseal band specifically: motivated
  primarily by Agent_02's specific observation, softly corroborated by
  Agent_04. No agent opposed it; included as targeted follow-up.

No agent proposed anything that another agent flagged as actively
inappropriate or misleading; disagreement here is about priority/emphasis,
not about validity. That absence of opposition is preserved below rather
than manufactured.

===============================================================================
PLANNED ANALYSES
===============================================================================

1. OUTPUT: images/01_clahe_full.png
   TRANSFORM: Contrast-Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) on the
   full image (grayscale), clipLimit=2.5, tileGridSize=8x8.
   QUESTION: Are there subtle cortical, joint-space, or trabecular density
   changes hidden by the image's compressed mid-tone contrast?
   REASON: Unanimous proposal (6/6 agents); addresses the single most common
   quality limitation (compressed contrast) cited by every agent.
   SUPPORTING: Agent_01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 06.
   OPPOSING/QUESTIONING: None opposed; all six warned about the same risk
   (see artifacts).
   POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: Noise amplification producing pseudo-trabecular
   texture; halo/blotchy artifacts at high-contrast bone/background edges
   that could be mistaken for periosteal reaction.
   EXPECTED VALUE: High — directly targets the most-cited limitation.

2. OUTPUT: images/02_unsharp_mask_full.png
   TRANSFORM: Unsharp masking on the full image (Gaussian blur sigma=2,
   amount=1.5) to boost high-frequency cortical edges.
   QUESTION: Are cortical margins of the tibia, fibula, talus, and calcaneus
   truly continuous, or is a subtle discontinuity being smoothed out by the
   image's softness?
   REASON: Unanimous proposal (6/6 agents).
   SUPPORTING: Agent_01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 06.
   OPPOSING/QUESTIONING: None opposed; all six flagged the same risk.
   POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: Ringing/overshoot artifacts paralleling real cortex
   that can mimic a thin fracture line, periosteal reaction, or avulsion
   fragment; exaggeration of normal nutrient canals/vascular grooves.
   EXPECTED VALUE: High — complements CLAHE for cortical-continuity
   assessment.

3. OUTPUT: images/03_edge_map_sobel.png
   TRANSFORM: Sobel gradient-magnitude edge map (full image), normalized and
   inverted for legibility (dark edges on light background).
   QUESTION: Same as #2, presented as an explicit boundary map rather than a
   sharpened grayscale image, to make discontinuities easier to trace.
   REASON: Explicitly requested as a supplementary view by Agent_01, 02, 03,
   05 (4/6), as a companion to unsharp masking rather than a replacement.
   SUPPORTING: Agent_01, 02, 03, 05.
   OPPOSING/QUESTIONING: None opposed; all warned edge maps fire on normal
   bone-overlap lines (fibula over tibia, tarsal overlap), which are not
   pathology.
   POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: Strong response to noise and to normal projectional
   overlap lines; could be misread as fracture lines if not cross-checked.
   EXPECTED VALUE: Moderate-high — most useful in combination with #1/#2,
   not in isolation.

4. OUTPUT: images/04_roi_ankle_hindfoot_zoom.png
   TRANSFORM: Crop to the ankle mortise + hindfoot/midfoot region (tibiotalar
   joint through calcaneus/navicular/cuboid), bicubic upscale 3x, CLAHE
   applied within the crop independently of the full-image version.
   QUESTION: Is the ankle-mortise/hindfoot overlap zone hiding a subtle
   cortical break or joint-space irregularity that is invisible at
   whole-image scale?
   REASON: Proposed by 5/6 agents as the single highest-value ROI given it is
   the most anatomically complex, most overlapped region in this projection.
   SUPPORTING: Agent_02, 03, 04, 05, 06.
   OPPOSING/QUESTIONING: None opposed. Agent_04 cautioned that independent
   local windowing of a small ROI can exaggerate normal overlap as if it
   were joint-space narrowing.
   POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: Interpolation blur/blockiness from upscaling; local
   CLAHE tiling artifacts; apparent joint-space change that is a windowing
   effect, not anatomy.
   EXPECTED VALUE: High — targets the region of greatest diagnostic
   complexity and greatest agent consensus.

5. OUTPUT: images/05_roi_tibial_metaphysis_zoom.png
   TRANSFORM: Crop to the distal tibial metaphysis (the transverse lucent
   band region), bicubic upscale 4x, unprocessed pixel values (no filtering)
   to avoid conflating a real margin with a processing artifact.
   QUESTION: Does the transverse lucent band crossing the distal tibial
   metaphysis have smooth, symmetric, well-corticated margins (consistent
   with a normal physis) or irregular/discontinuous margins?
   REASON: Directly follows Agent_02's specific, well-articulated
   observation, softly corroborated by Agent_04's independent note of
   tibial-shaft density variation in the same general area.
   SUPPORTING: Agent_02 (primary), Agent_04 (partial/independent echo).
   OPPOSING/QUESTIONING: Agents 01, 03, 05, 06 did not independently flag
   this band in their first-pass review; it is not corroborated by the
   majority, so it is treated as a single-agent-driven follow-up, not a
   consensus finding.
   POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: Upscaling interpolation blur could either mask a thin
   irregular margin or create a falsely "smooth" appearance.
   EXPECTED VALUE: Moderate — resolves one specific, well-reasoned but
   minority-sourced hypothesis.

6. OUTPUT: images/06_histogram_and_contrast_stretch.png
   TRANSFORM: (a) Pixel-intensity histogram of the full image; (b) linear
   min-max contrast stretch of the full image to the full 0-255 range.
   QUESTION: Is the image using its full dynamic range, or is contrast
   compressed in a way that could hide or mimic anatomic detail? Is the
   lighter background region a smooth gradient (vignetting) or a hard-edged
   geometric shape (collimation/second exposure/digitization artifact)?
   REASON: Directly resolves the background-artifact question independently
   raised by 4/6 agents, and was explicitly proposed by Agent_05 for exactly
   this purpose.
   SUPPORTING: Agent_05 (explicit); Agent_02, 04, 06 (implicit, via their
   independent artifact observations).
   OPPOSING/QUESTIONING: None opposed.
   POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: Stretching contrast will also amplify noise and any
   scan/digitization banding; the background artifact will become more
   visually prominent and must not be misread as anatomy.
   EXPECTED VALUE: High — this is the analysis most likely to resolve a
   genuine cross-agent point of uncertainty (artifact vs. feature) rather
   than just re-confirming consensus.

7. OUTPUT: images/07_background_artifact_map.png
   TRANSFORM: Isolate and false-color (perceptually uniform colormap) the
   pixel-intensity map of the background/collimated region only, with a
   horizontal and vertical intensity profile plot through the anomalous
   lighter region.
   QUESTION: What is the shape, edge sharpness, and intensity profile of the
   lighter rectangular region noted independently by 4 agents in the
   background — is it consistent with collimation, vignetting, or a
   digitization/scan artifact?
   REASON: Follow-up to #6, going further to characterize the specific
   artifact geometrically since it was raised independently by a majority of
   agents and could otherwise bias interpretation of brightness elsewhere in
   the image.
   SUPPORTING: Derived from independent observations of Agent_02, 04, 05, 06.
   OPPOSING/QUESTIONING: None — this is a quality-control step, not an
   anatomic claim.
   POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: False-color mapping can make a benign gradient look
   more "structured" than it is; must be interpreted only as a
   background/technical-quality characterization, never as anatomy.
   EXPECTED VALUE: Moderate — improves confidence in how much weight to give
   brightness-based findings elsewhere, but does not by itself change any
   anatomic conclusion.

8. OUTPUT: images/08_denoise_comparison.png
   TRANSFORM: Non-local-means denoising of the full image, shown side-by-side
   with the original, plus a difference map (original minus denoised) to show
   exactly what was removed.
   QUESTION: How much of the fine texture seen in bone and background is
   acquisition noise versus real structure? Do any candidate findings from
   #1-#5 survive denoising (more likely real) or vanish (more likely noise)?
   REASON: Proposed by 2/6 agents (Agent_01, Agent_03) as a calibration step
   for interpreting the enhancement outputs above; lower agreement than
   items 1-4 but directly useful for validating them.
   SUPPORTING: Agent_01, Agent_03.
   OPPOSING/QUESTIONING: None opposed, but not corroborated by the other 4
   agents, so treated as a secondary/calibration output rather than a
   primary analysis.
   POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: Denoising can blur or erase genuinely fine but real
   detail (thin cortical margins), creating a false impression that a subtle
   finding "isn't there."
   EXPECTED VALUE: Moderate — supporting/calibration value for interpreting
   items 1-5, not a standalone diagnostic view.
