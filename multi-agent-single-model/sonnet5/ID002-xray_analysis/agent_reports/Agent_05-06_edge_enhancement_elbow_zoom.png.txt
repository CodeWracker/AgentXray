PURPOSE: Side-by-side comparison of the original crop (left) centered on the radial-neck/coronoid/olecranon region versus the same crop after a stronger, tightly-scoped unsharp-mask edge enhancement (right), intended to check for subtle occult fracture lines in the classic high-yield elbow fracture sites, particularly the zone where the radial head overlaps the coronoid process.

OBSERVATIONS: Both halves show the proximal radius/ulna and distal humerus in the same flexed lateral position as image 02, zoomed further into the radial neck, coronoid, and olecranon region. On the right (edge-enhanced), the trabecular pattern throughout the radius, ulna, and humeral condyle is strongly sharpened, producing a dense mesh of fine crossing lines within the cancellous bone. Several features stand out: (1) a near-vertical linear marking running through the soft tissue superficial to the radius shaft, extending from the soft tissue near the radial neck down toward the mid radius; (2) a cluster of short, crossing trabecular-oriented lines concentrated right at the radial-head/coronoid overlap zone, where the radial neck's cortical margin crosses over the coronoid's cortical margin; (3) generally increased "crispness" of the radial neck waist and coronoid tip contours compared to the left.

RELEVANT FINDINGS: (1) The vertical soft-tissue line lateral to the radius shaft has a very faint, barely perceptible counterpart at the same location on the left (original) half — its course and slight obliquity are more consistent with a skin fold or soft-tissue crease than a bone finding, since it crosses soft tissue, not cortex, and does not align with any cortical margin. I do not consider it a fracture-related finding. (2) The crossing lines concentrated at the radial-head/coronoid overlap zone: some of these have faint counterparts in the left-half image, but this exact location is the projectional overlap of the radial neck cortex, coronoid cortex, and the humeral condyle edge — a site where superimposition of three separate bone contours normally produces crossing linear densities on a single 2D projection. I cannot distinguish, with confidence, any single line in this cluster from that expected overlap pattern. Per the verification rule, these are flagged as likely overlap artifact, not a confirmed finding, rather than reported as a probable fracture line. No single discrete lucent line with a corresponding faint left-half counterpart, oriented perpendicular to expected trabecular flow and clearly separate from the overlap zone, was identified.

RELATION TO ORIGINAL IMAGE: The general bone contours and joint alignment in the right half are consistent with the left half; enhancement has not altered gross anatomic relationships. The features noted above are traceable, at least faintly, back to the left/original half, but none of them has an appearance on the left that would independently prompt a fracture concern before enhancement — they only become visually prominent after the stronger unsharp-mask pass.

POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: Aggressive unsharp masking is well known to exaggerate existing low-contrast edges and can generate or accentuate Mach-band-like linear artifacts at points where multiple real edges converge, which is exactly the situation at the radial head/coronoid/condyle overlap zone targeted by this crop. The dense mesh of crossing lines in the cancellous bone is most consistent with amplified normal trabecular pattern rather than trauma. The vertical soft-tissue line is most consistent with a skin-fold/soft-tissue artifact amplified by the sharpening filter.

CONFIDENCE:
low

NEXT STEP: Given the deliberate targeting of the highest anatomic-overlap zone (radial head over coronoid), a follow-up with an orthogonal/AP-equivalent view or oblique projection (if available) would be the only reliable way to separate true cortical disruption from overlap-induced crossing lines — further processing of this same single lateral projection is unlikely to resolve the ambiguity, since the ambiguity stems from projectional superimposition, not from image contrast/sharpness.
