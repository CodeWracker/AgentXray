PURPOSE: Determine whether the elbow anterior/posterior fat pads are normal or show signs of effusion, by comparing the original elbow-joint crop (left) against a CLAHE (local adaptive contrast enhancement) version of the same crop (right).

OBSERVATIONS: Side-by-side crop of the elbow joint. LEFT (original): smooth, low-noise soft tissue shadow anterior and posterior to the joint, humeral condyle, and proximal radius/ulna with a modest triangular dark area anterior to the joint (normal fat pad region), no discrete elevated lucent triangle. RIGHT (CLAHE): the same anatomy is present, but the entire soft-tissue field — not just the joint region — now shows a fine mottled/grainy texture and increased local contrast; trabecular striations in the proximal ulna/olecranon are much more pronounced and "sharper" than on the left; the soft-tissue outline anterior to the joint shows more textured variation but no single well-defined, discretely elevated triangular lucency distinct from the surrounding noise pattern.

RELEVANT FINDINGS: No convincing discrete fat-pad elevation or posterior fat pad sign (the classic finding for elbow joint effusion) is seen. The increased texture/contrast in the anterior soft tissue on the right half is diffuse and uniform across the whole soft-tissue field (including areas overlying the mid-shaft, distant from the joint capsule), which argues against a focal, clinically meaningful fat-pad finding and favors a global processing effect.

RELATION TO ORIGINAL IMAGE: The faint, non-bulging anterior fat pad shadow seen on the right (CLAHE) half is also faintly present on the LEFT (unprocessed) half, so that baseline shadow is real anatomy, not fabricated. However, the amplified graininess/texture and sharper trabecular striations seen only on the right are NOT present on the left half or in the 00_original.png, and should not be interpreted as a real posterior fat pad sign or effusion.

POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: Classic CLAHE noise amplification — the algorithm boosts local contrast uniformly across tiles, which exaggerates quantum noise and normal soft-tissue texture, making it look more "mottled" or "textured" than it truly is. This can create the visual impression of a fat pad or fluid line that is actually just enhanced noise/trabecular texture. Any apparent posterior fat pad-like density on the right half that cannot be matched to a corresponding (even faint) shadow on the left half should be treated as a likely CLAHE artifact.

CONFIDENCE:
moderate

NEXT STEP: Yes — a true window/level adjustment (linear, not CLAHE) or comparison against a contralateral/normal elbow, plus a second orthogonal view if available, would better resolve whether any subtle posterior fat pad is real, since CLAHE alone is not reliable for confirming or excluding an effusion sign.
