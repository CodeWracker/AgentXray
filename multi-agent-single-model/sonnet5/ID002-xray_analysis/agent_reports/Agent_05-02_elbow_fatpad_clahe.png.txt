PURPOSE: Side-by-side comparison of the original elbow-joint crop (left) versus the same crop after CLAHE local-contrast enhancement (right), intended to test whether an anterior or posterior fat-pad/joint-effusion sign becomes visible with improved local soft-tissue contrast.

OBSERVATIONS: Both halves show a lateral elbow view with distal humerus (trochlea/capitellum), proximal radius, and proximal ulna (olecranon/coronoid) in a flexed position. On the left (original), the soft tissue anterior and posterior to the joint is a smooth, fairly uniform mid-gray with no discrete triangular lucency lifted off the bone. On the right (CLAHE), bone trabecular texture is much more pronounced (cortex and cancellous striations in the humerus, radius, and ulna are sharply brought out), and the soft-tissue background gains a faint mottled/grainy texture, but I do not see a discrete, well-demarcated triangular or "sail-shaped" lucent structure elevated away from the anterior or posterior humeral cortex in either half. There is a faint, subtle curvilinear lucent line paralleling the anterior distal humeral cortex in the right-half image, slightly more conspicuous than on the left, but it stays close to and parallels the cortex rather than forming an elevated triangular fat pad.

RELEVANT FINDINGS: No convincing posterior fat pad ("sail sign") or grossly elevated anterior fat pad is seen in either half. The faint anterior lucent line noted above is the only soft-tissue feature of note; it is subtle and its contour does not clearly depart from the normal anterior fat stripe position, so I do not consider it a confident positive finding for effusion.

RELATION TO ORIGINAL IMAGE: The faint anterior lucent line has a very faint, plausible counterpart in the left (original) half at the same location along the anterior humeral cortex, so it is not purely a right-half-only artifact. However, because it is so subtle on the left and only becomes clearly traceable after CLAHE, I cannot confidently call it a true elevated fat pad versus normal anterior soft-tissue planes made more visible by local contrast stretching.

POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: CLAHE is a local-contrast technique and is well known to amplify noise and faint density transitions in relatively uniform soft-tissue regions, which can create the appearance of new lines or textures (including pseudo fat-pad-like lucencies) that do not represent true anatomic boundaries. The increased graininess of the soft-tissue background on the right is consistent with this kind of amplification rather than a true pathological structure. Bone trabecular contrast is also exaggerated, which could make normal trabecular patterns near the joint look more "irregular" than they are.

CONFIDENCE:
low

NEXT STEP: A dedicated, higher-resolution crop centered tightly on just the anterior and posterior fat-pad regions (without CLAHE, using only mild windowing) would help determine whether the faint anterior line is a genuine, mildly displaced fat pad or simply normal anatomy. Comparison with the true unprocessed original image at full resolution (not just this crop) is recommended before drawing any conclusion about effusion.
