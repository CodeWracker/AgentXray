PROCESSING PLAN — ID002-xray.png
Synthesized by: Orchestrator, from independent first-pass reviews of Agent_01–Agent_06
and independent council critiques of Council_Reviewer_A and Council_Reviewer_B.
Context: university research/testing exercise on multi-agent LLM image-analysis
pipelines. This plan and its outputs are NOT a clinical read and are not intended
to substitute for professional radiological interpretation.

================================================================================
BASIS FOR THIS PLAN
================================================================================
All 6 independent first-pass agents converged on the same reading: a lateral
radiograph of a flexed elbow extending through the forearm (radius/ulna) toward
a partially-cropped wrist, no obvious displaced fracture, dislocation, or focal
lesion on visual review, with residual uncertainty concentrated in two places:
(1) whether the elbow anterior/posterior fat pads are normal or show an occult
effusion sign, and (2) whether the cortex (especially at the radial neck,
coronoid, and olecranon — classic occult-fracture sites) is truly continuous
given an exposure setting that appears optimized for bone over soft tissue.

Two independent council reviewers (A and B) then critiqued the combined set of
proposals. Both converged on grouping the 6 agents' ~20 individual proposals into
~5 underlying techniques, both promoted Agent_02's single-agent histogram/
clipping check to a high priority despite its "minority" status, both flagged
Group B (edge enhancement) as carrying the highest artifact risk in the set
despite being the other 6/6-supported proposal, and both independently
recommended dropping Agent_05's intensity-profile-sampling proposal as
redundant/confounded. Their one disagreement was whether the unfiltered
magnified joint crop deserves its own priority slot (Reviewer B) or should be
folded into the contrast-enhancement step's presentation (Reviewer A) — this
plan keeps it as a distinct, separately-inspectable output, since it costs
almost nothing to keep separable and helps isolate which processing step
produced any given visual impression.

No agent, in Section A or Section B, proposed a global/whole-image generic
filter (global histogram equalization, full-frame edge detection, pseudocolor/
heatmap recoloring). Agent_01 explicitly considered and rejected these; both
council reviewers independently affirmed that rejection as a design constraint.
This plan does not include any such global generic filter.

ANALYSIS 1
Output filename: images/01_elbow_joint_crop.png
Transformation: Crop to a bounding box around the distal humerus / elbow joint
  / proximal radius-ulna region, then upscale (bicubic) ~3x. No contrast or
  filtering applied — pure geometric crop + resample.
Parameters: crop box determined from visual landmark detection of the joint
  region; upscale factor 3x; bicubic interpolation.
Question investigated: Does closer, larger-scale framing of the joint change
  the "unremarkable" impression from the whole-limb view, given the joint
  occupies a small fraction of total frame pixels?
Reason for performing: Serves as the substrate/base image for Analyses 2 and 5
  (enhancement and edge work are more legible and easier to audit when run on
  a legible crop rather than a postage-stamp region embedded in a large black
  background), and stands alone as a zero-risk inspection aid.
Agents supporting: Agent_01 (P3, proposed it). Council_Reviewer_B explicitly
  elevated it to priority #3, calling it "the natural substrate" for later
  steps. Council_Reviewer_A downgraded it to a "presentation layer, not a
  standalone analysis" rather than opposing it outright.
Agents opposing/questioning: Council_Reviewer_A (methodological point only —
  argues it adds no new diagnostic information beyond the original pixels,
  per Agent_01's own admission; not a safety objection).
Possible artifacts: Interpolation blur/blockiness from upscaling can look like
  texture or trabecular change; this is display resampling, not new data.
Expected informational value: Low on its own; high as a base for Analyses 2/5,
  and useful as an explicit record of "this is the same pixels, just larger."

ANALYSIS 2
Output filename: images/02_elbow_fatpad_clahe.png
Transformation: Contrast-Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE)
  applied within a bounding box around the distal humerus and the anterior/
  posterior joint capsule region (built from Analysis 1's crop), leaving bone
  cortex intensity outside a soft-tissue-focused band relatively unchanged.
Parameters: CLAHE clip limit ~2.0, tile grid 8x8, applied to the joint-region
  crop; output shown alongside the matching original-crop for comparison.
Question investigated: Are the anterior/posterior elbow fat pads normal, or
  displaced/effaced/bulging in a way that would indicate an occult joint
  effusion (and therefore possible occult fracture) not visible as a bone
  line?
Reason for performing: This was the single most convergent proposal in the
  entire process — independently proposed by ALL SIX first-pass agents (as
  "dual-range windowing," "local/adaptive contrast enhancement," "soft-tissue
  window," "CLAHE crop," "joint-region contrast redistribution," and
  "localized soft-tissue contrast enhancement" — functionally the same
  operation) and endorsed by both council reviewers as the highest-value item
  in the set, because bone and soft tissue compete for the same 8-bit display
  range under one global contrast setting.
Agents supporting: Agent_01, Agent_02, Agent_03, Agent_04, Agent_05, Agent_06
  (6/6). Council_Reviewer_A (ranked #1). Council_Reviewer_B (ranked #2, just
  under the histogram gate).
Agents opposing/questioning: None opposed the technique itself. Both council
  reviewers required it run only after Analysis 4 (histogram/clipping check)
  establishes whether the target region has recoverable (non-clipped) signal.
Possible artifacts: CLAHE can amplify noise/compression blocking into false
  texture, and can produce halo/ringing at the bone/soft-tissue boundary that
  looks like a soft-tissue edge; a skin fold or triceps tendon fat plane can
  be mistaken for a true fat-pad stripe. Mitigation (enforced at inspection
  stage): any apparent fat-pad finding must be checked for anatomic
  plausibility (smooth crescentic shape, expected location) and must have at
  least a faint counterpart in the unprocessed original before being reported
  as a real finding, not an artifact.
Expected informational value: High — directly addresses the single
  highest-yield open question identified across all 6 independent reviews.

ANALYSIS 3
Output filename: images/03_dualrange_window.png
Transformation: Whole-image piecewise/dual-range intensity remap — split the
  intensity histogram into a "soft tissue" low/mid band and a "bone" high
  band, apply a more aggressive stretch to the soft-tissue band while
  compressing the already-saturated bone band, recombine.
Parameters: split point set near the histogram valley between soft-tissue and
  bone-dominated intensities (determined empirically from Analysis 4's
  histogram); separate gamma/stretch curves per band.
Question investigated: Same clinical question as Analysis 2 (fat-pad/effusion
  visibility), via an alternative, whole-image (non-ROI-cropped) technique, to
  cross-check whether the ROI-limited approach and a global piecewise approach
  agree.
Reason for performing: Agent_01's originally-proposed variant of the Group A
  question; both council reviewers treated this as a secondary/comparison
  technique for the same underlying question as Analysis 2, not a separate
  deliverable — kept here as an optional cross-check, not the primary method.
Agents supporting: Agent_01 (originating proposal). Council_Reviewer_A
  ("optional secondary/comparison technique"). Council_Reviewer_B (folds it
  into Group A, same verdict).
Agents opposing/questioning: Council_Reviewer_A and B both prefer the
  ROI-limited method (Analysis 2) as primary specifically because whole-image
  remapping carries more risk of altering the already well-exposed bone
  regions.
Possible artifacts: Visible banding/step at the band-split boundary if not
  smoothly blended; same halo/noise-amplification risks as Analysis 2 but
  applied globally instead of locally.
Expected informational value: Moderate — a useful cross-check against
  Analysis 2, lower priority than Analysis 2 itself.

ANALYSIS 4
Output filename: images/04_intensity_histogram.png
Transformation: Pixel-intensity histogram (matplotlib plot, not a modified
  radiograph) of (a) the whole image and (b) the proximal/bright ROI (humerus/
  joint region) specifically, to test for hard clipping at the maximum
  intensity value vs. a merely-compressed-but-recoverable distribution.
Parameters: 256-bin histograms, log-scale y-axis, ROI boundary matched to
  Analysis 1's crop.
Question investigated: Is the bright proximal (humerus/joint) region
  genuinely losing information to sensor/export clipping (a hard spike at max
  value = data is gone, no processing can recover it), or is it merely
  visually compressed and recoverable via contrast stretching?
Reason for performing: Raised as an explicit Section B proposal only by
  Agent_02, but the underlying symptom (apparent overexposure/brightness
  gradient favoring the proximal region) was independently noticed in Section
  A by 5 of 6 agents (Agent_01, 03, 04, 05, 06) without being turned into a
  formal test. Both council reviewers independently promoted this from
  "single-agent minority" to top priority, arguing it is a logical
  precondition for correctly interpreting Analyses 2 and 3: if the region is
  hard-clipped, any "finding" Analysis 2 reveals there is stretched noise, not
  recovered signal.
Agents supporting: Agent_02 (originating proposal); Agent_01/03/04/05/06
  (independently noticed the underlying symptom in Section A). Both council
  reviewers ranked this #1 or #2, explicitly overriding a simple vote-count
  ranking to do so.
Agents opposing/questioning: None — both reviewers called this the
  lowest-risk item in the entire proposal set (pure statistics, no pixel
  modification, cannot itself manufacture a false finding).
Possible artifacts: A clipped histogram in this PNG export does not
  necessarily mean the original clinical acquisition was overexposed — prior
  export/compression could have introduced clipping not present in a native
  acquisition; this caveat is recorded alongside the result.
Expected informational value: High as a gating/context measurement for
  Analyses 2 and 3, even though it produces no new visual finding on its own.

ANALYSIS 5
Output filename: images/05_edge_enhancement_fullshaft.png
Transformation: Unsharp-mask / high-pass edge enhancement applied along the
  full visible length of the humerus, radius, and ulna shafts.
Parameters: unsharp mask, radius ~3px, amount ~150%, threshold low, applied to
  the whole-limb image (not ROI-restricted, since the question spans the full
  shaft length).
Question investigated: Is the cortex of the humerus, radius, and ulna truly
  continuous along its entire visible length, or is there a subtle step-off,
  buckle, or hairline lucency smoothed over by the image's apparent moderate
  resolution/exposure?
Reason for performing: The second unanimous proposal — independently raised
  by all 6 agents as the direct tool for testing cortical continuity/occult
  fracture, the second-highest-yield open question after the fat-pad
  question.
Agents supporting: Agent_01, Agent_02, Agent_03, Agent_04, Agent_05, Agent_06
  (6/6). Both council reviewers rated the underlying technique "justified,"
  though both ranked it below Analyses 2 and 4.
Agents opposing/questioning: Both council reviewers pushed back on treating
  this as equally safe just because of unanimous support — all 6 originating
  agents independently flagged the identical failure mode (see artifacts,
  below), which both reviewers treat as a strong risk signal, not noise.
  Council_Reviewer_B explicitly ranked this 4th, below the two lower-risk
  analyses, "because this council's own agents unanimously flagged its
  overlap-artifact failure mode."
Possible artifacts: This is the highest-risk analysis in the plan. Ringing/
  overshoot along the high-contrast cortex can create a false parallel
  fringe misreadable as a second cortical line or periosteal reaction; more
  importantly, in THIS image the elbow is flexed with substantial anatomic
  overlap (radial head over coronoid, radius crossing ulna), and edge
  filtering at those superimposition boundaries can produce a line that looks
  exactly like a fracture but is simply two normal structures' silhouettes
  crossing. MANDATORY verification rule (per both council reviewers,
  generalizing Agent_05's own suggestion): a candidate line is only reportable
  if (a) it is visible at more than one filter-strength setting, (b) it has a
  faint counterpart in the unprocessed original at the same location, and (c)
  it is anatomically implausible as a superimposition boundary. Any candidate
  line falling within a known overlap zone must be treated as
  expected-artifact-until-proven-otherwise.
Expected informational value: High if the verification rule is applied;
  materially lower confidence, and higher false-positive risk, if a candidate
  line is reported without it.

ANALYSIS 6
Output filename: images/06_edge_enhancement_elbow_zoom.png
Transformation: Same unsharp-mask/edge-enhancement technique as Analysis 5,
  but restricted to a tighter crop around the radial neck, coronoid process,
  and olecranon specifically (the sub-region prioritization).
Parameters: same filter parameters as Analysis 5, applied to Analysis 1's
  joint crop instead of the whole image.
Question investigated: Is there a subtle, non-displaced cortical fracture
  specifically at the radial neck, coronoid, or olecranon — the classic
  occult elbow fracture sites — that a full-shaft sweep at whole-image scale
  might under-resolve?
Reason for performing: Three agents (Agent_03, Agent_04, Agent_06)
  independently named these three specific sub-sites as the highest-yield
  locations for this technique, distinct from (and in addition to) the
  full-shaft sweep in Analysis 5. Both council reviewers kept this
  sub-prioritization explicitly rather than treating it as redundant with
  Analysis 5.
Agents supporting: Agent_03, Agent_04, Agent_06 (3/6, for the targeted
  sub-region emphasis specifically); implicitly all 6 for the underlying
  edge-enhancement technique. Both council reviewers retained it as a
  distinct output from Analysis 5.
Agents opposing/questioning: Same overlap-artifact caveat as Analysis 5
  applies with equal or greater force here, since this crop is centered
  exactly on the highest-overlap part of the joint.
Possible artifacts: Same as Analysis 5, with the overlap-artifact risk
  concentrated rather than diluted by the tighter crop; same mandatory
  verification rule applies.
Expected informational value: High for the specific classic fracture sites,
  complementary to Analysis 5's whole-shaft sweep.

ANALYSIS 7
Output filename: images/07_alignment_lines_overlay.png
Transformation: Geometric annotation only (radiocapitellar line: a line drawn
  through the axis of the radial neck/shaft, should intersect the capitellum;
  anterior humeral line: a line along the anterior humeral cortex, should
  intersect the middle third of the capitellum), drawn on top of a copy of
  the unmodified original image. No pixel intensity manipulation.
Parameters: line endpoints selected from visually identified anatomic
  landmarks (radial shaft axis, anterior humeral cortex, capitellum center);
  estimated flexion angle and any apparent obliquity recorded alongside the
  overlay.
Question investigated: Is the radial head properly aligned with the
  capitellum, and does the anterior humeral cortex pass through the expected
  portion of the capitellum, as a geometric (not textural) check for subtle
  subluxation or supracondylar injury that neither contrast nor edge
  enhancement would catch?
Reason for performing: Independently proposed by two agents (Agent_03 and
  Agent_06) using the same two classic alignment constructs — both council
  reviewers noted this is a real 2/6 convergent signal, not two unrelated
  minority views, and both rated it higher than a raw vote count would
  suggest specifically because it is the only proposed technique in the set
  that adds zero pixel manipulation to the diagnostic image.
Agents supporting: Agent_03, Agent_06. Council_Reviewer_A (ranked #4,
  explicitly above two other 2/6-or-fewer proposals). Council_Reviewer_B
  (ranked #5).
Agents opposing/questioning: Both originating agents themselves, and both
  council reviewers, flag that the elbow in this image is flexed beyond the
  canonical ~90° these heuristics are calibrated for, and possible obliquity
  is not excluded — a positive (malalignment) finding must be reported with
  this positioning caveat front and center, not as a clean pass/fail.
Possible artifacts: Not a pixel artifact — a methodological one. Applying
  angle-sensitive alignment heuristics outside their validated positioning
  range risks a false "malalignment" call that is actually just projection
  foreshortening.
Expected informational value: Moderate — a genuinely different, complementary
  axis of information (geometric vs. textural), but its result must be
  qualified by the positioning caveat rather than read as definitive.

ANALYSIS 8
Output filename: images/08_wrist_fragment_crop_contrast.png
Transformation: Crop to the small cluster of bone visible at the very bottom
  edge of the frame (partial carpal row), with an independent local
  contrast stretch applied to that crop only.
Parameters: crop box at the bottom-edge fragment; simple min-max contrast
  stretch (not CLAHE, to avoid assuming a particular curve in a region with
  little surrounding context to calibrate against).
Question investigated: Does the small, cropped cluster of bone at the bottom
  edge show any overt abnormality, or is it simply too incomplete to
  interpret — and should its presence prompt a recommendation for a properly
  centered wrist view?
Reason for performing: Independently proposed by two agents (Agent_03: local
  contrast stretch; Agent_04: gamma/brightness recovery — same underlying
  proposal, two candidate techniques). Both council reviewers merged these
  into one low-priority deliverable, valuable mainly for documentation of a
  field-of-view limitation rather than new-finding-recovery.
Agents supporting: Agent_03, Agent_04. Both council reviewers included it,
  ranked low (5th/6th of the recommended set).
Agents opposing/questioning: Both proposing agents themselves predicted the
  most likely outcome is "confirms non-diagnostic due to cropping" — this is
  treated as a feature (an honest negative/limitation finding), not a
  weakness of the proposal.
Possible artifacts: Contrast stretching at the image boundary can exaggerate
  edge/vignette darkening into a false appearance of a lucent lesion; the
  cropped nature of the structure makes any bone-count or alignment judgment
  unreliable regardless of processing quality.
Expected informational value: Low for new pathology detection; useful
  specifically to formally document that the wrist is not adequately imaged
  in this exposure.

ANALYSIS 9
Output filename: images/09_inverted_polarity.png
Transformation: Full tonal inversion (negative image) of the original,
  unmodified pixel data.
Parameters: simple 255-minus-value inversion, no other processing.
Question investigated: Does the "no obvious gross abnormality" impression
  from Section A hold up under an alternative, independent visual
  presentation of the identical pixel data?
Reason for performing: Proposed by a single agent (Agent_05). Both council
  reviewers treated it as a legitimate but non-priority "free" add-on: it is
  lossless and reversible, cannot itself manufacture a finding, and costs
  almost nothing to produce, but is not tied to a specific Section-A
  observation the way the higher-priority analyses are.
Agents supporting: Agent_05. Council_Reviewer_A (kept as a cheap add-on,
  ranked 6th). Council_Reviewer_B (kept as "optional/bonus only," ranked 7th,
  explicitly declining to treat it as a core deliverable).
Agents opposing/questioning: Council_Reviewer_B noted its justification is "a
  plausibility appeal" rather than tied to a concrete Section-A uncertainty.
Possible artifacts: None from the inversion itself (lossless, reversible);
  risk is purely interpretive — unfamiliar-looking normal structures (nutrient
  canals, soft-tissue folds) could be misread as new findings simply because
  they look different from the standard presentation.
Expected informational value: Low; included as a zero-cost cross-check, not a
  primary analysis.

================================================================================
ANALYSES CONSIDERED AND NOT INCLUDED
================================================================================
- Quantitative intensity-profile sampling across the shafts (Agent_05, P4):
  both council reviewers independently recommended dropping this. Agent_05's
  own stated caveats (confounded by bone angle relative to sampling line,
  proximity to joint, compression/resampling artifacts) undercut its claimed
  objectivity, and both reviewers noted it targets the same question as
  Analysis 5/6 (cortical continuity) with more setup cost and a harder-to-
  interpret output, while still requiring the same visual re-confirmation
  step it was meant to reduce reliance on. Not implemented.
- Global/whole-image generic filters (global histogram equalization, full-
  frame edge detection, pseudocolor/heatmap recoloring): no agent proposed
  these in Section B; Agent_01 explicitly considered and rejected them as not
  tied to any concrete Section-A uncertainty, and both council reviewers
  independently affirmed this rejection as a design constraint given how much
  of the frame is empty collimated background plus one saturated bone region.
  Not implemented.
