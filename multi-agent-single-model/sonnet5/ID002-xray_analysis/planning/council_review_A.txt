COUNCIL REVIEW — Council_Reviewer_A
Subject: Critique of Section B processing proposals from Agent_01–Agent_06
(Lateral elbow/forearm radiograph, university research/testing exercise — not a clinical read)

================================================================================
PREFATORY NOTE ON METHOD
================================================================================
I read all six Section A reviews and all six Section B proposal sets in full.
Six agents converged almost perfectly on Section A (unremarkable-appearing
lateral elbow/forearm view, no obvious fracture, residual uncertainty about
fat pads and subtle cortical lines, poor wrist/humerus framing, exposure
imbalance between bone and soft tissue). That convergence is reflected in
Section B: two proposal types were reinvented independently by all six
agents, which is a meaningful signal, not just noise. I've grouped proposals
by underlying clinical question rather than by exact technique, since several
agents used different technical language (CLAHE vs. "local histogram
equalization" vs. "contrast redistribution") for what is functionally the
same operation.

I also want to flag upfront: the task brief characterizes alignment-line
geometry as raised only by "Agent_03" and separately by "Agent_06," as if
each is an isolated minority view. That's not quite right — both proposed it
*independently*, which makes it a 2/6 convergent proposal, not two unrelated
1/6 proposals. I address this below (Section 5) and it changes my priority
ranking (Section 6).

================================================================================
1. WELL-JUSTIFIED ANALYSES — GROUPED, WITH ATTRIBUTION
================================================================================

GROUP A — Peri-elbow soft-tissue / fat-pad local contrast enhancement
  Proposed by: ALL SIX agents (Agent_01 Prop.1 "dual-range windowing";
  Agent_02 Prop.2 "local/adaptive contrast enhancement, CLAHE";
  Agent_03 Prop.1 "soft-tissue window / local contrast stretch";
  Agent_04 Prop.1 "fat-pad local contrast enhancement, CLAHE";
  Agent_05 Prop.1 "joint-region contrast redistribution";
  Agent_06 Prop.1 "localized soft-tissue contrast enhancement").
  Verdict: STRONGLY JUSTIFIED, highest priority. This is the single most
  important open clinical question raised in every Section A (is there an
  occult fat-pad/effusion sign), and every agent independently proposed the
  same fix for the same, well-articulated reason: bone and soft tissue
  compete for the same 8-bit display range, and a global contrast setting
  necessarily under-serves one of them. Unanimous, mechanistically sound,
  reversible, and directly answers the highest-yield question in the image.
  Two candidate implementations exist among the six proposals — Agent_01's
  whole-image piecewise/dual-range remap vs. everyone else's ROI-cropped
  CLAHE/local-stretch. These are not separate analyses; they're two
  candidate techniques for the same job (see Section 2). I'd implement the
  ROI-cropped version as primary (lower risk of introducing artifacts
  outside the region that matters) and treat dual-range global windowing as
  optional secondary/comparison output.

GROUP B — Cortical-margin edge enhancement (fracture-line conspicuity)
  Proposed by: ALL SIX agents (Agent_01 Prop.2; Agent_02 Prop.3;
  Agent_03 Prop.2, targeted at radial neck/coronoid; Agent_04 Prop.2;
  Agent_05 Prop.2; Agent_06 Prop.2, targeted at olecranon/coronoid/radial
  neck).
  Verdict: JUSTIFIED, second-highest priority, but implement with the strict
  comparison protocol every single agent independently insisted on (any
  candidate line must be relocated and confirmed in the unprocessed original
  before being reported as a finding — not just described from the enhanced
  version). This is the other unanimous proposal in the whole set, aimed at
  the second most important open question (subtle cortical fracture,
  especially at the radial neck/coronoid — the classic occult sites named
  by four of six agents specifically). See Section 3 for why this one
  carries materially more artifact risk than Group A and needs a harder
  verification gate.

GROUP C — Alignment-line geometry (radiocapitellar line / anterior humeral
line)
  Proposed by: Agent_03 (Prop.3) and Agent_06 (Prop.3), independently.
  Verdict: JUSTIFIED, and I'd rate it higher than its "2/6" count suggests
  (see Section 5). It is categorically different from Groups A/B: it adds
  zero pixel manipulation to the diagnostic image — it is a measurement
  overlay on the unmodified original. That means it carries none of the
  halo/ringing/noise-amplification risk that dominates the caveats sections
  of every other proposal in this set. It also targets a failure mode
  (malalignment/subluxation) that neither fat-pad enhancement nor
  edge-enhancement would catch. Both agents flag the same caveat
  themselves — the elbow in this image is flexed beyond the canonical ~90°
  used to calibrate these lines, and possible obliquity is not excluded, so
  a positive finding must be read with that positioning caveat front and
  center, not as a clean pass/fail.

GROUP D — Objective exposure/clipping diagnostic (histogram analysis)
  Proposed by: Agent_02 (Prop.1) explicitly in Section B; independently
  echoed as a Section A concern by Agent_01, Agent_03, Agent_05, and
  Agent_06 (all flagged apparent overexposure/brightness gradient in the
  proximal humeral/joint region without proposing a formal test for it).
  Verdict: JUSTIFIED and underrated by the group. Only one agent turned this
  into an actual Section B deliverable, but five of six independently
  noticed the symptom it's designed to test. I'd promote this out of
  "one-agent minority" status — it's genuinely foundational, not a nice-to-
  have: it tells you, before you spend effort on Groups A/B, whether the
  brightest region has recoverable structure (compressed, not clipped) or
  is a dead end (hard clipping — no amount of local contrast stretching
  will recover data that was never captured/exported). It's also the
  cheapest and lowest-artifact-risk proposal in the entire set (pure
  statistics, no image modification, cannot itself introduce a fake
  finding). See Section 6 for why I rank it #2 despite the "single agent"
  framing.

================================================================================
2. REDUNDANT PROPOSALS — MERGE OR DROP
================================================================================
- Agent_01 Prop.1 (dual-range windowing) and the five ROI-CLAHE/local-stretch
  proposals (Group A above) are redundant at the level of clinical question
  and should be scoped as ONE deliverable with (at most) two candidate
  techniques compared, not six separate outputs. Producing six near-identical
  "fat pad enhancement" images would be wasted effort and would make
  cross-agent comparison harder, not easier.
- All six edge-enhancement proposals (Group B) are likewise redundant with
  each other at the conceptual level. The only real technical difference
  worth preserving is scope: Agent_01/02/04/05/06 apply it along the full
  bone shafts (systematic sweep), while Agent_03/06 additionally call out a
  targeted, possibly higher-gain pass restricted to the radial
  neck/coronoid/olecranon specifically. I'd keep both scopes as one combined
  deliverable (full-shaft sweep + a zoomed high-yield-site pass) rather than
  as six independent proposals.
- Agent_03 Prop.4 (contrast-stretched crop of the cropped wrist fragment) and
  Agent_04 Prop.3 (gamma/brightness recovery of the same fragment) are the
  same proposal with two different candidate techniques (local contrast
  stretch vs. gamma adjustment) aimed at the same small, low-value crop.
  Merge into one deliverable; I'd default to Agent_03's contrast-stretch
  approach since it doesn't require assuming a particular gamma curve and
  is easier to justify as "not altering perceived structure, only
  remapping it."
- Agent_02's ROI-crop-for-histogram (Prop.1) and Agent_01's magnified crop of
  the elbow joint (Prop.3) both start from "crop the joint region," but they
  are NOT redundant with each other — one produces a quantitative
  diagnostic (histogram stats), the other produces a viewing aid
  (upscaled image, no numeric output). Keep both concepts, but note the
  magnified crop is a presentation convenience, not an analysis in its own
  right (see Section 4).

================================================================================
3. PROPOSALS THAT RISK MISLEADING ARTIFACTS — IMPLEMENT CAUTIOUSLY
================================================================================
- Group B (cortical edge enhancement) is the riskiest widely-endorsed
  proposal in the set, and every single agent flagged the same failure
  mode independently: unsharp-mask/high-pass ringing can produce a thin
  parallel fringe next to a real edge that looks like a second cortical
  line or periosteal reaction; and — more importantly for THIS specific
  image — normal anatomic overlap (radius crossing ulna, radial head
  overlapping coronoid, the humeral condyle overlapping the radial head in
  a flexed lateral view) can produce an edge-filter response that looks
  exactly like a fracture line but is just two normal structures'
  silhouettes crossing. Given how much overlap this particular
  projection has (elbow flexed >90°, per Agent_03), I'd treat any
  edge-enhancement "finding" here with more suspicion than the agents'
  own caveats already suggest — I would require that a candidate line be
  (a) visible at more than one filter strength/parameter setting, AND
  (b) anatomically implausible as a superimposition boundary, before it
  is elevated to "worth flagging," not just "present in the original at
  some faint level" as several agents proposed. Agent_05's suggestion
  (only trust a line that's consistent across multiple enhancement
  strengths) is the single best verification criterion offered across
  all six agents and should be the standard applied to Group B generally,
  not just Agent_05's own proposal.
- Group A (fat-pad contrast enhancement) is lower risk than Group B but
  not risk-free: CLAHE-style local methods reliably manufacture texture
  from noise/compression blocking in low-signal regions, and several
  agents (03, 04, 06) specifically warn that a skin fold or triceps
  tendon fat plane can be mistaken for a true fat-pad stripe. The
  mitigation everyone proposes (side-by-side against the original,
  require anatomic plausibility of shape — crescentic, not a hard edge)
  is adequate and should be enforced as a hard requirement on the output,
  not an optional discussion point.
- Agent_04's gamma/brightness recovery of the wrist crop (superseded by
  Agent_03's contrast-stretch per Section 2, but worth flagging on its own
  merits): recovering brightness in what is likely a genuinely low-signal,
  near-edge-of-field region is the proposal most likely in this whole set
  to manufacture "detail" that is pure sensor/export noise, because unlike
  Group A/B it isn't anchored to a well-exposed adjacent region for
  comparison. If implemented, output must be captioned as exploratory only.

================================================================================
4. PROPOSALS I WOULD NOT IMPLEMENT (OR WOULD DOWNGRADE TO NON-ANALYSIS)
================================================================================
- Agent_01 Prop.3 (magnified/upscaled crop of the elbow joint, "mild
  adaptive contrast, no other filtering"): I would not count this as a
  standalone image-processing analysis. Agent_01 is admirably honest that
  simple upscaling "creates the appearance of more detail without actually
  adding new diagnostic information" — I agree, and I'd go further: in a
  deliverable list that already includes an ROI crop for Group A and Group
  D, adding a third, separately-branded "magnified crop" invites exactly
  the false impression Agent_01 warns against (that more pixels = more
  information). Fold it into the presentation layer of Group A's ROI crop
  (i.e., "here is the enhanced joint ROI, shown at higher zoom") rather
  than shipping it as its own numbered analysis.
- Agent_05 Prop.4 (quantitative intensity-profile sampling across the
  shafts): I disagree with treating this as a worthwhile addition despite
  it being the most "rigorous-sounding" proposal in the set. Agent_05's
  own caveats undercut it: profiles are confounded by bone angle relative
  to the sampling line, by proximity to the joint, and by
  compression/resampling artifacts, such that (Agent_05's words) "an
  outlier profile point is a prompt for re-inspection, not a diagnosis on
  its own." That's also exactly what Group B (edge enhancement) already
  gives you, more directly and more interpretably by a human reviewer,
  without needing to define sampling-line geometry or correct for bone
  obliquity first. I'd skip this unless Group B flags a genuinely
  ambiguous location that specifically needs a numeric tie-breaker — it's
  solving the same problem as Group B with more setup cost and a harder-
  to-interpret output (a numeric curve vs. a visually inspectable line).
- Global, whole-image generic filters (histogram equalization applied
  globally, whole-image edge detection, pseudocolor/heat-map recoloring):
  no agent proposed these in Section B, and Agent_01 explicitly called out
  and rejected them by name. I fully agree with that restraint and want to
  make it an explicit council position rather than leave it as an aside in
  one agent's report — given how much of this frame is empty collimated
  background plus one saturated bone region, any global transform would
  either blow out the good bone-cortex contrast to fix soft tissue, or
  vice versa. Every agent that considered scope at all converged on
  ROI-restricted / locally-adaptive methods over global ones. This is a
  real, useful convergence and should be stated as a design constraint for
  whoever implements Groups A/B, not just a rejected alternative.

================================================================================
5. MINORITY PROPOSALS ON THEIR OWN MERITS
================================================================================
- Agent_01: dual-range windowing — not actually a stand-alone minority
  proposal; it's a technique-level variant of Group A (see Section 1/2).
  On its own technical merits it's reasonable (piecewise remap is simpler
  to implement than ROI-limited CLAHE and easier to explain/audit), so I'd
  keep it as the secondary/comparison technique under Group A, not reject
  it, but it doesn't warrant its own line item.
- Agent_01: magnified crop — see Section 4. Keep as presentation, not
  analysis.
- Agent_02: histogram/clipping check — see Group D in Section 1. I
  actively disagree with leaving this at "minority, low priority." On its
  own merits (cheap, objective, zero-artifact-risk, and directly answers a
  concern five of six agents raised qualitatively) it deserves promotion,
  and I've ranked it accordingly in Section 6.
- Agent_03: alignment-line geometry — not a true 1-agent minority; see
  Group C (Agent_03 + Agent_06 converged independently). On its own merits
  it's one of the strongest proposals in the whole set precisely because
  it's the only one that touches zero pixels of the diagnostic image.
- Agent_03: wrist-fragment crop — modest merit. Agent_03's own expected
  outcome is refreshingly honest ("most likely... too incomplete for any
  diagnostic conclusion... itself a useful, honest negative finding"). I'd
  keep it for exactly that reason: its value isn't in extracting a wrist
  diagnosis (it won't), it's in formally documenting field-of-view
  inadequacy to justify recommending a proper wrist series. Worth doing
  once, cheaply, with expectations calibrated down front.
- Agent_05: inverted polarity — genuine minority (1/6), and I think the
  majority under-weighted it rather than fairly rejecting it. It is
  literally risk-free (a lossless, reversible relabeling of the same
  data — it cannot introduce an artifact the way any of the contrast/edge
  methods can) and costs almost nothing to produce. Its marginal
  diagnostic yield is probably low, but "low yield, zero risk, near-zero
  cost" is still a positive expected value, so I'd keep it as a cheap
  add-on rather than drop it — just not resource it ahead of Groups A–D.
- Agent_05: intensity-profile sampling — see Section 4. I disagree with
  including it; redundant with Group B for a real cost in setup complexity
  and interpretability.
- Agent_06: alignment-line geometry — see Group C above; this is the same
  proposal as Agent_03's, converged on independently, and I've treated the
  two as one 2-agent proposal throughout rather than two isolated minority
  views.

================================================================================
6. PRIORITY RANKING OF RECOMMENDED ANALYSIS SET (most → least valuable)
================================================================================
1. Group A — Peri-elbow fat-pad local contrast enhancement (ROI-limited
   CLAHE/local stretch as primary; Agent_01's dual-range windowing as an
   optional comparison technique). 6/6 agents, highest clinical yield
   question in the image.
2. Group D — Histogram/clipping diagnostic of the bright proximal region.
   Formally 1/6 in Section B, but I've promoted it to #2: it's the
   cheapest, lowest-risk item in the whole set, and it should logically run
   before/alongside #1 and #5 since it tells you whether there's real
   recoverable signal to enhance in the first place.
3. Group B — Cortical-margin edge enhancement, both full-shaft sweep and
   targeted radial-neck/coronoid/olecranon pass, run under the strict
   multi-parameter-consistency verification rule (Section 3). 6/6 agents,
   second-highest clinical yield question, but held to a harder
   verification bar than Group A because of this image's substantial
   anatomic overlap.
4. Group C — Alignment-line geometry (radiocapitellar line + anterior
   humeral line), presented as an annotation on the unmodified original,
   with the atypical-flexion-angle caveat stated explicitly alongside any
   result. 2/6 agents (independently convergent), zero pixel-manipulation
   risk, complementary failure mode to Groups A/B.
5. Wrist-fragment crop + local contrast stretch (merged Agent_03/Agent_04
   proposal). 2/6 agents; modest value, mainly to document
   non-diagnostic field-of-view and support a recommendation for a proper
   wrist series.
6. Inverted-polarity cross-check. 1/6 agent; keep as a free add-on, lowest
   resourcing priority, not worth deferring anything above it for.
7. Magnified elbow-joint crop. 1/6 agent; retain only as a presentation
   layer on top of Group A's ROI, not as an independently resourced
   analysis.

NOT RECOMMENDED:
- Quantitative intensity-profile sampling across the shafts (Agent_05
  Prop.4) — redundant with Group B, adds setup/interpretation cost for
  weak incremental value; skip unless Group B produces a specific
  ambiguous case that needs a numeric tie-breaker.
- Any global/whole-image filter (global histogram equalization, whole-
  image edge detection, pseudocolor) — no agent proposed these, and the
  council affirms that rejection as a design constraint, not just an
  absence.

================================================================================
SUMMARY OF WHERE I DISAGREE WITH THE MAJORITY / DEFAULT FRAMING
================================================================================
1. I do not treat the histogram/clipping check as a low-priority,
   single-agent afterthought — I rank it #2 overall because it is
   foundational (gates interpretation of the two unanimous proposals) and
   carries essentially zero artifact risk, unlike almost everything else
   in this set.
2. I do not treat alignment-line geometry as two unrelated 1-agent minority
   proposals (Agent_03 and Agent_06 separately) — they converged on it
   independently, which makes it a real 2/6 signal, and I rank it above
   two of the other 2/6-or-fewer proposals precisely because it's the only
   technique here that never touches the diagnostic pixel data.
3. I push back on treating Agent_05's intensity-profile sampling as a
   worthwhile "quantitative" addition just because it sounds more rigorous
   than a visual method — by its own stated caveats it's confounded enough,
   and redundant enough with edge enhancement, that I'd cut it from the
   recommended set entirely rather than implement it "cautiously."
4. I downgrade Agent_01's magnified elbow crop from a standalone analysis
   to a presentation detail of Group A, agreeing with Agent_01's own
   admission that it adds no new information — I don't think it should
   occupy a line item alongside the genuinely information-bearing
   proposals.
