MISLEADING-ARTIFACT RISK:
- CLAHE family (family 1, 7): noise speckle mimicking subchondral cysts; tile-
  boundary blockiness; halo/ringing at cortices. Guard: raw-image-first judgment
  (A3P5), two-clip-limit concordance (A2P1), side-by-side matched-geometry
  comparison (all agents).
- Edge filters (family 2): false linear structures mimicking fracture lines;
  symmetric halos mimicking double cortices; trabecular striations flagged as
  "lines". Guard: every candidate line must be traceable in the unprocessed
  original across its full length; must not cross a joint space; must not
  merely follow trabecular flow or a cortical edge; A3P3's colored overlay and
  A5P4's discriminant (real cortex terminates at bone; scratches run through
  empty space/soft tissue) are the two best checks in the set.
- Deconvolution (A4P7): HIGHEST risk - phantom streaks that resemble fracture
  lines, strong regularization dependence. Guard: multi-regularization
  persistence + agreement with original edge locations; otherwise skip.
- Flat-field division (family 5): divide-by-estimation errors create curved
  brightness artifacts mimicking periosteal reaction; can erase genuine broad
  low-frequency soft-tissue swelling. Guard: region-by-region check that
  anatomy is unchanged; original as arbiter (A5P4, A6P5).
- Bone subtraction (A2P3): residual halo at bone margins mimicking
  calcification; over-subtraction distorts the silhouette.
- Denoising (A5P2): opposite-direction risk - over-smoothing can MASK a true
  subtle lesion (false negative); two strengths required.
- Magnification/resampling (A6P2): interpolation adds no resolution; Lanczos
  ringing can mimic osteophytes; use original for final gestalt.
- Skin-line segmentation (A6P3): the bright beam-field rectangle and streaks
  near the limb border can break thresholding; manual correction and
  standardized reference points required.
- Universal guard already unanimous across all 32 proposals: a finding that
  exists only after processing, with no counterpart in the original, is an
  artifact, not pathology. Preserve this as the standing rule.

MEASUREMENTS AND EXTRA VISUALIZATIONS:
Yes - several proposed measurements add real information, not just numbers:
- Joint-space width in pixels (A1P1) and, better, in mm via locally calibrated
  scale (A6P2) - only quantitative check on early arthropathy available on a
  single lateral.
- Böhler's angle (A3P4, A6P4) - standard index that can exclude an old
  calcaneal fracture even without a visible line.
- Calcaneal pitch / tibiocalcaneal / tibiometatarsal / talonavicular angles
  (A1P3, A2P4, A3P4, A4P5, A5P5, A6P4) - but must be reported jointly with the
  true-lateral/rotation check (A2P4, A6P4); a rotated view systematically
  biases them. Measure on native resolution (A6P4), with uncertainty bands
  (A4P5, A6P4), and note A1P3's caveat that the first-metatarsal axis is
  truncated at the crop.
- Fibula/tibia shadow separation (A2P4) - unique rotation proxy; no other
  agent proposed it.
- Heel-pad thickness in mm with standardized reference points (A6P3) - concrete
  number against expected norms; unique.
- Dorsal-vs-plantar soft-tissue thickness profile (A1P4) - internal control
  that separates focal swelling from global gradient; unique.
- Noise floor + edge-spread detection limit (A4P6) - unique; calibrates how
  much weight every negative fracture screen can carry. Must be measured on
  the original, not enhanced versions.
Useful visualizations already proposed (no new techniques invented here):
flat-field-corrected full image (A2P5/A5P4), CLAHE with two clip limits
(A2P1), bone-saturating soft-tissue window (A4P4), edge-map overlay on the
original (A3P3), difference images across window settings to expose
window-dependent streaks (A4P2).
