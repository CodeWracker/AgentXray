The council converged on a small set of well-justified analyses, each matching a documented
limitation of this specific image (rescanned film: moderate contrast, grain, upper-left
darkness gradient, faint vertical streaks, right-edge cropping of the toes):

   subtalar, incl. talar dome/plafond and talus body/neck (A01-P1, A02-P1, A03-P5, A05-P1,
   A06-P1, A06-P2). Highest consensus: 6 of 6 agents. Joint spaces are a few pixels wide in

3. Quantitative angular measurement — calcaneal pitch, tibiometatarsal, tibiocalcaneal,

4. Soft-tissue contour/intensity analysis — dorsum fullness, heel pad, Achilles region

MISLEADING-ARTIFACT RISK:
- CLAHE/noise amplification: grain clusters can masquerade as subchondral cysts, "holes",
  speck calcification, or a spur (families 1, 3). Guard: side-by-side with identically
  cropped original at matched zoom (all agents already require this); a finding must be
  faintly traceable in the original; persist across two enhancement strengths (A02-P1's
  dual-clip-limit bracket is the best guard proposed and should be adopted everywhere).
- Edge filters (families 2, 3): respond to ANY sharp transition — trabeculae, normal
  cortices, grain, scratches — so false "fracture lines" are the dominant failure mode.
  Guards already proposed: verify each line in the unprocessed original across its full
  length (A01-P5, A02-P2, A03-P3, A04-P3, A05-P3); require anatomical plausibility (line
  continuous, not crossing a joint, endpoints meeting cortex — A06-P1 criteria a/b/c);
  cross-window persistence (A04-P2: a real line survives different window settings, a
  streak shifts or vanishes). A03-P3's median pre-denoise is a double-edged sword: it kills
  grain but can also erase a genuinely faint hairline — run with and without.
- A02-P3 bone subtraction: subtraction halos at bone margins can be misread as
  calcification; over-subtraction distorts the silhouette. Guard: treat halo-adjacent
  densities as suspect; original is arbiter.
- Flat-field correction (family 5): division by an imperfect background model creates
  curved/ringing brightness artifacts that can mimic periosteal reaction or fracture, and
  can erase genuine broad low-frequency signal (e.g., true soft-tissue swelling). Guard:
  region-by-region comparison to original; if correction changes any bone's apparent
  density or shape, distrust that region (A02-P5's criterion).
- A06-P2 Lanczos resampling: ringing ripples along sharp cortices can be misread as
  osteophytes; also the "mm" annotation is not supported — the image has no scale bar, so
  only pixel units are defensible.
- A05-P2 denoising: over-denoising produces waxy/blotchy textures that can MASK a real
  subtle lesion. Guard: two conservative strengths; verify a known thin cortex (e.g.,
  posterior calcaneal) is preserved after denoising.
- A04-P7 deconvolution: highest artifact risk in the entire council — phantom streaks that
  resemble fracture lines, ringing, heavy regularization dependence. Guard if run at all:
  persistence across multiple regularization levels and agreement with original edge
  locations; I do not believe it should carry diagnostic weight here (see votes).
- A03-P5/A06-P2 magnification: interpolation adds no resolution; banding in the joint
  space can mimic narrowing. Judge joint space first on the raw image (A03-P5's rule).
- Angles (family 3): landmark mis-ID among overlapping tarsals and non-true-lateral
  rotation bias every value. Guards: lines overlaid on the original, ranges not point
  values where edges are blurred (A04-P5), do not call an angle abnormal unless outside
  normal range by more than the estimated landmark error (A06-P4), run the rotation check
  before interpreting angles (A02-P4, A06-P4).
- A06-P3 segmentation: grain and the beam-field edge can split or jitter the skin line;
  the plantar contour is uninformative (weight-bearing flat). Guard: overlay verification
  at every point, standardized measurement stations.
