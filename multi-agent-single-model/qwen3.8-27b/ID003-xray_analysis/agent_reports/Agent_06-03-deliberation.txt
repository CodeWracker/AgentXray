  Agent_02 (open physes elbow/wrist, small carpal centers), Agent_03
  (slender bones, separate rounded distal-phalangeal epiphyseal centers,
  metaphyseal flaring), Agent_04 (narrow physes, small/incomplete carpal

  multiple carpal/phalangeal centers).
- Agent_01 and Agent_06 (myself) did not commit; my first pass simply never
  addressed the physes, which is a gap in my own review, not counter-evidence.
  None of the six first passes described CLOSED physes or fully ossified
  carpal bones.
- Working position: pediatric is the leading hypothesis by evidence weight,
  but it is NOT formally established from this single oblique view, and I
  will not force consensus. It matters diagnostically: (i) it makes the
  mid-DIAphyseal location of the faint transverse line more concerning
  (Agent_04's point: it is not at a physis, so it cannot be dismissed as a
  growth plate); (ii) normal open physeal lines elsewhere must not be read
  as fracture lines; (iii) physeal (Salter-Harris) injury enters the
  differential for the wrist and elbow.
Which analyses resolve it, in order of yield:
 1. Carpal census (T18, Agent_04 M3) on the windowed wrist crop (T7):
    2-5 small, rounded, smooth-margined centers with large empty gaps =
    young child; 8-9 fully ossified carpals with continuous cortices = adult.
 2. Physeal width/shaft width ratio (T19, Agent_05 M1): a lucent band of
    measurable width at the distal radius/ulna = open physis; its absence
    with a continuous cortex = closed.
 3. Elbow crop (T6): elbow ossification-center pattern (capitellum, radial
    head, olecranon, epicondyles) cross-checks the carpus.
 4. Distal phalangeal epiphyses (noted by Agent_03 first pass): separate
    rounded centers = immature.
Evidence pattern that would settle it:
 - PEDIATRIC: open physeal lucent bands at distal radius/ulna and phalanges
   + a handful (roughly 2-5) of small rounded carpal centers + metaphyseal
   flaring + slender diaphyses.
 - ADULT: no physeal bands anywhere (continuous cortex), all carpal bones
   ossified and fused, no metaphyseal flaring, wider diaphyses.
 - MIXED/AMBIGUOUS: e.g., closed distal radius but open phalanges, or
   unreadable physes due to obliquity — in that case report maturity as
   "indeterminate, features favor [X]" and say which structure was
   unreadable.
Cross-check: if Agent_05's D2 second limb is real, its small rounded
"carpal-like" centers are consistent with a CHILD — the two hypotheses
(pediatric + contralateral child's limb) are mutually reinforcing, which
raises my confidence in both, though neither is confirmed yet.
