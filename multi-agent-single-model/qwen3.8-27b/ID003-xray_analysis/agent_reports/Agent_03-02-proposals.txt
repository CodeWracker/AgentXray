A. GLOBAL ENHANCEMENTS
====================================================================

1.
- ANALYSIS: CLAHE (contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalization) over the full image, plus a global level/gamma window tuned to mid-gray bone (e.g., gamma ~0.7-0.8 with a level stretch that keeps the bright metacarpal region below saturation).
- QUESTION: What bone detail is hidden in the overexposed hand and the faint forearm shafts?
- RATIONALE: The image has two opposite exposure problems (saturated hand, underexposed forearm). Adaptive local equalization normalizes contrast in each region independently, which a single global window cannot do simultaneously.
- EXPECTED INFORMATION: Recovered carpal/metacarpal cortical detail; higher-contrast forearm cortices so the mid-forearm irregularity can be re-examined; a view where both ends of the extremity are readable at once.
- ARTIFACT RISKS: CLAHE can amplify noise in the dark background into mottled texture (fake "soft-tissue" or "fissure" appearances); contrast limits must be set so bone edges do not develop ringing; enhanced noise may mimic a hairline lucency.
- COMPARISON: View side-by-side with the original; any "new" lucency or break that appears only in the enhanced image must be checked for noise origin before being trusted; the global window version should be used to re-check the hand region against the original's saturation.
- PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: ID003-clahe-global-window.png

2.
- ANALYSIS: Full-image edge map: gradient magnitude (Sobel) and/or Laplacian, plus unsharp masking (original + k*(original - blurred)) at two strengths (gentle, strong).
- QUESTION: Does the mid-forearm irregularity correspond to a true cortical break/step-off, or is it superimposition/processing artifact?
- RATIONALE: A real cortical break should appear as a local discontinuity (offset or gap) in the cortical edge line; superimposition of two intact cortices should give parallel continuous edges. An edge map exaggerates exactly this kind of structure.
- EXPECTED INFORMATION: A clear line trace of the radial and ulnar cortices through the mid-forearm; a visible gap/step at the irregularity if a break exists; confirmation of parallel intact cortices if it is overlap.
- ARTIFACT RISKS: Edge maps respond to ANY intensity change - noise, beam streaks, and the faint superimposed cortex can all light up; over-strong unsharp masking creates halos around bright bones that can fake a second cortical line; double-edge (bright-dark pair) patterns are normal for any thin high-contrast edge and must not be read as a fracture gap.
- COMPARISON: Overlay/compare directly with the original crop at the same coordinates: the "edge" of interest must sit exactly on the cortical line in the original; edge features present only in the edge map at other locations are suspect.
- PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: ID003-edge-enhance-forearm.png

- RATIONALE: A hairline transverse fracture should produce a local minimum in intensity along a transverse profile and/or a vertical displacement between cortex edges in the longitudinal profile; superimposition of intact cortices should produce a smooth double-peak without a central dip or offset.
- EXPECTED INFORMATION: Numeric dip depth (in intensity units) and width (in pixels) at the suspected fracture level; cortex offset in pixels; width of the forearm silhouette at y 0.38 vs y 0.42 (a width change suggests displacement or swelling).
- ARTIFACT RISKS: A 1-2 pixel dip may be quantization/noise rather than a fracture - compare dip depth to the noise floor of a known-intact adjacent level; sampling-row jitter matters (average several rows); the profile must be drawn exactly through the cortical line, not through the medullary cavity, or it will measure the wrong structure.
- COMPARISON: Run identical profiles at a normal-looking adjacent level (e.g., y 0.30 and y 0.48) as an internal control; only a dip clearly exceeding the control's noise level is meaningful.
