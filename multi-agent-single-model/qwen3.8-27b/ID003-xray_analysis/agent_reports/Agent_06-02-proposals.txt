PROPOSAL 1 — Full-image CLAHE (contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalization)
================================================================================
- ANALYSIS: Apply CLAHE (tile ~8x8, clip limit ~2.0-3.0) to the whole 1280x1600
  image, keeping output 8-bit PNG.
- QUESTION: Can local contrast recovery reveal additional subtle findings
  (elbow, distal forearm, carpus) that are washed out or too low-contrast on
  the original?
- RATIONALE: The image has strong global dynamic range (black background,
  bright trunk on the right, washed-out carpus). CLAHE redistributes local
  histogram, improving visibility in both the dark and the overexposed
  regions without a second exposure.
- EXPECTED INFORMATION: Sharper cortical outlines at the elbow and distal
  radius; carpal trabecular detail; confirmation or refutation of the
  mid-forearm lucency with better context.
- ARTIFACT RISKS: Amplification of background noise and quantum mottle;
  edge halos (blocking) around high-contrast cortices; CLAHE can make noise
  look like fine lucency/cortical irregularity — do not over-read texture.
- COMPARISON: Overlay/compare side-by-side with the original at identical
  scale; any "new" finding must also be at least faintly traceable on the
  original to be credited.
- PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: ID003_06_clahe_full.png

- COMPARISON: Verify every apparent line exists on the original at the same

PROPOSAL 3 — Wrist/carpal crop with CLAHE (overexposure recovery)
================================================================================
- ANALYSIS: Crop normalized region x 0.35-0.70, y 0.58-0.88 (pixels approx
  x 448-896, y 928-1408), apply CLAHE with higher clip limit (~3.0) to
  compress the bright bone region; optionally a second output with a narrow
  linear window mapped to the upper percentiles of the histogram.
- QUESTION: Are there occult carpal or distal-radius fractures, or carpal
  malalignment, masked by overexposure (Q3)?
- RATIONALE: The carpal region is rendered nearly uniform white on the
  original; local histogram compression is the standard way to recover
  detail in saturated/overexposed bone areas from a single digital
  radiograph.
- EXPECTED INFORMATION: Carpal bone outlines and intercarpal spacing;
  scaphoid outline; distal radius articular surface; any lucent fracture
  line or step-off at the wrist.
- ARTIFACT RISKS: In bright, low-information regions, CLAHE mostly
  amplifies noise — apparent "lines" may be mottle; risk of false-positive
  "fracture" reading from noise texture.
- COMPARISON: Cross-check each suspected line against the original (it must
  be faintly present) and against the high-pass map (Proposal 4); carpal
  alignment judged against the expected arc of the carpal row on a lateral
  wrist.

PROPOSAL 4 — High-pass (unsharp/hat) filter, full image, for cortical tracing
================================================================================
- ANALYSIS: Subtract a heavily blurred version of the image (Gaussian sigma
  ~15-20 px) from the original (high-pass), then render the result as a
  mid-gray-centered PNG; also produce a version limited to the forearm+hand
  ROI.
- QUESTION: Where exactly are the high-contrast cortical edges, and does
  any cortical margin show a discontinuity (Q1)?
- RATIONALE: High-pass filtering suppresses soft-tissue and background
  gradients, leaving edges (cortices) — ideal for visually tracing cortical
  continuity across the suspected lesion and around the elbow/wrist.
- EXPECTED INFORMATION: A clean edge map of all cortical margins; any break,
  step, or angular deviation in the diaphyseal cortex becomes conspicuous.
- ARTIFACT RISKS: High-pass response at a true fracture gap produces a
  bright-dark-bright triplet that must not be confused with a normal two-
  edge cortex; scatter from the trunk on the right edge creates false
  edges; motion blur smears edges and could be misread as a lucency.
- COMPARISON: Trace each cortical line back onto the original; compare edge
  spacing across the lesion (normal cortex thickness is locally constant;
  a fracture gap changes the spacing pattern).
- PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: ID003_06_highpass.png

PROPOSAL 5 — Edge detection (Canny) on the mid-forearm crop
================================================================================
- ANALYSIS: On the Proposal 2 crop (before upscaling), run Gaussian
  smoothing (sigma ~1.5) then Canny edge detection (dual threshold tuned to
  the image's gradient histogram); render edges white on black.
- QUESTION: Objective confirmation of cortical continuity vs disruption at
  the mid-forearm lesion (Q1)?
- RATIONALE: Canny gives a binary, quantifiable trace of strong gradients —
  useful to decide whether the suspected lucency is a real gap (edges
  terminate/displace) or a projection/blur artifact (edges remain parallel
  and continuous).
- EXPECTED INFORMATION: Binary edge skeleton of both diaphyseal cortices;
  a true cortical break shows edge termination or angular deflection;
  callus shows a widened, irregular edge zone.
- ARTIFACT RISKS: Canny is threshold-sensitive — wrong thresholds drop
  real edges or keep noise edges; blur can merge adjacent parallel cortices
  into one edge; does not distinguish bone from other high-gradient
  interfaces.
- COMPARISON: Overlay Canny edges at 50% opacity on the original crop;
  edges that cannot be matched to a visible gradient on the original are
  rejected.
- PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: ID003_06_edges_midforearm.png

PROPOSAL 7 — Soft-tissue window of the hand (swelling/air assessment)
================================================================================
- ANALYSIS: Crop normalized region x 0.25-0.70, y 0.60-1.00 (pixels approx
  x 320-896, y 960-1600); suppress bone by low-pass/high-pass hybrid:
  either (a) subtract a strongly blurred version to show soft-tissue
  gradients, or (b) stretch the lower histogram range (soft-tissue window)
  to amplify small density differences in the soft tissues.
- QUESTION: Is there true soft-tissue swelling around the fingers/hand, or
  any soft-tissue gas/foreign body (Q4)?
- RATIONALE: On a standard bone window, soft-tissue differences are
  compressed; a soft-tissue window or bone-suppressed rendering reveals
  contour bulging and low-density gas, which are easy to miss on the
  original.
- EXPECTED INFORMATION: Symmetry of soft-tissue contours finger-by-finger;
  any circumferential bulge; any lucent gas streaks; the nature of the

- ARTIFACT RISKS: Bone-subtraction can leave residual bone "ghosts" that
  mimic soft-tissue masses; a flexed fist is naturally bulky (baseline
  asymmetry is expected); compression artifacts/noise can mimic gas
  streaks.
- COMPARISON: Compare hand contour to forearm contour on the original
  (same patient, adjacent region); compare left vs right contour of the
  fist; gas must appear as clearly lucent (darker than muscle) streaks,

PROPOSAL 8 — Sharpness quantification (motion-blur check, Q2)
================================================================================
- ANALYSIS: Compute the variance of the Laplacian (or Tenengrad) in three
  ROIs: (a) mid-forearm lesion region (Proposal 2 box), (b) elbow
  (x 0.47-0.63, y 0.13-0.28), (c) distal radius metaphysis
  (x 0.45-0.58, y 0.55-0.65); also compute a horizontal luminance profile
  across the suspected lucency (mean intensity per column) to measure the
  width and depth of the dip. Output a PNG of the three ROI outlines with
  the metric values annotated, plus the profile plot.
- QUESTION: Is the mid-forearm lucency a real structure, or an artifact of
  motion blur / partial volume (Q2)?
- RATIONALE: Motion blur reduces edge sharpness and widens/smears lucencies
  preferentially along the motion direction; a sharpness metric per ROI
  plus a 1-D intensity profile distinguishes a narrow true lucent gap
  (sharp dip) from a broad smeared low (blur) or from background
  fluctuation.
- EXPECTED INFORMATION: Relative sharpness values (lesion ROI vs control
  ROIs); profile width (in pixels) and depth (in intensity units) of the
  suspected lucency; a narrow, deep, sharp dip supports a true lucent
  fracture line.
- ARTIFACT RISKS: ROI selection bias (placing the box to maximize/minimize
  the metric); compression noise affects variance; the profile is 1-D and
  can conflate a fracture gap with a shadow of an overlying structure if
  the crop is not centered exactly on the line.
- COMPARISON: Metric values must be interpreted relative to the control
  ROIs on the SAME image (same exposure/compression), not in absolute
  terms; the profile must be re-centered if the maximum gradient column is
  not at the line's expected position.
- PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: ID003_06_sharpness_profile.png
