=== PROPOSED ANALYSES ===

1. ANALYSIS: Mid-forearm crop with contrast enhancement (CLAHE,
   clip limit ~2.0, tile 8x8) + 2x bilinear upscale.
   Crop region (normalized): x 0.44-0.61, y 0.35-0.46.
   QUESTION: Is the faint transverse lucency at approx. x 0.50-0.56,
   y 0.395-0.41 a true cortical interruption (hairline/transverse
   diaphyseal fracture of the ulna, and possibly radius) or an artifact?
   RATIONALE: The finding is low-contrast relative to the diaphyseal
   cortex; local histogram equalization raises local contrast without the
   global washout of a whole-image stretch, and upscaling makes the
   cortex resolvable at pixel level.
   EXPECTED INFORMATION: A sharp, continuous cortex on both sides of the
   line (artifact) vs an interrupted/stepped cortex or a lucent gap
   crossing one or both bones (fracture); also any subtle periosteal
   elevation adjacent to it.
   ARTIFACT RISKS: CLAHE can amplify noise into false "lines"; upscale
   interpolation creates no real detail. A noise ridge may mimic a
   fracture line.
   COMPARISON: Overlay/compare side-by-side with the unprocessed same
   crop from the original; a genuine cortical break must be present in
   the original, just fainter.
   PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: ID003-crop-midforearm-clahe-2x.png

2. ANALYSIS: High-pass (unsharp-mask: original - Gaussian-blur, sigma
   ~2-3 px, then add ~25% of the difference) applied to the same
   mid-forearm crop (x 0.44-0.61, y 0.35-0.46), plus a full-forearm
   version (x 0.45-0.60, y 0.15-0.62).
   QUESTION: Do the four cortical margins of the radius and ulna show any
   discontinuity or step-off anywhere from elbow to wrist, especially at
   the transverse lucency level?
   RATIONALE: Edge/high-pass filtering suppresses low-frequency shading
   (exposure gradient, soft-tissue shadow) and emphasizes high-frequency
   cortical edges, making small steps visible.
   EXPECTED INFORMATION: A traceable, unbroken cortical edge line along
   each bone, or a clear break/offset at one location.
   ARTIFACT RISKS: Grid lines, moiré, and quantization noise produce
   spurious edges; bone ends (physes) light up strongly and can be
   confused with injury; strong blurring can split one edge into two.
   COMPARISON: Verify every candidate break by returning to the original
   at the same location; confirm edges in the original before believing
   the map.
   PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: ID003-forearm-highpass-edge.png

3. ANALYSIS: Global illumination normalization / background subtraction
   (estimate a smooth background via heavy Gaussian or morphological
   dilation, subtract, then re-add a constant) applied to the whole
   image; then display the normalized full image.
   QUESTION: Does the uneven brightness (dark left background,
   burned-out right edge) hide low-contrast structure, and does the
   mid-forearm lucency persist after flat-field correction?
   RATIONALE: Correcting the illumination gradient lets a uniform window
   be used over the whole image, improving visibility of faint structures
   on the dark side and recovering some gradation near the bright edge.
   EXPECTED INFORMATION: A uniformly exposed image in which the
   diaphyseal lucency, periosteum, and soft-tissue outline can be judged
   without local re-windowing; assessment of the right-edge region
   (limited, since burned-out data is unrecoverable).
   ARTIFACT RISKS: Over-subtraction rings around high-density bone;
   residual vignette; the overexposed right edge may show banding rather
   than anatomy.
   COMPARISON: Compare with original; anatomy and all candidate findings
   must be present in both, only the contrast distribution should differ.
   PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: ID003-flatfield-normalized.png

4. ANALYSIS: Bone window (linear level/width) and soft-tissue window

   EXPECTED INFORMATION: Sharper cortical margins and trabeculae (bone

   ARTIFACT RISKS: Windowing exaggerates noise in soft-tissue regions;

   COMPARISON: Judge each window against the same region in the original;
