B. DISAGREEMENT D1 — SKELETAL MATURITY
================================================================

Assessment. Four of six first-passes (A02, A03, A04, A05) read the
skeleton as PEDIATRIC, citing open physes at elbow/wrist, small
incomplete carpal ossification centers, metaphyseal flaring, and
separate rounded distal phalangeal epiphyses. A01 and A06 never
committed to a maturity statement. No agent reported evidence FOR an
adult skeleton: nobody described closed physes, a fused carpal mass,
or absence of epiphyseal centers. The "markedly dense carpal zone"
flagged by A02/A03/A06 is compatible with either (superimposed small
centers in a child vs fused dense carpus in an adult), so it is
genuinely double-edged and cannot break the tie. Weight of first-pass
evidence favors pediatric, but the image has not been processed to
prove it, and maturity matters: it changes (i) the differential for
the mid-forearm lucency (physes are normal lucencies only at the
metaphysis — all agents correctly place the lucency in the DIAPHYSIS,
so this does not itself change the call, but it does change the
misread risk everywhere else) and (ii) the expected carpal count.
Status: PEDIATRIC favored, UNRESOLVED from available evidence.

Which analyses resolve it (in order of decisiveness):
1. Wrist crop (T9) + carpal census (A04 M3). Evidence pattern:
   - Pediatric: 2-5 discrete, rounded, smooth-margined carpal
     ossification centers with empty intercarpal spaces; pattern
     consistent with the maturation sequence (capitate/hamate
     earliest, then scaphoid, triquetrum/lna, trapezoid/trapezium —
     approximate ages vary by source).
   - Adult: no discrete centers — a single fused carpal mass, and no
     lucent physeal line at the distal radius/ulna.
2. Physeal-width ratio at the distal radius (A05 M1). A visible
   lucent physeal line (physeal width > 0, ratio physeal/shaft
   width > 0) = open = pediatric; cortical continuity across the
   metaphyseal-epiphyseal junction = closed = adult. The dimensionless
   ratio is the right design given the missing physical scale.
3. Elbow epiphyseal census (my addition F2): number of visible
   elbow ossification centers (capitellum, radial head, medial
   epicondyle, trochlea, olecranon) independently brackets age, and
   the elbow is a LESS degraded site than the wrist (no hand
   superimposition, less overexposure), making it the better tie-
   breaker when the distal radius is oblique.
4. Distal phalangeal epiphyses (noted by A03): separate rounded
   epiphyseal centers = pediatric; fused to shafts = adult. Visible
   in the hand/wrist crops already being produced.
Decision rule I propose: ONE clear lucent physeal line at the distal
radius suffices for "open (pediatric)"; absence of any lucent physis
PLUS a fused carpal mass = adult; if the distal radius is ambiguous
under obliquity, defer to the elbow census (F2).

C. DISAGREEMENT D2 — RIGHT-EDGE / LOWER-RIGHT STRUCTURE
================================================================

Assessment. I think the council is slightly conflating two structures,
and splitting them sharpens the disagreement:
(i) The extreme right-edge band (x ~0.88-1.00): all six agents agree
    this is out-of-field torso/axillary/shoulder superimposition,
    overexposed. Not disputed. (A02 P8's gamma attempt on it I oppose:
    the data is clipped and unrecoverable — E.2.)
(ii) The lower-right oblique long bones (A05: x ~0.55-0.75 from the
    bottom edge up to a wrist-like cluster at x ~0.45-0.60, y ~0.88-
    0.95, with a carpal-like cluster at x ~0.60-0.80, y ~0.78-0.93):
    DISPUTED. Five agents assigned these pixels to the examined
    hand's rotated metacarpals/phalanges (A01 metacarpals x 0.35-
    0.59 y 0.69-0.91; A02 carpus/metacarpal bases x 0.38-0.64 y
    0.66-0.90; etc.); only A05 reads them as a SECOND (contralateral)
    forearm/hand entering the field from the bottom edge. This is
    material: if A05 is right, part of the "dense carpal zone" is the
    OTHER hand, the examined hand's non-assessable zone is larger, and
    the small dense focus at x~0.63, y~0.82 (flagged by A04/A05) may
    belong to the second limb rather than the examined hand.

Key anatomical discriminator (what would count as evidence):
- The examined wrist is already identified proximally (distal radial/
  ulnar epiphyses at y ~0.63-0.70, with the carpal chain per A04/A05).
  If the lower-right oblique bones are CONTINUOUS with that wrist,
  they are the examined hand's metacarpals (majority hypothesis). If
  they articulate with a SECOND wrist-like cluster at y ~0.88-0.95,
  A05's hypothesis stands.
- Metacarpal count: exactly 5 shafts converging on the dense zone
  favors one hand; more than 5 favors two.
- Entry geometry: a single rotated hand should enter the field via
  its carpus, not via distal-forearm shafts with their own epiphyses
  at the bottom edge (A05's specific observation).
- One-vs-two wrist joints visible in a single field spanning y
  0.55-1.00 is the cleanest single-image test.

Which proposed analyses resolve it:
1. A05 A7 (crop x 0.40-0.88, y 0.76-1.00; CLAHE + Sobel/Laplacian
   overlay; explicit labeling of which edges belong to limb 1 vs limb
   2) — the most direct proposal in the union for D2. SUPPORT with the
   ambiguity condition stated above.
2. A single crop spanning y ~0.55-1.00, x ~0.38-0.88 (the union of
   A01 P6 and A05 A7 boxes) so the ONE-vs-TWO-wrist-joint test is
   visible in one frame, with the plain version (T4) as reference.
3. Metacarpal census traced on the hand crop (T10): count shafts,
   record bases-to-carpus continuity.
4. T15 saturation check for the lower-right zone: if that zone is
   clipped, the second-limb question may be partially "not assessable
   for exposure" and must be said so.
What would settle it: one identifiable second wrist joint at y
~0.88-0.95 that is not continuous with the proximal examined wrist,
or >5 metacarpal shafts. What would refute A05: all oblique bones
tracing continuously to the single proximal wrist, total 5 shafts,
and the "cluster" being the examined hand's own carpus + metacarpal
bases. I cannot resolve D2 from the text evidence available to me;
it is preserved for the processed outputs.

F1. ANALYSIS: Aligned profile difference (focal rows minus control
    row, cross-correlation aligned). Take the transverse profiles
    from T12: for each of the 2-3 rows through the lucency, shift
    against the control-level profile by integer pixel offset to
    maximize correlation, subtract, and plot the three delta curves
    (and their mean) against a noise-floor band estimated from two
    control levels subtracted from each other.
    QUESTION: Is the intensity anomaly at the lucency level a
    consistent, localized feature (same x-position, same sign,
    row-stable) rather than row-to-row noise?
    RATIONALE: Raw profiles (T12) still require the reader to visually
    match features across curves; alignment + subtraction converts
    "is the dip real" into a single signed delta that must repeat
    across rows. A true cortical feature produces a consistent delta;
    quantization noise and texture do not.
    EXPECTED INFORMATION: A signed delta curve with a noise-floor
    band; a peak beyond the band at a stable x-position supports a
    real cortical feature; a delta within the band supports
    noise/shadow.
    ARTIFACT RISKS: Cross-correlation can lock onto the wrong peak if
    the control row has a similar dip; alignment jitter of +/-1 px
    smears the delta; 8-bit quantization sets a hard noise floor that
    a narrow 1-2 px dip may not exceed.
    COMPARISON: Delta peak must coincide spatially with the lucency
    seen in the plain crop (T4) and with the T12 raw-dip x-position;
    report the correlation offset used.
    PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: ID003-profile-delta-midforearm.png
    (with numeric table in the T12 report text).

F2. ANALYSIS: Elbow ossification-center census (maturity tie-breaker).
    On the T8 elbow crop, enumerate which distal-humerus/proximal-
    radius/ulna epiphyseal centers are visible (capitellum, radial
    head, medial epicondyle, trochlea, olecranon) with (x,y)
    coordinates, and record the physeal status (open lucent line vs
    closed) at the proximal radius and proximal ulna.
    QUESTION: How many elbow epiphyseal ossification centers are
    present, and are the proximal forearm physes open or closed?
    RATIONALE: The carpal census (A04 M3) is the primary D1 test but
    sits in the overexposed, superimposed wrist — the degraded site.
    The elbow is partially in field but far LESS degraded (no hand
    superimposition, better exposure), and its center maturation
    sequence independently brackets skeletal age; two independent
    sites agreeing is far stronger than one. No agent proposed the
    elbow as an age site; all elbow proposals target fracture only.
    EXPECTED INFORMATION: Count and positions of visible elbow
    centers (e.g., capitellum + radial head only = younger child;
    all five = older child/adolescent); open vs closed proximal
    physes; a second, independent maturity bracket to combine with
    the carpal census.
    ARTIFACT RISKS: Oblique/partial elbow view may hide a center that
    is actually present (undercount); CLAHE halos around small centers
    can fake an extra center; tangential projection can make an open
    physis look wider than it is.
    COMPARISON: Each marked center must be visible in the plain elbow
    crop (T4) and in the original full image; compare the bracket

    PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: ID003-elbow-centers-annotated.png
