ANALYSIS 1: Global CLAHE contrast enhancement
====================================================================
ANALYSIS: Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization over the whole
  image (gray scale, tile ~8x8 of image blocks, clip limit 2.0-3.0).
  Pure visualization transformation, no geometry change.
QUESTION: U1, U3, U5 (global) — are there low-contrast structures
  (faint cortical lines, soft-tissue margins, carpal outlines) invisible
  at standard viewing of the 8-bit PNG?
RATIONALE: The image has a wide intensity span (black background to
  saturated body). Global histogram stretching would be dominated by the
  saturated body region (x>0.88); CLAHE enhances local contrast while
  limiting noise amplification, preserving the dark-background context.
EXPECTED INFORMATION: Sharper cortical margins in the forearm shafts;
  clearer carpal and metacarpal outlines; visible soft-tissue envelopes
  around the fingers; possible detection of faint periosteal lines.
ARTIFACT RISKS: Noise amplification in low-density regions (background,
  fat), halo/banding artifacts at strong edges (bone borders), possible
  creation of false "lines" from amplifying detector noise; saturated
  body region cannot be recovered.
COMPARISON: View side-by-side with the original at identical zoom; any
  candidate new line/edge must be confirmable (at least partially) in the
  original before being reported as a finding.
PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: ID003-A1-clahe-full.png

ANALYSIS 3: 1-D intensity profiles across the mid-forearm (quantitative)
====================================================================
ANALYSIS: Extract horizontal intensity profiles (grayscale 0-255) across
  both forearm bones at the focal-line level (y = 0.345, 0.365, 0.385,
  x 0.46-0.60) and at two control levels without the finding (y = 0.44,
  0.48). Compute per-bone: cortical peak positions (x), cortical widths
  (FWHM of the bright peaks), and any secondary local minima/maxima
  between cortex and medulla. Tabulate in a text output alongside the
  annotated profile plot.
QUESTION: U1 — does the focal line correspond to a discrete, sharp,
  localized intensity change (real cortical feature) or to a broad,
  low-amplitude variation consistent with superimposition?
RATIONALE: A true cortical edge produces a steep, localized intensity
  gradient at a consistent x-position across several rows; a
  superimposition band is broader and less consistent. Comparing against
  control rows quantifies what "normal" cortex looks like at this kV/
  exposure.
EXPECTED INFORMATION: Numerical gradient amplitudes and widths; x-stability
  of the feature across rows; side-specific widths (radius vs ulna
  contour change, i.e., any local shaft widening suggesting callus).
ARTIFACT RISKS: Detector noise and 8-bit quantization add spurious
  steps; beam hardening and patient motion create broad intensity drifts;
  choosing the wrong threshold may split or merge the two bones' peaks.
COMPARISON: Compare focal-row profiles with the two control rows at the
  same x-range; a feature is "real-looking" only if its gradient and
  amplitude exceed the control rows' noise floor by a clear margin.
PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: ID003-A3-midforearm-profiles.png (profile plot;

ANALYSIS 5: Hand bone window (metacarpals/phalanges)
====================================================================
ANALYSIS: Crop x 0.26-0.66, y 0.62-0.96 (examined hand), bone window
  (percentile clip emphasizing cortical density range), 2x upscale, mild
  sharpening.
QUESTION: U3 — are there metacarpal fractures (especially necks),
  phalangeal fractures, or MCP/PIP dislocations hidden by the flexed-fist
  superimposition?
RATIONALE: A bone window suppresses soft tissue and spreads cortical
  intensity across the display range, making fine cortical lines of the
  metacarpals/phalanges traceable despite obliquity.
EXPECTED INFORMATION: Traceable cortical margins from metacarpal base to

ARTIFACT RISKS: Clipping of dense superimposed bone (carpal mass,

  halos that can be mistaken for fracture lines; the flexion
  superimposition itself is NOT fixed by any window — residual
  non-assessability must be stated.
COMPARISON: Trace each metacarpal axis on the original; any "fracture
  line" must be visible on the original at the same orientation before
  being reported.

QUESTION: Is the radial physis widened relative to the shaft (a screen

RATIONALE: Absolute mm measurement is impossible (no scale); a

EXPECTED INFORMATION: 3 ratio values; comparison against expected narrow
  physeal appearance for this age band (qualitative flag if widened).
ARTIFACT RISKS: Physeal obliquity (measuring perpendicular vs along the

  width unreliable; the physis is only partly on this oblique view.
COMPARISON: Compare the ratio between the 3 measurement points (internal
  consistency) and against the ulnar physis ratio as a second internal
  reference.
PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: ID003-M1-physeal-width-annotated.png (annotated
  crop with the 3 measurement lines; values in report text)

M2. Shaft-axial angulation at the focal-line level
ANALYSIS: Fit straight lines (in image coordinates) to the medial and
  lateral cortices of each forearm bone over two segments — proximal to
  and distal to the focal line (each segment ~0.08 image-height long) —
  and compute the angle between proximal and distal segments for each
  bone; also compute the interosseous space width (px) at the focal level
  and at control levels.
QUESTION: Is there any measurable angular deformity or shaft widening at
  the focal-line level (supporting a healing-fracture/callus
  interpretation)?
RATIONALE: A healed/healing fracture typically shows mild angulation or
  focal widening; a pure superimposition artifact shows neither.
EXPECTED INFORMATION: Angles (degrees, in image space) and width deltas
  (px) at focal vs control levels.
ARTIFACT RISKS: Cortex fitting is sensitive to noise and to the
  superimposed interosseous membrane shadow; 1-2 degrees of image-plane
  angulation is easily within fitting error and must NOT be over-
  interpreted; no 3D correction possible.
COMPARISON: Focal-level values vs control-level values from the same
  bones; only differences beyond the fitting noise (assessed by repeated
  fits with slightly shifted windows) are reportable.
PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: ID003-M2-angulation-annotated.png (annotated
  crop with fitted lines; values in report text)

M3. Wrist position angle (forearm axis vs hand axis)
ANALYSIS: Define the forearm axis from the mid-diaphyseal centerline

QUESTION: How oblique/flexed is the position (characterizing the

RATIONALE: Quantifies the non-standard position, which drives most of

EXPECTED INFORMATION: A single angle in image-plane degrees.
ARTIFACT RISKS: Axis tracing is subjective on an oblique view; the value

COMPARISON: None needed; report the value with a description of the

PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: ID003-M3-position-annotated.png (full image
