T1. Full-image exposure normalization (CLAHE / percentile stretch /
    flat-field / bone window)
    Proposers: A01(P1,P2), A02(P1), A03(P1), A04(P3,P4), A05(A1), A06(P1)
    Verdict: SUPPORT (consolidate to at most 2 outputs)
    Six agents proposed five variants of the same fix for the same two
    opposite exposure problems (dark left background, saturated hand/right
    edge). One full-image CLAHE plus one linear bone window is sufficient;
    the standalone percentile stretch (A01-P1) and flat-field/background
    subtraction (A04-P3) are redundant with CLAHE on this image and add
    ring/banding risk around dense bone. The CLAHE output is a context
    re-scan, not a primary deliverable: the regional windows in T3-T5 answer
    the actual questions.

T2. Mid-forearm focal lesion: magnified crop + local enhancement
    Proposers: all six (A01-P4, A02-P4, A03-P3, A04-P1, A05-A2, A06-P2)
    Verdict: SUPPORT (top priority; every variant justified in intent)
    Unanimous top priority, and the proposed boxes converge tightly
    (x ~0.40-0.64, y ~0.28-0.52), which de-risks crop placement. The union
    should produce ONE enhanced crop (moderate CLAHE + mild unsharp, 2-4x)
    paired with a plain nearest-neighbor 2x reference crop (A04-M4, A05-C2):
    a true 1-2 px lucency can be smoothed away by smooth 4x interpolation,
    so the plain twin is the ground truth the enhanced version must agree
    with. All six agents correctly identified the failure mode (halo/ringing
    faking a second cortical line); the plan must keep unsharp amount low.

T3. Wrist/carpal crop (overexposed zone + distal physes)
    Proposers: A01(P6), A02(P2), A03(P4), A04(P7), A05(A4), A06(P3)
    Verdict: SUPPORT
    Serves two questions at once: occult carpal/distal-radius injury and
    disagreement D1 (maturity). Boxes converge on x ~0.42-0.64,
    y ~0.50-0.80. Use a bone window (percentile clip) as the primary
    processing rather than CLAHE alone, since the issue here is a
    compressed bright bone range, and include the distal radial/ulnar
    physes in frame (A05-A4, A04-P7 do; A02-P2's box is too high and would
    cut them off). Must ship with a plain crop twin (A05-C1).

T4. Hand crop (metacarpals/phalanges, bone window)
    Proposers: A01(P7), A02(P5), A03(P4), A04(P8), A05(A5), A06(P7)
    Verdict: SUPPORT-WITH-CAUTION
    Justified, but the ceiling is low: flexed-fist superimposition is
    geometric and no window fixes it, and the histogram (T10) may show much
    of the metacarpal mass is clipped, making parts of this crop
    unrecoverable by construction. Worth one output to assess the
    least-superimposed shafts and the small dense focus near x~0.63,
    y~0.82 (A04) and to delineate the non-assessable zone. Any "fracture
    line" claimed here must exist on the plain crop.

T5. Elbow crop
    Proposers: A01(P5), A02(P3), A03(P5), A04(P6), A05(A8), A06(sharpness ROI)
    Verdict: SUPPORT-WITH-CAUTION
    Mostly non-assessable (partial FOV, oblique), so it must not be
    over-promised; but it is cheap and it is one of the two key D1
    (maturity) sites because the elbow ossification centers (capitellum,
    radial head, olecranon) have a well-known appearance sequence. Keep
    ONE crop (x ~0.44-0.66, y ~0.08-0.30) with mild enhancement; explicit
    "not assessable for subtle injury" labeling per A05-A8.

T6. Edge / high-pass / Canny maps (full limb or forearm band)
    Proposers: A01(P3,P8), A02(P4), A03(P2), A04(P2,P5), A05(A7 partly),
    A06(P4,P5)
    Verdict: SUPPORT-WITH-CAUTION (consolidate to one primary product)
    The underlying question (cortical continuity from elbow to wrist) is
    real and an edge product is the cleanest way to show it, so keep ONE:
    a high-pass/unsharp band over the full forearm (x ~0.45-0.60,
    y ~0.15-0.62). A Canny overlay is an acceptable companion but a
    second full edge product is redundant. Two caveats to enforce: (1)
    physes light up strongly on edge maps (A04 flagged this) - on a
    pediatric skeleton the growth plates at both ends will dominate and
    must not be read as injury; (2) double-edge (bright-dark pair) patterns
    are normal for any thin edge and must not be read as a fracture gap
    (my own Phase-2 note, kept).

T7. Soft-tissue window (hand + distal forearm)
    Proposers: A01(P9), A04(P4b), A05(A6), A06(P7)
    Verdict: SUPPORT (one output)
    Indirect-sign check (swelling, gas) that no bone window provides.
    Limitations are well stated by the proposers (no contralateral side,
    a child's fist is naturally bulky, 8-bit noise in the dark background),
    so the output should support a visual digit-to-digit comparison, not a
    numeric swelling index (see D).

T8. 1-D intensity profiles with control levels (mid-forearm)
    Proposers: A02(P6), A03(P6), A05(A3), A06(P8 profile part)
    Verdict: SUPPORT (core quantitative deliverable)
    This is the single most discriminating measurement in the union: a
    sharp, narrow, row-stable dip exceeding the noise floor at adjacent
    control levels supports a true lucent line; a broad, row-unstable
    gradient supports shadow/superimposition. Consolidate the four variants
    into one plot set: 3-5 transverse rows through the lesion
    (y ~0.385-0.415) plus 2 control levels (y ~0.30, ~0.48), plus
    longitudinal cortex profiles; report dip depth/width in intensity units
    and px against the control noise floor.

T9. Axis tracing / angulation / step-off measurement
    Proposers: A01(P10, M1-M3), A02(P7), A04(M2), A05(M2,M3), A06(P6, M1-M3)

    of image-plane angle is within fit error on an oblique view. The
    wrist-position angle (A05-M3) is descriptive only - include it on the
    same overlay, not as a separate output.

B. DISAGREEMENT D1 (SKELETAL MATURITY)
================================================================
Assessment: The pediatric reading (A02, A04, A05, and my own tentative
first pass: slender bones, rounded epiphyseal centers on distal phalanges,
metaphyseal flaring, small carpal centers) is the better-supported
hypothesis at present: four agents independently saw open physes and small
carpal centers, versus two agents (A01, A06) who simply did not commit.
Non-commitment is weaker evidence for adult than the positive physeal
observations are for pediatric. But the four pediatric readings are
impressions from full-scale viewing, and on an oblique view with a
superimposed second limb (if D2 is real), both under- and over-counting of
ossification centers is plausible. So: lead with "consistent with
pediatric/adolescent; confirm by census" rather than declaring maturity.

Which analyses resolve it, in order of value:
1. Wrist crop (T3) + carpal census (T12): count and position of carpal
   centers is the single most informative maturity test on this image.
   Pattern supporting PEDIATRIC: 3-5 small, rounded, smoothly margined
   centers with multiple empty carpal positions (sequence: scaphoid
   ~6-12 mo, capitate/hamate ~2-3 y, triquetrum/lunate ~3-5 y,
   trapezium/trapezoid ~5-7 y, remaining centers later). Pattern supporting
   ADULT: full 8-center carpus, no lucent physeal line at the distal
   radius/ulna (fusion), thicker cortices.
2. Elbow crop (T5): the elbow center set (capitellum first, then radial
   head, medial epicondyle, olecranon) is an independent, well-sequenced
   age marker; also shows distal-humeral physeal status.
3. Physeal width ratio (T13): a wide, lucent, continuous physis relative
   to shaft width = immature; a thin or absent physeal line = maturing/
   adult. Doubles as a physeal-injury screen.
4. Hand crop (T4): separate rounded epiphyseal centers on distal phalanges
   (as I noted in first pass) = immature; their absence with fused
   epiphyses = mature.
5. Edge band (T6) incidentally shows physeal lines as strong horizontal
   edges at both ends of the forearm - a corroborating, not primary, sign.
Unresolved by anything in this plan: exact skeletal age, and whether any
"missing" carpal center is truly absent or hidden by the D2 superimposition.
The census must report positions of EMPTY expected centers too, and flag
those that lie in the second-limb overlap zone as indeterminate.

C. DISAGREEMENT D2 (RIGHT-EDGE / LOWER-RIGHT STRUCTURE)
================================================================
Assessment: First, the union actually contains two claims that must not be
merged: (i) the saturated structure at the extreme right edge
(x ~0.88-1.00, y ~0.35-1.00) - all six agents agree this is body/torso
superimposition, out of focus, largely burned out; there is no real
disagreement here; and (ii) A05's claim of a SECOND (contralateral)
forearm/hand in the lower right (two oblique long bones from the bottom
edge x ~0.55-0.75, y ~1.0 up to a wrist-like joint at x ~0.45-0.60,
y ~0.88-0.95, with carpal-like centers at x ~0.62-0.78, y ~0.80-0.92) that
overlaps the examined hand. The disagreement is about (ii) only.

I find A05's hypothesis plausible but unproven from the available evidence.
In its favor: the examined wrist is already identified proximally at
y ~0.63-0.66 (all agents), so bones below/right of it cannot be the same
limb's carpus unless the limb loops implausibly; the described chain
(two long bones + a wrist-like joint + a separate carpal cluster) is
anatomically self-consistent; and a second pediatric limb with its own
ossification centers would look exactly like small rounded densities.
Against it: no other agent, looking at the same image, committed to a
second limb; the lower-right zone is also where the overexposed trunk and
the external wire/fiber artifact sit, so "two oblique long bones" could be
trunk edge + artifact + mis-traced metacarpals; and A05's own first pass
confirms the claim came from self-generated zoomed crops, i.e., it is a
crop-level reading that the full-scale reviewers did not share.

Which proposed analyses would resolve it:
1. A05-A7 (T14): the lower-right crop with CLAHE + edge overlay, tracing
   which edges belong to the oblique long-bone pair and which to the
   examined hand's carpus/metacarpals, with explicit ambiguity marking.
   This is the decisive instrument for (ii).
2. T10 histogram + F3 spatial saturation map, extended to the lower-right
   region: if much of that zone is clipped, part of the "second limb"
   evidence may be unassessable rather than disproven.
3. T3 wrist crop (plain + windowed): if the examined hand's carpus is
   fully accounted for at y ~0.62-0.72, the bones below it need an
   independent explanation (second limb vs external object).
4. A02-P8 gamma/highlight recovery: only useful for the extreme right edge
   (i) if the histogram shows unclipped gradation there; full-image gamma
   is the wrong tool (see E).

What would count as evidence:
- For SECOND LIMB: the oblique bones form a continuous chain ending in a
  joint and carpal cluster that is NOT continuous with the examined
  forearm; the pair has its own physis/epiphysis pattern at the
  wrist-like joint (a second distal forearm would be nearly decisive);
  cortical caliber comparable to the examined forearm's; and the examined
  hand's carpus/metacarpals are traceable WITHOUT using the lower-right
  bones.
- For SINGLE LIMB + TRUNK/ARTIFACT: the "long bones" fail continuity
  (no joint, no distal continuation), their edges appear only in
  enhanced images (noise/texture), or they align with the trunk margin or
  the external wire artifact; the examined hand's carpus cannot be
  separated from them.
Whichever side wins, the practical consequence is the same: it defines
how much of the examined hand's carpal/metacarpal zone is non-assessable,
so T14 earns its slot even though it does not touch the mid-forearm
priority. I do not force a resolution here: with one view and one set of
proposals, the council can specify the test but not the verdict.

E. OBJECTIONS
================================================================
1. A03-P7 (my own, Phase 2): global binary bone mask overlay. Withdrawn -
   see T17. On this image the mask would merge the superimposed radius and
   ulna, include the torso and the wire artifact, and clip the hand, so a
   "break" in the mask is more likely an exposure or thresholding artifact
   than anatomy; its questions are answered by T6 + the width curve.
2. A01-P11 (inverted hand): no new information; a perceptual curiosity that
   spends an output slot. Opposed for the plan (T16).
3. A02-P8 (full-image gamma >1): full-image highlight compression lowers
   contrast everywhere, including the already-fine forearm, to recover a
   region (extreme right edge) that the histogram may show to be clipped
   anyway. Opposed as proposed. A regional highlight window on a small
   right-edge crop, gated on T10 showing unclipped gradation, is an
   acceptable substitute.
4. A04-P3 (flat-field/background subtraction): ring artifacts around dense
   bone and residual banding near the burned-out edge; on this image CLAHE
   already normalizes local illumination, so it is redundant (T1). Drop
   the standalone output.
5. Smooth 4x upsampling of the lesion crop (Lanczos in A01-P4/A05-A2/A06-P2
   framing): interpolation of a 1-2 px lucency can erase it or fake edges;
   require the plain nearest-neighbor twin (T15) and keep any smooth
   upscale at most 2x with enhancement kept mild.
6. A05-A7 "labeling" pixels as limb-1 vs limb-2: risk of over-confident
   assignment in genuinely mixed overlap zones; the output must carry an
   explicit "indeterminate" class. Supported only with that constraint.

F1. Ossification-center census map (elbow + wrist + hand, single annotated
    output)
- ANALYSIS: On the original (not enhanced), mark every visible
  ossification center and epiphyseal center - carpal (T12), elbow (capit-
  ellum, radial head, medial epicondyle, olecranon), distal radial/ulnar
  epiphyses, and distal phalangeal epiphyses - with (x,y) coordinates and
  an ID, and mark expected-but-empty carpal positions; produce a table
  listing present/absent per site.
- QUESTION: Does the complete set of centers, taken together, support a
  specific pediatric age band, or is the pattern adult?
- RATIONALE: D1 is currently answered by three separate crops (T3, T5, T4)
  whose findings have to be mentally combined; nobody proposed a single
  consolidated maturity map. The sequence of center appearance is the
  standard pediatric age indicator, and one annotated image plus one table
  makes the evidence checkable in a single view.
- EXPECTED INFORMATION: A present/absent matrix per center site, a
  consistent age band (e.g., "center set compatible with ~3-6 years"), or
  a pattern inconsistent with the pediatric reading.
- ARTIFACT RISKS: Overcount from noise in the saturated hand (mitigate:
  mark only centers visible on the original); undercount from super-
  imposition (mitigate: flag centers in the D2 overlap zone as indeter-
  minate); sequence tables are approximate guides, so report an age band,
  never a precise age.
- COMPARISON: Every marked center must be individually verifiable on the
  unprocessed original; enhanced crops (T3/T5) used only to find, not to
  confirm, centers.
- PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: ID003-ossification-census.png
  (report: ID003-ossification-census-report.txt)

F2. Difference image (enhanced minus plain) on the mid-forearm lesion crop
- ANALYSIS: From the T2 pair (enhanced crop minus its plain twin, same
  geometry and zoom), compute the pixel difference, rescale mid-gray-
  centered, and render it; any structure that is anatomy should appear
  weakly (it is present in both), while enhancement artifacts (halos,
  ringing, amplified noise) light up strongly.
- QUESTION: Does the mid-forearm lucency/irregularity persist as low-
  difference (real, present pre-enhancement) or high-difference (created
  or exaggerated by processing)?
- RATIONALE: Every agent's safeguard is "check the original by eye," which
  is slow and subjective for a 1-2 px feature; a difference image makes
  that check explicit and spatial. No agent proposed it.
- EXPECTED INFORMATION: A map localizing exactly which pixels the
  enhancement changed; confirmation that the focal line exists (faintly)
  in the plain crop, or refutation that it is a processing byproduct.
- ARTIFACT RISKS: Mis-registration between the two images (any zoom/
  crop/offset error creates ring artifacts) - the pair must be generated
  from identical coordinates before any processing; 8-bit quantization
  noise sets a floor, so use a perceptually flat mid-gray scale and
  report the difference's max/mean values.
- COMPARISON: Interpret against the plain crop (T2 twin) and the profile
  plot (T8): a line that is high-difference AND absent from the plain
  crop's profile dip is an artifact, full stop.
- PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: ID003-diff-midforearm.png

F3. Spatial saturation map (extension of my Phase-2 P9)
- ANALYSIS: Render a binary overlay on the original marking all pixels at
  or within 2 levels of the maximum value (truly clipped), in the hand,
  wrist, and right-edge regions; report clipped fraction per region
  alongside the T10 histograms.
- QUESTION: Which specific structures lie in unrecoverable (clipped)
  territory, as opposed to merely dark-on-display?
- RATIONALE: T10 gives percentages; a reviewer needs to know WHERE the
  clipping is to state "scaphoid not assessable because clipped" instead
  of "hand not assessable because overexposed." It also sets the ground
  truth for D2: if the lower-right "second limb" zone is largely clipped,
  part of that debate is undecidable from this file.
- EXPECTED INFORMATION: A spatial map of unrecoverable data; per-region
  clipped fractions; a defensible list of structures to report as "not
  assessable (clipped)" vs "not assessable (superimposition)."
- ARTIFACT RISKS: Near-saturated but not clipped pixels are recoverable;
  using the maximum value as the threshold (rather than a guessed one)
  keeps this objective; compression banding near white may make the 2-
  level margin noisy - report the margin used.
- COMPARISON: Compare against the T10 histograms (they must agree) and
  against the plain crops (T15) for spot checks.
- PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: ID003-saturation-map.png
