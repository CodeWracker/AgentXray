MISLEADING OR RISKY PROPOSALS:
- Agent_06 #3 as specified (global histogram equalization/gamma of the FULL image): highest artifact risk in the set. Global HE on an image with a large black-clipped background will amplify sensor noise into phantom soft-tissue structures and wash out the already-saturated cortices. The age/ossification-center question it serves is better answered by local crops and zoom, not global rescaling. Downgrade the method; keep the question.
- Agent_05 #3 and the straightening step of Agent_04 #3 (affine re-orientation to a canonical view): resampling/interpolation blurs exactly the fine cortical detail these tests are meant to evaluate, and on an oblique projection the "abnormal" radiocapitellar line may be pure projection effect. Alignment must be drawn on the unrotated original; straightening is acceptable only as a sanity check, never as the basis of a subluxation call.
- Agent_06 #2: the posterior humeral line criterion and the "pediatric elbow trauma" framing presume (a) a true lateral and (b) a pediatric patient. Neither is verified. If the view is oblique, this test produces a false "dislocation"; if the patient is skeletally mature, several of the differentials it implies (apophyseal avulsion, epiphyseal separation) are less relevant. Explicitly flag both assumptions before using it.
- Agent_02 #2 / Agent_04 #5: any "normal" read of the wrist is false confidence — the carpus and distal radius/ulna are truncated at the image border. These regions can only be reported as "intact where visible; incompletely assessed."
- Minor: Agent_03 #1's CLAHE halo artifacts adjacent to the humeral condyles are a plausible false fat-pad; the proposal itself flags this, so the risk is contained if the original-comparison rule is enforced.
