PURPOSE: Quantitative documentation of periarticular soft-tissue fullness: traced soft-tissue contour over the image plus a width profile (distal forearm vs proximal humerus width vs signed distance from the joint), to interpret where maximal width lies, the elbow-to-mid-forearm ratio, and the method's limitations.
OBSERVATIONS: Two-panel figure. LEFT panel: the original radiograph with a traced soft-tissue contour - cyan traces follow the humerus (upper arm) edges (superior edge from ~x=1080 to the right image border at ~x=1550; inferior edge from the elbow region to the right border), red traces follow the forearm edges from the elbow (y~420-480) down to the truncated bottom of the image (y~1230-1250). Notably, the red contour on the forearm's right (lateral/anterior) side is convex - it bulges outward near the top (y~420-600) before tapering more linearly distally; the left (medial/posterior) red contour is also slightly convex near the elbow. This convexity at the top of the forearm contour corresponds to periarticular fullness that tapers down the forearm. RIGHT panel: width profile vs signed distance from the joint origin (+ = distal/forearm, - = proximal/humerus). Blue (distal/forearm): starts ~380px at 0, falls to ~241px at +400px, and continues down to ~185-190px by ~+750px (truncated at image edge). Green (proximal/humerus): ~350px at -100px rising to ~410px at ~-480px (truncated at the right image border). Title annotation: "peak width 572px at +0px; mid-forearm(400px) 241px; ratio 2.37." So the maximal width is AT the joint (0px), the elbow:mid-forearm ratio is 2.37, and both limbs taper away from the joint.
RELEVANT FINDINGS: (1) The soft tissue is widest at the elbow joint and tapers in both directions, with a ratio of 2.37 versus the mid-forearm - quantitatively supporting the qualitative impression of periarticular soft-tissue fullness/swelling (effusion/hematoma/contusion) seen on the original and the soft-tissue window. (2) The maximal width (572px at 0px) sits exactly at the joint, consistent with a joint-centered process (hemarthrosis/effusion) rather than a distal forearm or upper-arm process. (3) The convex bulge in the red forearm contour near the elbow corroborates the profile's peak at the joint.
RELATION TO ORIGINAL IMAGE: Supported. The traced contours follow the actual visible soft-tissue boundaries on the original at the same locations (forearm edges, upper-arm edges, and the convex periarticular bulge), and the profile's joint-centered peak matches the full elbow contour seen on the original. The 572px peak width is a derived (joined) measurement spanning the L-shaped elbow region and is not a single-limb width read directly off the original.
POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: (a) Geometric inflation: with the elbow flexed ~90-100 deg, a transverse width through the joint crosses BOTH limbs at an angle (the L-shape), so the "peak width" at 0px is geometrically larger than a true periarticular bulge - the 2.37 ratio therefore OVERSTATES the degree of swelling and should not be compared to published swelling ratios from true-lateral, extended views. (b) The peak (572px) does not match the endpoints of either plotted curve (blue ~380px at 0, green ~350px at -100), indicating it was measured differently (full transverse extent across the joint) and is not directly comparable to the single-limb widths on the plot. (c) Truncation: the forearm trace ends at ~+750px and the humeral trace at ~-480px because the image borders cut the arm off - the true maximal humeral width (and the distal reference) may lie off-image, and the "mid-forearm" point is chosen, not anatomically defined. (d) No contralateral reference: absolute widths are uninterpretable without the opposite arm (and even with it, habitus/muscle mass makes the proximal-humerus width non-comparable to the forearm). (e) Obliquity/rotation mixes transverse and anteroposterior dimensions into the "width."
CONFIDENCE: moderate
NEXT STEP: For QUANTIFICATION, this local method has reached its limit - the ratio is directionally useful (fullness is real and joint-centered) but not a reliable magnitude. Materially better would be: (a) a contralateral elbow film under the same conditions for a true comparison; (b) ultrasound of the elbow, which directly quantifies effusion/hemarthrosis and a displaced fat pad and can detect an occult fracture; (c) if the DICOM is available, a full-range rendering to re-examine the anterior fat-pad zone. No further local image analysis of soft-tissue width would add much beyond confirming the joint-centered peak already shown here.
