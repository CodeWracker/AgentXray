Confounders: (1) the distal half of the 64px profiles may extend beyond the small ulnar epiphysis into lucent ulnocarpal space/background, which naturally produces a distal falloff; (2) modest proximal-flank density; (3) oblique beam and carpal superimposition. Net: density evidence at the ulnar cleft is less consistent with a normal physis than at the olecranon or radial control, so a nondisplaced distal-ulnar fracture/epiphyseal injury cannot be excluded; the profiles also do not prove fracture, since a lucent distal flank is expected if the lucent line lies at the distal margin of the small epiphysis facing the ulnocarpal space.
