RELEVANT FINDINGS:
1. The CLAHE transformation preserves the overall anatomical structure very faithfully
   (correlation 0.966). The major bony landmarks (distal humerus, radial head, proximal ulna)
   remain clearly identifiable and spatially consistent with the original.

2. Local contrast enhancement is most effective in regions of mixed density (joint space,
   trabecular bone patterns), where it increases local std by 0.99-1.45x. This modest
   enhancement may improve visibility of trabecular detail and subtle joint space narrowing,
   though no new discrete findings (fracture lines, calcifications) are apparent from the
   quantitative analysis alone.

3. The 7.21x amplification at one sampling point (200,300) raises concern that CLAHE is
   amplifying noise or creating artifactual texture in low-signal regions. This region
   corresponds to the soft-tissue envelope where the original had very low local variance
   (std=2.1 vs 15.2 after CLAHE).

RELATION TO ORIGINAL IMAGE:
The observations in the CLAHE image are largely supported by the original cropped image.
The high pixel correlation (0.966) confirms that no major structures have been created or
removed. The enhanced local contrast in the joint region and trabecular bone is a legitimate
revelation of pre-existing detail that was present but less visible in the original's global
contrast mapping. The original image's mean intensity (28.3 for the full X-ray, 79.8 for the
cropped) with high std (57.4 and 79.3 respectively) indicates that the original had a broad
dynamic range that compressed anatomical detail.

POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS:
1. The 7.21x local contrast amplification in the soft-tissue window (200,300) is likely
   artifactual — the original had std=2.1 in a 50x50 window, which is nearly uniform.
   CLAHE has imposed texture on a homogeneous region.

2. Moderate noise amplification (1.27x high-frequency energy) means that any grainy texture
   visible in the enhanced image may partly represent amplified noise rather than true
   anatomical trabecular detail.

3. The CLAHE block size (not specified but typically 8x8 to 32x32 pixels) may introduce
   artificial blocky boundaries if the local histogram normalization creates visible seams
   between adjacent blocks. This cannot be confirmed from pixel statistics alone but should
   be inspected visually.

CONFIDENCE:
Moderate. The quantitative analysis provides robust evidence that CLAHE modestly enhances
local contrast without creating spurious major structures. However, pixel statistics cannot
fully confirm or exclude the visibility of subtle pathological findings (e.g., nondisplaced
fracture lines, early joint space narrowing). The 7.21x amplification zone warrants visual
inspection to determine whether it corresponds to a region of clinical interest or represents
pure noise amplification.
