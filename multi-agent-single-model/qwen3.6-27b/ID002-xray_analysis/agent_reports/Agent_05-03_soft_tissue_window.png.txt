PURPOSE: Independent assessment of soft tissue structures and periarticular anatomy. This image was produced by mapping the intensity range [10, 130] of the cropped elbow image linearly to [0, 255], selectively enhancing low-density anatomical regions (soft tissue, joint spaces, fat pads) while clipping both very bright (bone cortex) and very dark (background) values to pure white and pure black respectively. The question it attempts to answer: can soft tissue structures — including fat pads, muscle silhouettes, joint capsule outlines, and periarticular swelling — be resolved at higher contrast than the global view?

OBSERVATIONS: The image measures 875x636 pixels, grayscale, 8-bit. The global mean intensity is 210.89, with a median of 255, indicating that the majority of the image area (62.2% of pixels, 347,140 pixels) is mapped to pure white. This is a direct consequence of the soft tissue window: pixel values in the original cropped image below 10 (background) and above 130 (dense bone) are both pushed to the extremes. The histogram is strongly bimodal: a massive peak at 255 (347,140 pixels), then a secondary cluster around bins 239-246 (22,716 pixels total), then a small dark cluster near 0 (37,843 pixels, 6.80%).

The pixel map across sampled rows reveals the anatomical layout in a lateral elbow projection. At the top rows (0-50), the image is mostly white (background/soft tissue saturation) with a dark vertical strip at columns 500+ (likely the posterior arm shadow or an edge effect). Moving downward, rows 100-250 are overwhelmingly white, indicating that much of the mid-image area falls outside the soft tissue window range — likely because the humeral shaft and ulnar shaft pixels are clipped to 255. 

Rows 300-400 show the most anatomical detail: alternating bands of white, gray, and dark pixels. Row 340 has a mean of 133.6 with minimum 0, indicating significant contrast variation. Rows 350-375 contain mixed intensity values (white/gray/black alternating), suggesting the elbow joint region where cortical bone edges, joint space, and periarticular soft tissue coexist within the window range.

Gradient analysis reveals that the strongest edges concentrate in rows 340-380 and columns 137-163 and 424-447. The highest-gradient rows (375, 344, 376, 345, 374) have mean gradients of 10-20, corresponding to bone-soft tissue interfaces. Only 5 high-gradient runs (>50 magnitude, length >15) were detected across the entire image, which is notably sparse. The maximum gradient magnitude is 360.62, occurring at a single transition.

Quadrant analysis shows the top-left and bottom-right quadrants are brighter (mean ~226) while the top-right and bottom-left are slightly darker (mean ~195), with the bottom-left having the highest standard deviation (82.31), suggesting more anatomical detail in that region.

RELEVANT FINDINGS: 
1. The soft tissue window fails to produce a uniformly useful image: 62.2% of pixels are clamped to 255 (pure white), meaning the window mapping loses most information by hard-clipping both below-10 and above-130 values to the extremes.
2. The concentrated gradient activity at rows 340-380, columns 137-163 and 424-447 likely corresponds to the elbow joint line — the humeroulnar and humeroradial articulations. These are the only regions where both soft tissue and adjacent bone cortex fall within the window range simultaneously.
3. The scarcity of high-gradient runs (only 5) suggests that the linear window mapping [10, 130] is too narrow to capture meaningful soft tissue structure across the full image extent. Most soft tissue regions are either below 10 (mapped to black) or above 130 (mapped to white), providing no gradation.
4. The bottom-left quadrant has the highest standard deviation (82.31), which may correspond to the anterior soft tissue compartment containing the coronoid fossa and anterior fat pad region — an area of clinical interest for occult fracture detection.
5. No clear fat pad sign is discernible from the pixel statistics alone. The soft tissue window's hard clipping obscures the subtle triangular lucencies that would indicate anterior or posterior fat pad elevation.

RELATION TO ORIGINAL IMAGE: The soft tissue window is a derived image from the cropped elbow (01_cropped_elbow.png), which itself is cropped from ID002-xray.png. The window parameters [10, 130] were selected based on histogram analysis of the cropped image. Since the original image has a mean of 28.3 and 78.8% of pixels below intensity 30, this window targets the lower-intensity band of the histogram. The observation that most of the image is white (clipped) is consistent with the fact that significant portions of the elbow anatomy (bone cortex, trabecular bone) have intensities well above 130 in the cropped image and are therefore mapped to 255. The dark pixels (<50, 6.80%) correspond to regions in the original image with intensities below 10, which are background or very air-filled spaces. The intermediate gray values (bins 239-246) represent the actual soft tissue range [10, 130] mapped to upper-mid gray [200-246], which is a counterintuitive result: the "soft tissue" values are mapped to nearly-white intensities, leaving no visual distinction from clipped bone.

POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS:
1. Hard-clipping artifact: The most significant issue. By mapping both below-10 and above-130 to extremes, the image conflates background air (0) with dense bone cortex (>130), both appearing as pure extremes. This destroys the ability to distinguish between "very dark soft tissue" and "dense bone."
2. Histogram bimodality: The massive peak at 255 (62.2% of pixels) is not anatomical — it is an artifact of the windowing process. The window was too narrow for the content range of the cropped image.
3. False uniformity: Regions that appear uniformly white could be either true background, very bright bone, or very dark air — the window mapping makes these indistinguishable.
4. Edge amplification at window boundaries: The sharp transitions at the window limits (intensity 10 and 130 in the source) create artificial high-gradient edges at the boundaries between clipped and non-clipped regions, which could mimic cortical margins or fracture lines.
5. Loss of spatial context: The soft tissue window, by design, discards information outside its range. Any finding that relies on the relationship between bone density and adjacent soft tissue (such as the fat pad sign, which requires seeing both the lucency and the adjacent cortical margin) is compromised.

CONFIDENCE: low

The soft tissue window, as implemented with a simple linear mapping of [10, 130] to [0, 255], provides limited utility for soft tissue assessment. The hard-clipping artifact eliminates over 62% of pixels as pure white, and the remaining informative pixels are concentrated in a narrow band (bins 239-246) representing only 3.8% of the image. While the gradient data identifies the approximate joint region (rows 340-380), the image lacks the tonal gradation necessary to distinguish between different soft tissue structures or to evaluate fat pad signs. The processing plan notes that CLAHE was intended for this purpose (Step 2), and the soft tissue window (Step 3) was designed to complement rather than replace it. The simple linear window is inherently limited for soft tissue assessment in this image, where the dynamic range of soft tissue values is much narrower than the window range.
