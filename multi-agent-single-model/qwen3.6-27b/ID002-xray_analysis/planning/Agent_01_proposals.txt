PROPOSAL 1 — Smart Crop to Anatomical Content
================================================================================

WHAT: Detect the bounding box of the anatomical content (the arm, elbow,
and forearm silhouettes) and crop the image to remove the ~50% empty black
space on the left side of the image frame. Upscale the resulting crop to
match the original pixel dimensions if possible.

WHY (diagnostic question): Is the spatial resolution adequate for confident
assessment of cortical margins, especially at the olecranon, radial head,
and distal radioulnar joint? The current image wastes roughly half its pixel
budget on black background. A tight crop would effectively double the
sampling density of the anatomical content.

EXPECTED RESULT: The anatomical content should occupy the full frame, making
fine cortical details, the interosseous space between radius and ulna, and
the joint space widths more resolvable. Regions previously spanning ~200
pixels should span ~400 pixels in the upscaled crop.

ARTIFACTS/CAVEATS:
  - Upscaling by interpolation will not recover true resolution but can
    make details easier to perceive. Avoid aggressive bicubic or Lanczos
    interpolation that introduces ringing artifacts along sharp edges.
  - Aggressive cropping may cut off portions of the proximal humerus or
    distal wrist that are at the edge of the current frame. Preserve a
    small margin (~2-3% of image width/height) to avoid losing context.

VALIDATION: The cropped image should show the same anatomical relationships
as the original, just larger. Verify that no cortical margin appears more
irregular in the crop than in the original — if so, the upscaling may have
introduced artifacts. Side-by-side comparison is essential.

IMPLEMENTATION NOTE:
  - Convert to grayscale (it is already functionally grayscale).
  - Threshold to find non-black pixels (e.g., pixels > 5 intensity).
  - Find the bounding box of all non-black pixels.
  - Crop to this box with a small margin.
  - Resize to original dimensions using nearest-neighbor or bilinear.

PROPOSAL 2 — Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE)
================================================================================

WHAT: Apply Contrast-Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) to the
cropped image. CLAHE computes histogram equalization over small tiles of the
image and then blends the results, avoiding the amplification of noise that
comes with global histogram equalization.

WHY (diagnostic question): Can we recover visibility of low-contrast
structures — specifically, the anterior fat pad in the coronoid fossa, the
posterior fat pad region, the interosseous membrane space, and subtle
trabecular patterns? The first-pass analysis found that 78.8% of pixels
fall below intensity 30 on a 0-255 scale, indicating that the dynamic
range is heavily compressed into the lower end, and the anatomical content
occupies a narrow band of the available intensity range.

EXPECTED RESULT:
  - Soft tissue structures (fat pads, muscle planes) should become more
    visible in the anterior and posterior elbow regions.
  - Trabecular bone patterns within the distal humerus and proximal ulna/
    radius should show more internal structure.
  - The interosseous space between the radius and ulna may show subtle
    density differences not visible in the original.

ARTIFACTS/CAVEATS:
  - CLAHE with too-small tile size or too-high clip limit will amplify
    background noise, creating a grainy or "salt-and-pepper" appearance
    that can be mistaken for cortical irregularity.
  - Recommended starting parameters: tile size 8x8 blocks, clip limit 2.0
    to 4.0. These are mild settings suitable for radiographic images.
  - CLAHE can create visible boundaries between tiles if the tile size
    is too large relative to anatomical features. Check for block artifacts.

VALIDATION: The enhanced image must not create apparent fractures, cortical
breaks, or joint space irregularities that are not present in the original
or the simple contrast-stretched version. Any new visible feature should be
verified against the original by zooming to the same region at the same
magnification.

IMPLEMENTATION NOTE:
  - Apply CLAHE to the grayscale channel only.
  - Generate both a moderate (clip=2) and aggressive (clip=4) version for
    comparison.
  - Also produce a simple global contrast stretch (linear mapping from the
    2nd to 98th percentile of non-black pixel values to 0-255) as a baseline.

PROPOSAL 3 — Dual Window/Level Rendering
================================================================================

WHAT: Create two distinct windowed renderings of the contrast-stretched
image:
  A. Bone window: narrow window width (~80-120 intensity units after
     contrast stretching), centered on the mean cortical bone density
     (~180-220 intensity units).
  B. Soft tissue window: wider window width (~150-200 intensity units),
     centered on soft tissue density (~40-80 intensity units).

WHY (diagnostic question):
  - Bone window: Are there subtle cortical step-offs, hairline fracture
    lines, or trabecular disruptions in the radial head, olecranon process,
    coronoid process, or distal humerus condyles?
  - Soft tissue window: Is there an elevated anterior fat pad (sail sign)?
    Is there any posterior fat pad visible (always abnormal)? Is there
    focal soft tissue swelling suggesting hematoma?

EXPECTED RESULT:
  - Bone window will maximize contrast along cortical margins, making the
    inner and outer cortices of the radius and ulna clearly distinguishable
    and revealing any thin radiolucent lines that might represent fractures.
  - Soft tissue window will make the dark fat pads adjacent to the elbow
    joint more visible against the surrounding soft tissue, and will reveal
    the thickness and contour of the soft tissue envelope.

ARTIFACTS/CAVEATS:
  - Narrow windowing will saturate (clip) regions outside the window range,
    losing information in very bright or very dark areas.
  - Soft tissue windowing may make cortical bone appear uniformly white
    with no internal detail, which is expected and acceptable for soft
    tissue assessment.
  - The 8-bit limitation means we cannot recover information that was
    discarded when the original DICOM was down-converted to PNG.

VALIDATION:
  - Bone window findings (cortical irregularities) must correspond to
    features visible in the original or the CLAHE-enhanced image at the
    same location.
  - Soft tissue window findings (fat pad elevation, swelling) must be
    corroborated by the original image, not just the processed version.

IMPLEMENTATION NOTE:
  - Use the contrast-stretched version (from Proposal 2's baseline linear
    stretch) as the source, not the raw image.
  - Apply window/level via the formula: output = 255 * (input - (level -
    width/2)) / width, clamped to [0, 255].
  - Save both renderings as separate outputs.

PROPOSAL 4 — Cortical Edge Detection and Fracture Line Screening
================================================================================

WHAT: Apply a Canny edge detector to the bone-windowed image (from
Proposal 3A), then use morphological operations to isolate edges that
follow the expected trajectory of the cortical margins of the ulna and
radius. Superimpose these extracted edges over the original image as a
semi-transparent overlay.

WHY (diagnostic question): Are there disruptions in the expected smooth,
continuous cortical margins of the ulna and radius? The first-pass review
found cortices "appear continuous" but acknowledged this assessment was
limited by image quality. Edge detection can reveal subtle angular
deviations, kinks, or gaps in the cortical line that are below the
threshold of reliable human visual perception.

EXPECTED RESULT:
  - The ulnar shaft should show two smooth, nearly parallel cortical
    margins from the olecranon to the distal ulna.
  - The radial shaft should show two smooth, converging cortical margins
    from the radial head to the distal radius.
  - Any break, kink, angular deviation, or secondary radiolucent line
    running parallel to and adjacent to the cortex would be highlighted
    as a potential fracture.

ARTIFACTS/CAVEATS:
  - Canny edge detection will also pick up non-bone edges: soft tissue
    boundaries, noise, and intensity transitions in the background.
    Morphological filtering (e.g., keeping only edges that are long and
    linear) is essential.
  - The olecranon fossa, coronoid fossa, and the trochlear notch have
    normal cortical discontinuities that will be flagged as "breaks" but
    are normal anatomy. These must be excluded from the analysis.
  - Image noise at the bone-soft-tissue boundary can create multiple
    parallel edge detections rather than a single clean cortical line.

VALIDATION:
  - Every edge discontinuity flagged by the algorithm should be manually
    verified against the original image and the CLAHE-enhanced version.
  - If an apparent "fracture line" is visible only in the edge-detection
    output and not in the original or enhanced image, it should be
    classified as a false positive (likely noise or processing artifact).

IMPLEMENTATION NOTE:
  - Apply Gaussian smoothing (sigma=1.5) before edge detection to reduce
    noise.
  - Canny thresholds: lower=20, upper=80 (on the bone-windowed,
    contrast-stretched image). Adjust empirically.
  - Use morphological thinning or skeletonization to reduce double edges.
  - Use Hough transform or line-segment detection to identify long linear
    features and flag those that deviate from expected cortical trajectories.
  - Render result as red overlay on original grayscale image.

PROPOSAL 5 — Line Profile Analysis Along Cortical Margins
================================================================================

WHAT: Define a series of line profiles (pixel intensity traces) that run
parallel to the expected cortical margins of the ulna and radius at a
fixed offset from the detected edges. Plot the intensity profiles and
their derivatives to identify abrupt changes that could correspond to
fracture lines, cortical thickening, or periosteal reaction.

WHY (diagnostic question): Is there quantitative evidence of cortical
irregularity that might be too subtle for visual inspection? A line
profile provides a one-dimensional signal that can be analyzed for
statistical anomalies (abrupt slope changes, intensity spikes) that
correspond to structural abnormalities.

EXPECTED RESULT:
  - Normal cortical margins show a sharp, consistent intensity transition
    (the "edge" from bright bone to darker marrow/soft tissue) followed by
    a smooth, gradual return to background intensity.
  - A fracture line would appear as an unexpected secondary intensity
    dip (radiolucent line) running alongside the main cortical edge.
  - Periosteal reaction would appear as an unexpected secondary bright
    line (new bone formation) on the outer cortex.
  - The first derivative of the profile (gradient) will highlight
    transitions: a normal cortex shows one major peak; a fracture shows
    an additional peak at a consistent offset.

ARTIFACTS/CAVEATS:
  - Line placement must be precise: even a 5-pixel offset from the true
    cortical margin will smooth out the signal and reduce sensitivity.
  - Intensity profiles are sensitive to rotation: if the arm is slightly
    rotated in the lateral view, the profile may cross overlapping
    structures, creating false signals.
  - The interosseous space between the radius and ulna creates a complex
    intensity landscape that can confuse automated analysis.

VALIDATION:
  - For each flagged anomaly in the line profile, return to the original
    image and verify by visual inspection at the corresponding coordinates.
  - Compare the profile from the medial ulnar cortex with the lateral
    ulnar cortex: they should show similar smoothness characteristics.
    Significant asymmetry warrants closer inspection.

IMPLEMENTATION NOTE:
  - Use the edges detected in Proposal 4 as a guide for line placement.
  - Place lines at 3-5 pixels inside and 3-5 pixels outside each cortical
    edge.
  - Sample at 1-pixel intervals along the length of each bone segment.
  - Compute both the raw profile and its first and second derivatives.
  - Plot profiles and highlight regions where the second derivative
    exceeds 2 standard deviations from the mean (potential anomalies).

PROPOSAL 6 — Radial Head / Neck Dedicated Enlargement and Sharpening
================================================================================

WHAT: Crop a tight region of interest (ROI) around the radial head and
neck (approximately 120x120 pixels in the original image), apply
unsharp masking for sharpening, and display at 4x the original pixel
size. Repeat the CLAHE enhancement within this ROI.

WHY (diagnostic question): Is there a nondisplaced or minimally displaced
radial head fracture? Radial head fractures (Mason type I) are notoriously
difficult to detect on plain radiographs, especially lateral views where
the radial head is seen en face or in slight obliquity. The first-pass
review noted the radial head "appears smooth" but acknowledged this was a
low-confidence assessment.

EXPECTED RESULT:
  - A normal radial head shows a smooth, convex articular surface with
    uniform cortical thickness (~2-3 mm).
  - A nondisplaced fracture would appear as a thin radiolucent line
    (<1 mm) crossing the radial head or neck, possibly with subtle
    step-off of the articular surface.
  - An impaction injury would show focal cortical thickening or
    increased density in one region of the radial head.

ARTIFACTS/CAVEATS:
  - Sharpening (unsharp masking) can create halo artifacts around edges,
    making normal cortical margins appear to have a radiolucent line
    adjacent to them. This is the most common source of false-positive
    fracture detection in sharpened images.
  - Unsharp masking parameters: radius=1.5-2 pixels, amount=0.5-0.8.
    Higher values dramatically increase false-positive risk.
  - Enlargement by pixel replication (nearest neighbor) is preferable to
    bicubic interpolation for this purpose, as it avoids creating new
    intermediate intensity values.

VALIDATION:
  - Any suspected fracture line must be verified in the unsharpened,
    non-enlarged version of the radial head ROI.
  - Compare the appearance of the radial head in the original image with
    the sharpened version: if a "fracture line" appears only after
    sharpening, it is likely a halo artifact.

IMPLEMENTATION NOTE:
  - ROI boundaries: approximately x=850-970, y=250-370 (estimate from
    visual inspection; should be refined by locating the elbow joint
    center and measuring outward).
  - Produce three versions: original resolution, 2x bilinear, and 4x
    nearest-neighbor.
  - Apply unsharp masking only to the highest-resolution version.

PROPOSAL 7 — Interosseous Space Width Measurement
================================================================================

WHAT: Measure the interosseous space (distance between the radius and ulna
cortices) at three standardized locations: proximal (5 cm below the elbow
joint), midshaft, and distal (5 cm above the wrist joint). Compare against
expected normal ranges.

WHY (diagnostic question): Is there evidence of interosseous space
narrowing (suggesting interosseous membrane injury or bowing deformity)
or widening (suggesting distal radioulnar joint disruption or proximal
radioulnar joint instability)? In lateral views, the interosseous space
should be relatively uniform along the shaft, typically 10-20 mm in
adults.

EXPECTED RESULT:
  - Normal: interosseous space narrows slightly from proximal to distal,
    typically ranging from 15-20 mm proximally to 10-15 mm distally.
  - Narrowing below 5 mm at any level suggests bone overlap from
    malrotation or malalignment.
  - Asymmetric narrowing (one side of the space narrower than the other)
    suggests forearm rotation rather than a true interosseous membrane
    injury.

ARTIFACTS/CAVEATS:
  - Without a scale bar or pixel-to-mm calibration, absolute measurements
    are approximate. Relative measurements (proximal vs. distal ratio)
    are more reliable.
  - The interosseous membrane itself is radiolucent and not visible on
    plain radiographs; we are measuring the space, not the membrane.
  - Forearm rotation during image acquisition can significantly alter the
    apparent interosseous space width.

VALIDATION:
  - Compare proximal and distal measurements: the ratio should be within
    1.2:1 to 1.5:1 (proximal wider than distal).
  - The measured widths should be consistent with the overall body habitus
    suggested by the soft tissue envelope.

IMPLEMENTATION NOTE:
  - Use the edge-detected cortical margins from Proposal 4 to automate
    distance measurement.
  - Define measurement points as percentages of the visible shaft length
    (20%, 50%, 80%) rather than fixed distances, since we lack calibration.
  - Measure perpendicular to the midline connecting the two bone axes.

PROPOSAL 8 — Olecranon-Fat Pad Region High-Contrast Enhancement
================================================================================

WHAT: Define a specific ROI covering the posterior aspect of the elbow
joint (approximately the olecranon fossa region and the adjacent soft
tissue behind the olecranon), apply aggressive local contrast enhancement,
and evaluate for the presence or absence of a posterior fat pad.

WHY (diagnostic question): Is there a visible posterior fat pad behind the
olecranon? A posterior fat pad is never normal and, when visible, is
highly specific for intra-articular effusion, which in trauma is virtually
always associated with an occult fracture (often supracondylar or
olecranon). The first-pass review found no "obvious posterior fat pad"
but acknowledged the assessment was limited by image contrast.

EXPECTED RESULT:
  - Normal: the posterior soft tissue behind the olecranon is homogeneous
    and continuous with the rest of the posterior arm soft tissue.
  - Abnormal: a triangular or lens-shaped lucent (dark) area adjacent to
    the posterior olecranon, distinct from the surrounding muscle tissue,
    representing displaced fat.

ARTIFACTS/CAVEATS:
  - Aggressive contrast enhancement in this small ROI may amplify noise
    to create false lucent areas that resemble fat pads.
  - The olecranon fossa itself is a radiolucent depression that can be
    confused with a fat pad. The key distinguishing feature is that a fat
    pad has a sharp, well-defined triangular or lens-shaped border, while
    the olecranon fossa has smooth, concave margins continuous with the
    posterior cortex.
  - This ROI is at the "corner" of the bent arm, where the soft tissue
    interface is complex and curved.

VALIDATION:
  - Any suspected posterior fat pad must be verified in the original image
    at the same magnification. The key diagnostic feature is a sharp
    triangular/lens-shaped radiolucency, not just any dark area.
  - Compare with the anterior fat pad region for symmetry of technique
    and interpretation.

IMPLEMENTATION NOTE:
  - ROI: posterior 40 pixels behind the olecranon tip, extending 30 pixels
    above and below.
  - Apply CLAHE with small tile size (4x4) and moderate clip limit (2.0).
  - Also produce a simple threshold segmentation to isolate the lucent
    areas and check their shape characteristics.

PROPOSAL 9 — Trabecular Pattern Analysis of the Distal Humerus
================================================================================

WHAT: In the metaphyseal region of the distal humerus (the spongy bone
between the articular surface and the shaft), compute the local variance
of pixel intensities in a sliding window and produce a variance map.
Regions of abnormally low variance may indicate osteoporosis, focal
demineralization, or marrow pathology. Regions of abnormally high variance
may indicate sclerosis, focal lesions, or artifact.

WHY (diagnostic question): Is the trabecular bone pattern in the distal
humerus consistent with normal bone quality? Osteoporosis, metastatic
disease, or infection can alter the trabecular pattern before cortical
fractures or gross deformities become apparent. The first-pass review
noted the trabecular pattern "appears normal" but this assessment was
visual and subjective.

EXPECTED RESULT:
  - Normal trabecular bone shows intermediate variance: a lacework of
    radiopaque trabeculae against a radiolucent marrow background, with
    a characteristic "mottled" appearance.
  - Osteoporosis: reduced overall bone density with thinning and loss of
    trabeculae, appearing as decreased variance (more uniform, darker
    appearance).
  - Focal osteolytic lesion: a circumscribed area of decreased variance
    (dark spot) within otherwise normal trabecular pattern.
  - Sclerosis: focal area of increased variance or increased mean intensity.

ARTIFACTS/CAVEATS:
  - This is a low-value analysis on an 8-bit downsampled PNG. True
    trabecular analysis requires high-resolution, high-bit-depth images.
  - The sliding window size must be large enough to capture the trabecular
    pattern but small enough to detect focal changes. Recommended:
    32x32 pixel windows with 50% overlap.
  - The distal humerus metaphysis in this image is partially superimposed
    by the proximal forearm bones, creating overlapping density patterns
    that will confuse variance analysis.

VALIDATION:
  - Variance map findings should be interpreted as suggestive only, not
    diagnostic. Any suspicious region should be manually inspected on the
  - enhanced contrast image.
  - This analysis is exploratory and low priority relative to fracture
    detection.

IMPLEMENTATION NOTE:
  - Restrict analysis to the lateral aspect of the distal humerus where
    there is minimal superimposition from the forearm bones.
  - Compute both mean and variance in each window.
  - Color-code: blue for low variance, red for high variance.
  - Overlay on the original image for anatomical context.

PROPOSAL 10 — Image Quality Diagnostic Report
================================================================================

WHAT: Generate a comprehensive quantitative report of the image's
photometric and geometric properties, including:
  - Full histogram with percentiles (1st, 5th, 25th, 50th, 75th, 95th,
    99th)
  - Spatial frequency analysis: compute the 2D Fourier transform and
    assess the frequency cutoff (to estimate effective resolution)
  - Signal-to-noise ratio estimate in bone and soft tissue ROIs
  - Dynamic range utilization: ratio of used intensity levels to total
    available (256)
  - Sharpness metric: Tenengrad or Laplacian variance computed on the
    bone regions

WHY (diagnostic question): What are the quantitative limitations of this
image for diagnostic purposes? This builds on the first-pass observation
that the image is an 8-bit PNG with suboptimal contrast utilization. A
systematic quality report will inform how much diagnostic confidence can
be placed in the other processing results and will help prioritize which
enhancements are likely to be most effective.

EXPECTED RESULT:
  - Histogram: confirm the highly skewed distribution toward dark values
    identified in the first pass.
  - Frequency analysis: determine the highest spatial frequency present
    in the image, which approximates the effective resolution limit.
  - SNR: estimate whether the signal in soft tissue regions is sufficiently
    above noise to support confident fat pad assessment.
  - Dynamic range: quantify how much of the 8-bit range is actually used
    for anatomical information.
  - Sharpness: provide a numerical metric to compare the original with
    sharpened versions.

ARTIFACTS/CAVEATS:
  - These metrics describe image quality, not patient pathology.
  - SNR estimates are approximate and assume additive noise, which may
    not be the dominant noise source in X-ray images (quantum mottle
    is signal-dependent).

VALIDATION: N/A — this is a diagnostic report about the image itself, not
about the patient. Results should be internally consistent (e.g., low
dynamic range utilization should correspond to a skewed histogram).

IMPLEMENTATION NOTE:
  - Compute histogram from non-zero pixels only.
  - For frequency analysis: compute FFT, compute radial power spectrum
    average, identify frequency at which power drops below 5% of peak.
  - For SNR: define bone ROI (high intensity) and soft tissue ROI
    (medium intensity), compute signal as mean and noise as standard
    deviation within each ROI.
  - For sharpness: compute Laplacian variance on the bone windowed
    image. Higher variance = sharper image.
  - Produce a summary report as a text file alongside the proposals.
