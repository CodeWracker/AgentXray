6. IMAGE PROCESSING THAT COULD CLARIFY UNCERTAINTIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  a) Histogram equalization / windowing:
     The current histogram is heavily biased toward near-black values.
     Applying window/level adjustments to optimize different tissue
     ranges would help:
     - "Bone window" (narrow, high): values 150-254 to assess bone
       detail in the bright structures
     - "Lung window" (wide, low): values 0-100 to assess lung fields
       and vascular markings
     - "Soft tissue window" (medium): values 30-120 to assess heart,
       mediastinum, and pleural structures

  b) Contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalization (CLAHE):
     Would enhance local contrast in both bright and dark regions,
     potentially revealing detail hidden in the current global
     contrast mapping.

  c) Edge enhancement:
     Sobel or Canny edge detection on the ROI could clarify the
     boundaries of the large bright mass, helping determine if it
     has a smooth anatomical contour or irregular pathological
     margins.

  d) Connected component analysis at lower thresholds:
     The current bright component analysis used thresholds of 150 and
     200. Repeating at lower thresholds (50, 80, 100) would reveal
     the structure of intermediate-density regions and help
     distinguish soft tissue from lung fields.

  e) Rotation correction:
     If the 142.6-degree principal axis indicates the image is
     rotated, correcting the orientation would make anatomical
     structures easier to identify and compare against normal
     templates.

  f) Image cropping:
     Cropping to the actual content area (removing the blank left
     third and top/bottom margins) would maximize the effective
     resolution of the diagnostic content. The content ROI
     (875 x 1180) could be upscaled for closer inspection.

  g) Noise filtering:
     The 154 single-pixel bright components could be removed with
     a small median filter or morphological opening, reducing noise
     without significantly affecting anatomical detail.
