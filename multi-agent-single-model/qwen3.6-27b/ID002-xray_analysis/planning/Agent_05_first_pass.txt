6. IMAGE PROCESSING THAT COULD CLARIFY UNCERTAINTIES
-----------------------------------------------------

  A. Histogram Equalization / Windowing:
     - The current 8-bit encoding wastes ~77% of its dynamic range on
       black background. Cropping to the anatomical content and re-
       mapping the histogram to use the full 0–255 range would dramatically
       improve soft-tissue visibility and potentially reveal subtle
       findings.
     - CLAHE (Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization) applied
       locally would enhance local contrast at bone-soft-tissue interfaces.

  B. Edge Enhancement:
     - Unsharp masking or high-frequency boosting could sharpen cortical
       margins, making subtle fracture lines more visible.
     - Sobel or Canny edge detection could be overlaid to highlight
       discontinuities in bone cortex.

  C. Bone Extraction / Segmentation:
     - Thresholding to isolate high-density bone regions would allow
       focused analysis of cortical integrity without soft-tissue
       interference.
     - Morphological operations (skeletonization) could trace cortical
       outlines for continuity analysis.

  D. Crop and Re-center:
     - Cropping to the bounding box of anatomical content would eliminate
       the wasted left-half of the image, effectively doubling the pixel
       density on the anatomy of interest.

  E. Grayscale Inversion:
     - Medical X-rays are conventionally viewed with bone as white on
       black (which this image already follows). No inversion needed.
       However, confirming the intended display convention is important.

  F. Noise Analysis:
     - Wavelet-based denoising could further separate signal from noise
       in marginal regions, though the current noise level appears
       manageable.
