PURPOSE: Evaluate whether CLAHE (clip=3.0, tile-grid 8x2) applied to the mid-forearm fracture zone improves visualization of fracture line characteristics, displacement, and comminution compared to the unprocessed original.

OBSERVATIONS: The CLAHE-processed image displays the complete forearm and hand of a pediatric patient. A blue ROI boundary labels the region subjected to CLAHE processing (clip=3.0, tile-grid 8x2), encompassing the mid-diaphyseal region of both the radius and ulna. Within this ROI, local contrast is visibly enhanced relative to surrounding bone. Both the radius and ulna demonstrate mid-shaft fractures at approximately the same longitudinal level. The ulnar fracture is a transverse to slightly oblique fracture with a visible radiolucent fracture line traversing the cortex; the fracture appears complete with mild cortical step-off and slight angulation. The radial fracture is less sharply defined as a discrete radiolucent line but shows cortical disruption and bowing, consistent with either an incomplete (greenstick) fracture or a torus/buckle variant with a subtle complete component. The interosseous space appears maintained. In the side-by-side comparison, the CLAHE-processed view (right panel) renders the cortical margins and fracture line edges more sharply delineated than the unprocessed view (left panel), particularly along the ulnar fracture line. The surrounding soft tissues appear normal with no obvious foreign bodies or radiopaque markers.

RELEVANT FINDINGS: (1) Bilateral mid-diaphyseal forearm fractures (radius and ulna) at a similar level, consistent with a pediatric both-bone forearm fracture. (2) The ulnar fracture line is more clearly visualized under CLAHE, confirming a complete transverse to mildly oblique fracture with minimal displacement. (3) The radial fracture shows cortical disruption and bowing; whether the fracture is complete or incomplete (greenstick/torus) is less certain even with CLAHE enhancement. (4) Growth plates at the distal radius and ulna are open, confirming pediatric skeletal maturity. (5) No obvious comminution is visible in either bone; the fracture pattern is simple. (6) Alignment shows mild angulation; significant translational displacement is not apparent on this projection.

RELATION TO ORIGINAL IMAGE: The fracture locations, bone involvement (both radius and ulna), and general alignment are all confirmed by the original unprocessed image. The fracture lines are visible in the original, though with lower local contrast. The CLAHE processing does not introduce new anatomical findings but enhances contrast at the fracture site, making existing features more discernible. The side-by-side comparison confirms that the key fracture characteristics (ulnar fracture line, radial cortical disruption) are present in both the original and processed images.

POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: The CLAHE processing (clip=3.0, tile-grid 8x2) accentuates local contrast, which may overemphasize cortical irregularities near the fracture line and could theoretically make a subtle buckle fracture appear more like a complete fracture. The tile-grid boundary (8x2) could introduce slight contrast discontinuities at tile edges, though none are clearly visible in this image. The enhanced sharpness should be interpreted cautiously: a fracture line that appears crisper under CLAHE does not necessarily indicate a more complete fracture than what is present in the original image. Additionally, the non-ROI regions of the processed image retain original contrast, creating a visible contrast difference across the blue boundary that could mislead an observer about true bone density variation.

CONFIDENCE: moderate

NEXT STEP: A lateral projection (if not already available in the full series) would materially help quantify angulation and displacement in the sagittal plane, which cannot be reliably assessed from this single AP view. A second analysis with a different contrast enhancement method (e.g., global histogram equalization or a different CLAHE clip limit) could verify whether the fracture characteristics observed here are robust to processing parameters or are influenced by the specific CLAHE settings used.
