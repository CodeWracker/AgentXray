The absence of findings on unsharp mask is somewhat reassuring, though it has limitations. Unsharp masking primarily enhances high-frequency (edge) information. Subtle fractures that do not produce a cortical step-off but instead manifest as trabecular compression or impaction within cancellous bone may not be enhanced by this technique. Additionally, fractures lying in the plane of the X-ray beam (tangential fractures) may be less visible even with edge enhancement.

POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: The unsharp mask processing introduces edge halos — a bright/dark rim on either side of sharp transitions. This can make perfectly smooth cortical margins appear slightly more textured or "ragged" than they truly are. Conversely, subtle linear lucencies within trabecular bone may be obscured by the high-pass nature of the filter. The processing also slightly reduces overall contrast in low-frequency regions (large areas of uniform density), which could make subtle density changes less visible. These are inherent properties of unsharp masking and do not represent errors but do limit the technique's sensitivity for certain fracture types.
