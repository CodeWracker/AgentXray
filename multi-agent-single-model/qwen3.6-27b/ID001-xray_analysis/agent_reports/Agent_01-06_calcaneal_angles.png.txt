RELEVANT FINDINGS:
1. Böhler's angle is elevated at 45.3° (normal 20-40°). An elevated Böhler's angle is atypical — classically, a decreased or absent Böhler's angle indicates calcaneal fracture with depression of the posterior facet. An elevated angle may be anatomical variant, may reflect positioning (excessive plantar flexion of the foot during imaging), or may indicate the landmark points were placed differently than standard convention.
2. Gissane's angle is decreased at 104.8° (normal 120-145°). A decreased Gissane's angle can indicate calcaneal body fracture, but can also be seen with normal anatomical variation, excessive foot flexion during imaging, or inaccurate landmark placement. The sharp angle at the posterior calcaneal sulcus is visually apparent.

- P2 (red, posterior facet apex) appears placed at a point along the posterior calcaneal superior margin, somewhat higher than the classical definition. If P2 is placed too high (closer to the tuberosity), this would artificially inflate the Böhler angle and decrease the Gissane angle.
