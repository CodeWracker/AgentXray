PURPOSE: Quantify soft tissue thickness along the foot contour to assess for swelling,
particularly over the cuboid/midfoot region where a possible bony abnormality was flagged.

OBSERVATIONS: The image presents two panels. The top panel overlays contour extraction
results on the original radiograph: a red line tracing the dorsal soft tissue boundary and a
green line tracing the plantar soft tissue boundary. Three vertical dashed lines demarcate
the hindfoot (yellow, ~200px), midfoot (cyan, ~245px), and forefoot (magenta, ~285px)
measurement zones.

The bottom panel plots soft tissue thickness (vertical distance between dorsal and plantar
contours) against horizontal pixel position. Three labeled measurements are annotated:
hindfoot = 453px, midfoot = 211px, forefoot = 189px.

Quality of the contour extraction: The algorithm performs poorly in the left portion of the
image (horizontal positions 0-140px). The thickness profile shows extreme values (starting
above 450px and trending to zero), which corresponds to the empty background region left of
the foot where the contour detection fails. The algorithm traces the image border rather than
actual soft tissue in this zone. Spurious jumps and spikes occur between pixels 110-170 where
the contours lock onto and off of the actual foot silhouette. The hindfoot measurement of
453px is clearly inflated -- it captures the transition zone between background and the
actual foot contour, not true soft tissue thickness.

The midfoot measurement (211px) and forefoot measurement (189px) appear more reliable, as
the contour extraction is smoother in these regions. The dorsal soft tissue over the midfoot
(cuboid region) does not show a localized bulge or focal thickening that would suggest
swelling associated with fracture, infection, or inflammatory process. The gradual decrease
from midfoot (211px) to forefoot (189px) is anatomically expected.

RELEVANT FINDINGS: No evidence of focal soft tissue swelling over the midfoot/cuboid region.
The dorsal soft tissue silhouette is smooth without mass effect, contour irregularity, or
focal thickening. The plantar soft tissue is similarly unremarkable. No subcutaneous gas is
visible. The heel fat pad thickness posterior to the calcaneus appears preserved.

RELATION TO ORIGINAL IMAGE: Consistent with the original image assessment, which noted
"moderate thickness, no gross swelling" of the dorsal foot soft tissue. The profile analysis
adds quantitative support for the absence of focal midfoot swelling. If the cuboid were
fractured or infected, one might expect a localized soft tissue reaction, particularly on
the dorsal aspect where soft tissue is thinner and swelling is more radiographically
evident. No such finding is present.

POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: The soft tissue contour extraction algorithm has significant limitations:
(1) Complete failure in the background region left of the foot, producing meaningless extreme
values. (2) The hindfoot measurement of 453px is unreliable -- it captures algorithmic
artifacts at the transition from background to the actual foot, not true soft tissue. (3)
The dorsal contour (red line) on the image itself traces a smooth, slightly convex curve over
the foot, but this is the expected normal contour and should not be overinterpreted. (4)
Without comparative reference values for soft tissue thickness on a lateral foot radiograph,
the absolute pixel measurements (211px, 189px) cannot be classified as normal or abnormal in
isolation. They are useful only for relative comparison across zones.

CONFIDENCE: Moderate. The absence of focal swelling over the midfoot is a negative finding
and is reasonably well-supported. However, the quantitative measurements are compromised by
algorithmic artifacts, particularly in the hindfoot region. The overall contour assessment is
reliable, but the specific pixel measurements should be interpreted cautiously.

NEXT STEP: Additional soft tissue analysis is not needed. The absence of swelling is
consistent with the absence of significant bony pathology in the cuboid region. The combined
evidence from the CLAHE-enhanced ROI (normal cuboid architecture), texture analysis (normal
trabecular statistics), and soft tissue profile (no swelling) converges on the conclusion
that the first-pass cuboid finding was likely a superimposition artifact of the single lateral
projection. No further image processing will materially alter this assessment.
