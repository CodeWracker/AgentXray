PROPOSAL 1 - Whole-image percentile contrast stretch (baseline normalization)
================================================================
- ANALYSIS: Percentile-based (e.g., 1.0-99.5%) linear contrast stretch /
  histogram normalization of the full grayscale image, producing a
  contrast-optimized reference version.
- QUESTION: What is the best global dynamic range for viewing bone and
  soft tissue in this image, removing the influence of the very dark
  background and the very bright torso tissue on the right?
- RATIONALE: The image spans from near-black background to saturated bright
  torso tissue; a percentile stretch reallocates display range to the
  examined limb and gives all later analyses a common, reproducible base.
- EXPECTED INFORMATION: Improved overall bone/soft-tissue separation across
  the limb; a consistent baseline for comparing all subsequent processed
  outputs.
- ARTIFACT RISKS: Clipping of true densities at either end; can exaggerate
  noise in dark background regions; no new structural information is added.
- COMPARISON: Compare side-by-side with the original; verify that no
  structure changes appearance except in brightness/contrast, and that the
  mid-forearm region remains visually consistent.
- PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: agent01_pct_stretch_full.png

PROPOSAL 2 - CLAHE local adaptive histogram equalization (whole image)
================================================================
- ANALYSIS: CLAHE (e.g., clip limit 2.0-3.0, tile ~16x16 or 32x32) applied
  to the full image, optionally on the percentile-stretched base.
- QUESTION: Does local contrast enhancement reveal a cortical defect,
  lucency, or angulation in the mid-forearm that is invisible at native
  display, without over-amplifying background noise?
- RATIONALE: The suspected finding is a low-contrast, subtle cortical
  irregularity; local (not global) equalization adapts to the local gray
  scale of the bone, increasing micro-contrast of cortical margins.
- EXPECTED INFORMATION: Sharper cortical margins along radius and ulna
  shafts; clearer confirmation or exclusion of the suspected transverse
  lucency at the mid-forearm; better carpal trabecular detail.
- ARTIFACT RISKS: Noise amplification in soft tissue and background;
  "edge halo" and banding artifacts; can create apparent lines that do not
  exist in the original (false-positive fracture lines).
- COMPARISON: Overlay/flip between CLAHE output and the original; any
  suspected new line must be traceable back to the original to be credited.
- PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: agent01_clahe_full.png

PROPOSAL 3 - Unsharp masking / high-frequency bone enhancement (whole limb)
================================================================
- ANALYSIS: Unsharp masking (Gaussian blur sigma ~2-4 px, amount ~0.5-1.0)
  or high-pass (band-pass) bone enhancement on the percentile-stretched
  image.
- QUESTION: Are there subtle cortical step-offs or lucencies (mid-forearm,
  distal radius, elbow) that high-frequency enhancement can make visible?
- RATIONALE: Cortical fractures are thin, high-frequency features against a
  slowly varying bone background; unsharp masking suppresses low-frequency
  tissue background and emphasizes edges.
- EXPECTED INFORMATION: Enhanced cortical edges along the full limb; any
  cortical interruption would stand out as a gap or break in the enhanced
  edge contour.
- ARTIFACT RISKS: Ringing (Gibbs-like halos) around sharp edges such as
  joint spaces and the image border can mimic fracture lines; noise
  amplification; over-enhancement can obscure the real line under halos.
- COMPARISON: Compare edge continuity with the original at identical zoom;
  verify that every "gap" in the enhanced edge map corresponds to a real
  gray-level change in the original.
- PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: agent01_unsharp_bone.png

PROPOSAL 8 - Edge-detection overlay (cortical continuity map)
================================================================
- ANALYSIS: Band-pass filter the percentile-stretched image (e.g.,
  difference-of-Gaussians), run Canny edge detection with two thresholds,
  then render the detected edges as a colored (e.g., red) overlay on top of
  the original image.
- QUESTION: Where exactly along the radius and ulna is the cortical edge
  continuous vs interrupted, and does the interruption align with the
  suspected mid-forearm line?
- RATIONALE: An edge map turns "cortex present/absent" into an explicit
  visual variable; a true cortical break should appear as a gap or
  misregistered edge pair.
- EXPECTED INFORMATION: Continuous edge traces along both forearm bones, or
  a localized gap at the mid-forearm (supporting the suspected fracture);
  cortical edges of metacarpals/phalanges for cross-check.
- ARTIFACT RISKS: Canny is threshold-sensitive; may break edges on real
  cortex (false gaps) or connect across joint spaces; halo edges from
  over-enhancement produce spurious "cortices"; overlay can be read
  literally when it is only a derivative.
- COMPARISON: Every gap or break in the overlay must be verified pixel-by-
  pixel in the original before being credited as a finding.
- PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: agent01_edge_overlay.png

PROPOSAL 9 - Soft-tissue window (gamma + soft-tissue range stretch)
================================================================
- ANALYSIS: Re-map the image to a soft-tissue window: gamma ~0.4-0.6 (or
  inverse image) with histogram stretch limited to the soft-tissue gray
  range, applied to the forearm and hand regions; bones will wash out.
- QUESTION: Is there soft-tissue swelling, asymmetry, or hematoma of the
  forearm (especially over the mid-forearm suspicion) or of the hand?
- RATIONALE: Swelling is a common indirect sign of fracture; at native view
  the soft tissue is under-displayed because the display range is dominated
  by bone density. A soft-tissue window redistributes range to tissue.
- EXPECTED INFORMATION: Forearm/hand soft-tissue contour and relative
  thickness along the limb; bulging over the mid-forearm, dorsal hand or
  elbow would support an injury.
- ARTIFACT RISKS: No internal bone information at all in this window (do
  not read cortex from it); background tissue noise; the adjacent out-of-
  focus torso on the right is uninterpretable and should be ignored.
- COMPARISON: Compare contour against the original (soft tissue visible
  there too); compare radial vs ulnar and proximal vs distal forearm
  thickness within the same limb.
- PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: agent01_soft_tissue_window.png

PROPOSAL 10 - Forearm axis tracing and angulation measurement (quantitative)
================================================================
- ANALYSIS: Manually/digitally trace the centerlines of the radius and
  ulna from elbow to wrist on the percentile-stretched image; fit straight
  lines to the proximal third and distal third of each shaft; compute the
  deviation angle between segments (per bone) and the interosseous width
  profile along the forearm. Output: annotated image with traces + a
  numeric angle table.
- QUESTION: Is there measurable angular deformity (bowing/angulation) of
  the radius or ulna at the mid-forearm, and if so of how many degrees?
- RATIONALE: A subtle angulated (greenstick-type or minimally displaced)
  fracture may show more reliably as axis deviation than as a cortical
  lucency; quantitative measurement removes the subjectivity of "looks
  bent".
- EXPECTED INFORMATION: Numeric angulation (degrees) per bone, location of
  apex, interosseous width changes; if angle >2-3 deg at one level it
  supports a fracture at that level.
- ARTIFACT RISKS: Tracing is subjective (operator bias); normal anatomic
  bowing of the ulna must be accounted for; tangential projection adds
  apparent curvature; noisy edges make centerline placement unstable.
- COMPARISON: Report absolute values but always interpret relative to the
  original image's visible cortex; a "bend" that does not correspond to a
  cortical change in the original is not a fracture.
- PROPOSED OUTPUT FILENAME: agent01_axis_measurement_annotated.png
