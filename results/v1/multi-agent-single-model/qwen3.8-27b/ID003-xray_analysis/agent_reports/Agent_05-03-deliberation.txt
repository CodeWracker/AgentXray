B. DISAGREEMENT D1 — SKELETAL MATURITY
====================================================================
ASSESSMENT: The preponderance of independent first-pass evidence favors a
PEDIATRIC skeleton: Agent_02 (open physes at elbow and wrist, small
immature carpal centers), Agent_03 (slender bones, separate rounded
distal-phalangeal epiphyses, metaphyseal flaring), Agent_04 (narrow open
physes, small/incomplete carpal ossification centers), and I (open physes
at distal forearm and phalanges, multiple carpal centers, small bone
size). Agent_01 and Agent_06 simply did not commit: neither mentions
ossification centers or physes in its first pass, and both frame the
mid-forearm finding in adult-trauma language (06 explicitly in
"transverse diaphyseal fracture" terms). I read this as non-attention
rather than genuine counter-evidence — but a non-attending majority is not
a deciding majority, so the image should be made to decide it.

WHICH ANALYSES RESOLVE IT (best first):
1. Carpal center census (Agent_04 M3): count and locate the visible carpal
   ossification centers on the wrist crop. The maturation sequence
   (capitate/scaphoid earliest, then hamate/triquetrum, lunate,
   trapezium/trapezoid) is a strong, countable discriminator: 2-5 small
   round centers = pediatric; a full 8-bone carpus of adult size = adult.
   This is the single most decisive test because the carpus is well inside
   the FOV and the count is nearly unambiguous.
2. Wrist bone-window crop (T6, ID003-A4) + plain wrist crop (C1): direct
   view of the distal radial/ulnar physes. A clearly lucent physeal band
   between epiphysis and metaphysis = open physis (pediatric); only a
   faint irregular fusion line or none = closed (adult).
3. Elbow crop (T5): elbow ossification centers (capitellum, radial head,
   olecranon, medial epicondyle) — separate centers with physes = pediatric;
   fused = adult. Secondary because the elbow is partly out of field and
   oblique.
4. My M1 (physeal-width/shaft-width ratio): keep only as a QUANTITATIVE
   FLAG — report whether the physeal band is visible and, if so, its width
   as a fraction of shaft width. On 8-bit data sub-pixel widths are
   unreliable and a late-adolescent nearly-closed physis can leave a faint
   line, so this supports but does not decide; the census (item 1) does.

EVIDENCE PATTERN FOR PEDIATRIC: (i) lucent physeal bands at distal
radius/ulna and at phalangeal bases; (ii) 2-5 small, round, separate carpal
centers rather than a full carpus; (iii) separate phalangeal epiphyses;
(iv) slender shafts with relatively wide metaphyses.
EVIDENCE PATTERN FOR ADULT: (i) no lucent physeal band (thin fusion line or
nothing); (ii) complete 8-bone carpus of adult proportions; (iii) fused
epiphyses at phalangeal bases and elbow; (iv) physeal-width ratio ~0.

CONSEQUENCE NOTE: maturity modulates how every small structure is read. If
pediatric, the small dense focus at x~0.63, y~0.82 (Agent_04, indeterminate
"normal or fragment?") is far more likely a normal ossification center
(possibly of the second limb, see D2) than a fracture fragment, and the
mid-forearm finding's differential shifts toward buckle/greenstick/healing
fracture. If adult, the differential shifts to adult fracture entities. I
expect D1 to be RESOLVABLE from this image; I will not carry it as an
unresolvable disagreement past the wrist-crop + census outputs.
