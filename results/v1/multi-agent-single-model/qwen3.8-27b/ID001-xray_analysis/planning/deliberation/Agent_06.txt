Deliberation on the council's full set of proposals (32 total: 5 each from Agents 01-03, 05-06; 7 from Agent 04). I re-inspected the original image before voting. Overall impression of the council: remarkably convergent — the six agents independently converged on the same five analysis families (local contrast enhancement, edge/fracture-line enhancement, soft-tissue contour, geometric angles, background/flat-field correction), plus three rare unique proposals (detection-limit estimation, deconvolution, edge-preserving denoising). Convergence is strong evidence these are the right analyses for a degraded film scan, but it also means the council shares blind spots: no proposal tests whether the image's blur is the limiting factor before or after enhancing, and no proposal handles the missing physical scale for mm-level claims.

JUSTIFIED PROPOSALS:

1. Local adaptive contrast enhancement (CLAHE) of targeted ROIs — A1P1, A2P1, A3P2, A3P5, A4P1, A5P1, A6P1/P2.
   Justified: the dominant documented limitation of this image is moderate, non-uniform contrast on a grainy film scan; joint spaces, subchondral plates, and the spur region sit near the resolution/contrast limit. Local (not global) equalization is the correct tool because the image has a strong intensity gradient that global stretching would waste. The variants target genuinely different questions (joint spaces vs. talar body/neck vs. heel spur vs. whole-image fade), so this family is justified per-ROI, not just as one repeated idea. A2P1's two-clip-limit bracketing and A6P2's native-scale cross-check are good self-guards.

2. Edge/fracture-line enhancement — A1P5, A2P2, A3P3, A4P3, A5P3 (and the edge component of A6P1).
   Justified: hairline/non-displaced fracture is the key thing every agent failed to exclude in first pass, and thin lucent lines are exactly what high-pass/gradient operators emphasize. Justified, but only with the strict guardrails the proposers themselves wrote (see MISLEADING-ARTIFACT RISK). A3P3 is the most disciplined variant (median pre-denoise + colored overlay tied back to the original); A5P3 correctly adds the osteophyte/enthesophyte question at the Achilles insertion.

3. Soft-tissue contour analysis — A1P4, A4P4, A6P3.
   Justified: dorsal fullness (A1, A4) and heel-pad/posterior swelling (A6) are first-pass uncertainties that bone-windowed views cannot answer; contour vs. smooth-envelope deviation plus a bone-saturated window (A4P4) is the right approach. A6P3's numeric heel-pad thickness at standardized reference points adds real information over the qualitative variants.

4. Geometric angle measurement + projection check — A1P3, A2P4, A3P4, A4P5, A5P5, A6P4.
   Justified: alignment, plantarflexion, and (critically) true-lateral status are geometric questions that only digitized landmarks answer; angles are scale-invariant so the missing physical scale is not a problem here. A6P4's explicit talar-head/body superimposition true-lateral check is the most important element in the family: if the view is rotated, every other angle and joint-space reading on this single image is biased, and this check is what licenses or limits all of them. A3P4's Böhler angle is the only proposal that can flag an old/healed calcaneal fracture without a visible fracture line — unique and valuable.

5. Flat-field/de-vignetting correction + right-edge crop analysis — A2P5, A3P1, A5P4, A6P5.
   Justified: the vertical density gradient and faint streak lines are documented in first passes (A2, A5, A6) and will be amplified by every enhancement in family 1-2, so characterizing/correcting them first (or at least in parallel) is methodologically necessary. The right-edge crop is a real question (A2's scope note, A4Q6, A6Q5): what is cut off, and is there visible distal-metatarsal pathology. A6P5's explicit field-edge-straightness test against fracture-line mimics is the right guard.

6. Detection-limit / noise-floor estimation — A4P6. Unique to Agent 04.
   Justified and high value per cost: it converts the universal "cannot exclude a subtle fracture" into a calibrated statement ("cannot exclude a defect below N pixels / M% contrast"), which directly weights every negative result from families 1-2. Low artifact risk since it is measured on anatomy-free background of the original.

7. Edge-preserving denoising of the talar region — A5P2. Unique to Agent 05.
   Justified because it is the only proposal that directly attacks A5's first-pass talar "mottled lucency" from the noise side: persistence after edge-preserving denoising = structured (real), vanishing = grain. It pairs correctly with A5P1 (enhancement side) as a two-sided test of the same finding.

MISLEADING-ARTIFACT RISK: (by technique, with guards)
- CLAHE (family 1): noise amplification into speckle that mimics subchondral cysts or "calcifications"; tile-boundary blockiness (A3P2, A5P1, A2P1 all flag this); halos/ringing around cortices; over-equalization making normal trabecular asymmetry look focal (A5P1). Guard: all agents correctly require side-by-side with the original at identical crop/zoom, and that any new structure has a faint counterpart in the original; A2P1's concordance across two clip limits is an extra guard. Adopt these as mandatory, not optional.
- Edge/high-pass (family 2): the highest false-positive risk in the council. Trabecular striations, film grain, dust, and overlapping intact cortices (fibula over talus, tarsals) all produce false linear edges; halos on both sides of a true edge can be misread as a double cortex or step-off (A4P3); deconvolution-style phantom lines are the worst case (see below). Guards: A3P3's median pre-denoise + red overlay; A2P2's orientation test (real fracture cuts across trabecular flow, does not follow the bone edge); A1P5/A2P2's "must be traceable in the original at full length" rule; A6P1's endpoint rule (line endpoints must meet cortex or another structure). A finding that appears only after edge enhancement is an artifact, full stop.
- Denoising (A5P2): over-denoising blurs real thin cortices and can MASK a genuine subtle lesion or create waxy false blotches; strength choice biases the answer. Guard: A5's two-strength comparison; the denoised image must read as the same anatomy, cleaner.
- Bone subtraction (A2P3): unique in family 3 and the riskiest member — subtraction leaves residual halos around bone margins that mimic calcifications, and over-subtraction distorts the very silhouette it is meant to reveal, on an image that already has uneven exposure (flat-field error compounds subtraction error). Guard: treat as optional/last-resort; contour methods (A1P4/A4P4/A6P3) should answer the soft-tissue questions first.
- Flat-field correction (family 5): division by an imperfect background estimate creates new low-frequency patterns; over-correction can make curved brightness artifacts mimic periosteal reaction (A6P5) and can ERASE genuine broad low-contrast soft-tissue findings (A5P4); division amplifies noise in the darkest regions. Guard: the corrected image must show the same anatomy on a flatter background; if correction changes any bone's apparent density or shape, do not trust the corrected version there (A2P5's rule).
- Deconvolution (A4P7): highest artifact risk of any proposal — regularized deconvolution is notorious for phantom linear streaks that resemble fracture lines, with strong dependence on regularization strength. A4 itself labels it exploratory/highest-risk. Guard: only as a final exploratory step; accept a "line" only if it persists across multiple regularization strengths AND its location agrees with the original's edge structure; otherwise report it as indeterminate, not as a fracture.
- Magnification (A6P2, A3P5, A4P2): upscaling adds no real resolution; Lanczos ringing around sharp cortices can mimic osteophytes or step-offs; soft resamplers erase exactly the ~1 mm detail being sought. Guard: sharp resampler only; re-verify every crop finding at native scale in the original (A6P2 does this).
- Angle measurement (family 4): landmark error (a few pixels shifts Böhler by several degrees, A3P4/A6P4); off-true-lateral rotation systematically biases ALL angles (A2P4, A5P5, A6P4); a spur can be mistaked for the tuberosity extreme (A3P4). Guard: A6P4's rule — do not call an angle abnormal unless outside the normal range by more than the estimated landmark error; A2P4/A6P4's projection check must be read together with every angle.
- Segmentation (A6P3): the bright beam-field rectangle and background non-uniformity can break threshold-based skin-line extraction; grain jitters the contour. Guard: A6's overlay-verification and manual slip correction; report measurements at standardized reproducible reference points.
- Scale caveat (applies to A6P2 and A6P3): this file is a film scan with no embedded physical scale. Joint-space width and heel-pad thickness can be reported in pixels reliably, but mm conversion is NOT supportable from this file alone; mm claims need a calibration object or the original source. Angles are scale-invariant and unaffected.
