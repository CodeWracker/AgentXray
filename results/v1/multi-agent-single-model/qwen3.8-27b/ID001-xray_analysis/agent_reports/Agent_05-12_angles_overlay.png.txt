RELEVANT FINDINGS: (a) Landmark check: all six drawn lines sit on their named landmarks; the Böhler apex is at the posterosuperior tuberosity and its anterior arm at the anterosuperior calcaneal point, the pitch reference is horizontal at the plantar point, the tibial line is mid-shaft, the talar line crosses the talar body, the metatarsal line is on the first metatarsal (truncated by crop, so tibiometatarsal 60.4 deg is an estimate). No line is grossly misplaced. (b) Applying the ~5-8 deg error band (Agent_06 rule): Böhler 56 deg is 16 deg above the 20-40 normal range — beyond the band, and because Böhler is an intra-osseous angle it CANNOT be explained by plantarflexion or rotation of the foot as a whole; the off-lateral bias (see below) is of the order of the band, so a residual Böhler abnormality stands. Calcaneal pitch 22.7 deg is within 15-30 (normal). Tibiometatarsal 60.4 deg (vs <=25), tibiocalcaneal 6.5 deg (vs 25-40) and talocalcaneal 13 deg (vs 25-50) are all outside their ranges by more than the band, but all three are position-dependent angles between moving bones (tibia-forefoot, tibia-calcaneus, talus-calcaneus) and are quantitatively consistent with the observed position (see c), not with intrinsic bony abnormality. (c) Position decomposition: metatarsal axis -24.5 deg below horizontal with the tibia ~vertical => forefoot in ~25-30 deg of plantarflexion relative to the tibia; talocalcaneal 13 deg (vs ~40-50 deg intrinsic) and tibiocalcaneal 6.5 deg show the talus rocked back on the calcaneus and nearly parallel to the tibia; yet the calcaneal pitch (22.7 deg) is normal, i.e. the calcaneus itself kept a near-neutral orientation. Net: the plantarflexion is at the talus/forefoot level (equinus-type position), not a whole-foot drop. Projection: 18 px fibula-tibia separation vs ~0 for a true lateral => the view is moderately rotated off true lateral; every angle carries this projection bias of roughly the 5-8 deg band.
