The joint space between olecranon and trochlea is visible and not pathologically widened. No obvious periosteal reaction or callus is apparent at this level.
RELEVANT FINDINGS: (1) Small rounded density at the olecranon tip with a possible thin separating lucency. Differential: (a) tiny avulsion fragment (e.g., olecranon-tip avulsion from posterior stress/dislocation or from a traction injury); (b) ossification center/apophyseal variant - note the examination context is pediatric, although the skeleton elsewhere reads as near-mature, so an olecranon apophysis is possible but less typical in a fused skeleton; (c) superimposition - e.g., projection of the humeral condylar edge, an adjacent ossicle, or the ulnar styloid/overlapping structure.
