MISLEADING OR RISKY PROPOSALS:
- The edge-enhancement family (Agent_01 P3, Agent_03 P2, Agent_04 P2, Agent_05 P2) is the highest-risk set: edge detectors respond to trabecular pattern, superimposition boundaries, and noise, and a lucent fracture line produces a double-edge that can be misread as a cortical step-off. All proposals self-flag this and require original-image verification, but the method is still the likeliest source of spurious "fracture" findings and should be used only as a look-here guide, never as evidence.
- Agent_06 P3 as I wrote it (global histogram equalization for the vignette) is genuinely risky: it amplifies background noise in the very dark regions and can manufacture phantom structures in soft tissue and at the field edge. A per-region local window (or gradient/vignette division) on the wrist crop is safer. I flag my own proposal for revision on this point.
- Agent_02 P3 (inverted / soft-tissue-bright rendering): inverting a radiograph inverts radiographic logic and can mislead orientation and density interpretation; low value, avoid.
- Agent_05 P3 affine re-orientation: resampling interpolation can blur the fine cortical detail the test depends on; acceptable only if the non-resampled line overlay (Agent_04 P3 / Agent_06 P2) already gives a clear answer.
- CLAHE tile-boundary banding (self-acknowledged in Agent_03 P1, Agent_04 P1, Agent_05 P1) is a moderate risk: a band can masquerade as a thin lucent line at exactly the locations (medullary cavity, soft tissue) where a subtle fracture would be sought.
