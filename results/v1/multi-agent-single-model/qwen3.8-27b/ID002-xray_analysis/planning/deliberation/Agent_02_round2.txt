MY POSITION ON Q1 (wrist cleft: which bone; physis vs fracture): After re-inspecting 09/10, I
maintain: the cleft is on the DISTAL ULNA (the right/narrower bone). The left bone is the radius:
it is clearly wider, flares into a broad metaphysis, and carries the large rounded epiphysis that
directly articulates with the proximal carpal row (scaphoid visible at lower left); the right bone
is narrow with a small rounded ossification center - the classic pediatric distal ulna. This
confirms the 02/04 assignment and contradicts Agent_06's swapped labeling. As to physis vs
fracture: I read it as the NORMAL UNFUSED DISTAL ULNAR PHYSIS - the lucent line is regular,
transverse, of uniform width, with smooth parallel margins, in the expected location; there is no
cortical step-off, no DRUJ widening or proximal migration, and no concomitant radial injury
(distal ulna fractures in children are usually paired with radial injury). Agent_04's
"spiculated/irregular margin" is best explained by carpal superimposition on the oblique view plus
nearest-neighbor edge jaggedness. Confidence moderate-high for "normal physis"; honest caveats:
the distal field is truncated and the projection is oblique, so a dedicated non-truncated wrist
PA/lateral remains the true arbiter - within this image, however, I would not call it a fracture.

MY POSITION ON Q2 (olecranon cleft: apophyseal physis vs avulsion): I favor the NORMAL
APOPHYSEAL/ossification-center reading. On re-inspection of 03/04: the tip density is smoothly

available, a contralateral comparison film; on imaging alone I weight this toward normal variant.
