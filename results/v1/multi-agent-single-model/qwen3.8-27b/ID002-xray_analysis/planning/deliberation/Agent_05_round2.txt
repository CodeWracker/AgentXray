MY POSITION ON Q1 (wrist cleft: which bone; physis vs fracture): Independent read after
re-inspection with grid + centroid tracking on 00_original:
- Bone assignment: the cleft is on the bone continuous with the RADIAL HEAD - i.e., the
  RIGHT bone of 09/10 = DISTAL RADIUS. Chain of evidence: (a) the olecranon (pathognomonic
  hook, carrying the separate apophyseal tip-center that is the Q2 finding) sits on the
  LEFT at the elbow (x~830-950); the radial head (round epiphysis under the capitellum)
  sits on the RIGHT (x~950-1030); (b) centroid tracking of both shafts from y=450 to
  y=1080 shows smooth, non-crossing continuities (gap 16-32 px, narrowing to ~7 px only
  in the distal convergence zone y>1100 where the distal ends abut); (c) the left distal
  end (large epiphysis, x~755-840) continues the left shaft; the right distal end (small
  epiphysis + oblique cleft + spiculated fragment, x~820-920) continues the right shaft.
  This sides with Agent_06's round-1 left/right assignment (ulna left, radius right) -
  the 2:1 minority - and contradicts the 02/04 (and round-2 03) "left = radius" reading
  based on epiphysis size and carpal articulation.
- TENSION I must disclose: the distal morphology (left epiphysis ~2x the width of the
  right; a large carpal articulating below the left bone) reads like a textbook radius on
  the left, and in a single oblique, inferiorly-truncated projection with the distal
  convergence superimposition I cannot fully explain that inversion. The continuity chain
  uses pathognomonic anchors plus exact arithmetic and no projection assumptions; the
  morphological chain assumes textbook appearance holds under obliquity. I weight the
  continuity chain as the more robust one, so I hold my assignment, but at LOWER
  confidence than the trace mechanics alone would suggest.
- Physis vs fracture, under my assignment: the cleft in the right bone (distal radius)
  runs OBLIQUE, has irregular/spiculated margins and a small separate fragment at the
  margin, over a small epiphysis - in stark contrast to the regular transverse lucent
  band with dense parallel sclerotic margins of the left bone in the same image. My
  exploratory profiles across the right-bone distal end show a broad irregular hypodense
  zone (values ~30-80 where adjacent bone runs ~100-170) rather than a narrow clean band.
  That pattern favors a NONDISPLACED DISTAL RADIAL (PHYSEAL/METAPHYSEAL) FRACTURE WITH A
  SMALL FRAGMENT - the commonest pediatric forearm fracture - over a normal physis.
  Under the council's alternative assignment (right = ulna), the same cleft becomes a
  distal ulnar physis over a small ossification center + styloid, where a normal variant
  is favored. The bone identity therefore flips the diagnosis; P12/P17 is the decision
  point, and P15's band-width/margin-symmetry measurements vs the contralateral-bone
  physis control are the quantitative tiebreaker.
- Confidence: moderate (high in the trace mechanics; moderate overall because of the
  unresolved tension with distal morphology on this single oblique view).
