MY POSITION ON Q1 (wrist cleft: which bone; physis vs fracture):
Bone: the cleft is in the DISTAL ULNA, the thinner RIGHT bone of 09/10. I side with 02/04 against 06. The decisive, projection-independent test is carpal articulation: only the radius articulates with the carpus, and in 09/10 the proximal carpal row (scaphoid/lunate centers) sits directly beneath the wide LEFT bone, whose large convex epiphysis is congruent with it. That also fixes the "fragment": it is the small ulnar epiphysis (with possible carpal superimposition at its ulnar margin on this oblique view), not a free fragment.
Physis vs fracture: I read this as the NORMAL pediatric distal ulnar physis over a small epiphyseal ossification center, not a fracture. Grounds: the lucent band runs transversely, perpendicular to the ulnar long axis and roughly parallel to the adjacent radial physis — normal physis geometry, whereas fracture lines tend to be oblique/spiral; both margins are fairly dense and parallel (sclerotic metaphyseal and epiphyseal borders); the "fragment" is rounded and corticated, in the exact expected anatomic position; the skeleton is unmistakably immature (open distal radial and ulnar physes, only a handful of carpal centers), so an unfused distal ulnar physis is the expected finding; no ulnar cortical step-off, no periosteal reaction, no definite soft-tissue swelling at the wrist. Residual uncertainty, stated honestly: the ulnar margin of the distal ulna looks mildly irregular/spiculated, which I attribute mostly to carpal superimposition on an oblique view, but a small nondisplaced avulsion of the distal ulnar epiphysis/styloid CANNOT be excluded on this single truncated oblique image. Adjudication path: P12 (continuity + margin character) + P15 (width/depth/offset vs the radial-physis control); if still equivocal, dedicated non-truncated wrist PA + lateral and clinical correlation (ulnar-point tenderness), as 04 already recommended.
