MY POSITION ON Q1 (wrist cleft: which bone; physis vs fracture): After re-inspection of 09/10

round 2: the continuity trace from the elbow (olecranon-descending shaft) is what P12 is for,

fragment - that constellation favors a distal ulnar (metaphyseal/epiphyseal or styloid)

regular, and flanked by sclerotic margins. Carpal superimposition on a truncated oblique view
remains the main mimic, and if the visible carpal count implies a young child, an ossified
distal ulnar head/styloid of this size would be atypical, which makes the fragment more
concerning. Not resolvable in-image definitively; P12+P15(c,e) are the in-image adjudicators,

MY POSITION ON Q2 (olecranon cleft: apophyseal physis vs avulsion): FAVORS NORMAL OLECRANON
APOPHYSEAL PHYSIS (fusing apophysis), moderate confidence. On 03/04 the tip density is small,
smoothly rounded, corticated, in anatomic position without displacement, and the separating
lucent line is thin, relatively straight, and INCOMPLETE (does not traverse the full width) -
the classic fusing-apophysis pattern, not a wide irregular offset fracture cleft. Olecranon
cortex is continuous; the only blemish is a small contour notch at the density-body junction
(Agent_05) - noted but not a clear step-off. A nondisplaced avulsion cannot be fully excluded
on one oblique view (avulsion usually accompanies posterior dislocation; faint posterior
soft-tissue fullness is noted but non-specific), and skeletal age on this image is genuinely
ambiguous - the ossified olecranon complex reads older (apophysis present, ~9-10y+) while the
VISIBLE carpal centers read younger (~3-6y) and the carpus is truncated, so age cannot be used
to adjudicate. P15(a) vs (b) is the decisive in-image test: a real, smooth, narrow dip with
dense parallel margins on the ORIGINAL confirms a real physis-type band and refutes the
artefact hypothesis; an irregular/offset profile would reopen avulsion.
