Proposal 1:
  QUESTION: Is there a positive fat pad sign (displaced anterior "sail" fat pad and/or posterior fat pad in the olecranon fossa) or other sign of elbow joint effusion/hemarthrosis?
  METHOD: Crop the elbow region (distal humerus through proximal forearm), apply CLAHE (local adaptive histogram equalization, tile ~8x8, clip limit ~2.0) and a soft-tissue window, then review the anteroinferior humeral and posterosuperior (olecranon fossa) soft-tissue zones at 2x digital zoom.
  WHY IT MAY HELP: The fat pad is a low-contrast lucent structure hidden by the bright humeral shadow and overall windowing; local contrast enhancement is the standard way to make a displaced fat pad visible when it is faint on a routine window.
  EXPECTED INFORMATION: Whether a triangular/lentiform anterior fat pad is displaced away from the humerus (sail sign) and/or a posterior fat pad is visible; combined with the visible soft-tissue fullness this would support an intra-articular effusion and raise suspicion for an occult fracture.
  POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: CLAHE can amplify noise and create halo/edge-darkening artifacts around dense bone that mimic a lucent fat pad; over-clipping can make normal fat appear artificially conspicuous; interpolation at zoom may smooth a truly absent fat pad into a false positive.
  COMPARISON WITH ORIGINAL: Any fat pad or lucency seen must be traceable to the same anatomic position in the original unprocessed image; findings confirmed only on the enhanced image (especially halo artifacts adjacent to the humeral condyles) should be downgraded or flagged as equivocal.

Proposal 2:
  QUESTION: Is there a subtle, non-displaced fracture of the distal humerus (supracondylar/metaphyseal), radial head/neck, or olecranon?
  METHOD: Generate an edge-enhanced/bone-outline version of the elbow (high-pass filter or unsharp mask, with an optional Sobel/Canny edge overlay rendered in a contrasting color at low opacity) and review the cortical margins of the distal humerus, radial head and neck, and olecranon tip at 2-4x magnification.
  WHY IT MAY HELP: Thin fracture lines and small cortical step-offs are low-contrast against saturated bright bone in this exposure; edge enhancement preferentially highlights high-frequency cortical discontinuities, which is where subtle fractures live.
  EXPECTED INFORMATION: Focal cortical interruption, lucency traversing cortex, or a small step-off in the distal humeral cortex, radial head margin, or olecranon; alternatively confirmation of smooth continuous cortices.
  POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: Edge detectors respond to all high-frequency structure: normal trabecular pattern, cortical-periosteal interfaces, superimposition of the radial head on the ulna, and image noise all produce false edges; color overlay can create an illusion of lines that do not exist in the underlying image.
  COMPARISON WITH ORIGINAL: Every candidate fracture line must be verified on the original image at the same coordinates and zoom before being reported; edges present only on the enhanced version are treated as artifacts.
  PRIORITY: high

  COMPARISON WITH ORIGINAL: The traced contour and marked peak location must be visually consistent with what is seen on the original image; the profile quantifies but does not replace visual confirmation of the swelling.
