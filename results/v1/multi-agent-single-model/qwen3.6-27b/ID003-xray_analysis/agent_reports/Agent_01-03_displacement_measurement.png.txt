POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: The centerline estimation uses a single binary threshold (p80) applied to the combined radius and ulna within the ROI, which merges both bones into a single structure rather than resolving them separately. This means the centerline represents a composite axis, not the true anatomical axis of either individual bone. The discrete dot sampling (every 5 pixels along the ROI) may introduce minor stair-step artifacts in the centerline path. Near the fracture zone, where cortical margins are disrupted and bone fragments may be displaced, the binary thresholding may include or exclude fragments inconsistently, shifting the computed centerline from the true anatomical axis. The ROI boundaries are fixed by percentage-based cropping, which may not perfectly align with anatomical landmarks across different image resolutions or field-of-views.
