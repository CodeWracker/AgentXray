RELEVANT FINDINGS: The texture analysis is fundamentally invalid. The "Calcaneus Control ROI" (blue box) is NOT positioned on the calcaneus. It is positioned in the dark, empty background region to the left of the patient's foot. The cropped control ROI confirms this: it shows no bone tissue, no trabecular pattern—only empty space with minor digital artifacts. Consequently, the dramatic differences in all three texture metrics (entropy, local variance, energy) simply reflect the comparison of actual bony texture (cuboid ROI) against uniform empty background (control ROI). Any region of actual bone would show dramatically higher entropy, higher local variance, and lower energy compared to an empty ROI. This comparison provides zero diagnostic information about whether the cuboid is abnormal relative to other normal bone.
RELATION TO ORIGINAL IMAGE: The ROI placement is clearly visible in the top-left subplot. The blue "Calcaneus Control ROI" box is in the background area, approximately 120 pixels left of the distal tibia. The actual calcaneus is the large posterior bone at the bottom of the heel, which is NOT covered by either ROI. The red cuboid ROI does appear to cover part of the midfoot region, but the ROI is small (56x78) and includes overlapping structures beyond just the cuboid.
POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: The entire analysis is an artifact of incorrect ROI placement. The "calcaneus control" is empty space, making the comparison meaningless. Additionally, the cuboid ROI at 56x78 pixels is very small for texture analysis of trabecular bone—standard texture features (GLCM-based entropy, energy, local variance) require sufficient spatial sampling of the trabecular pattern to be meaningful, and this ROI likely captures only a fraction of the actual cuboid bone along with adjacent overlapping structures. The dramatic numerical differences (e.g., local variance of 797 vs 0.77) are not evidence of pathology—they are the expected result of comparing bone to empty space.
CONFIDENCE: High that this texture analysis is invalid and should not be used as evidence for or against the presence of cuboid pathology. The control ROI was placed in empty background, not on the calcaneus bone as labeled.
NEXT STEP: A valid texture comparison would require (a) placing the control ROI on actual calcaneal bone tissue (e.g., the calcaneal body, away from the posterior facet), (b) ensuring both ROIs are of comparable size and resolution, and (c) ideally including a second normal bone control (e.g., distal tibia metaphysis). However, even a correctly performed texture analysis on a single lateral view would be limited by superimposition artifacts in the cuboid region. The primary value of this image is as a negative control: it demonstrates that the methodology, as executed, produces meaningless results. The analysis should be disregarded in the council's assessment of the cuboid finding.
