8. SUGGESTED ADDITIONAL ANALYSES
--------------------------------
Specific image processing and analysis steps that could clarify
findings:

a) Böhler's angle measurement:
   - Question: Is there an occult calcaneal fracture with subtle
     depression?
   - Method: Measure Böhler's angle (normally 20-40°) on the lateral
     view. A reduced angle suggests calcaneal fracture even without
     obvious cortical disruption.

b) Gissane's angle measurement:
   - Question: Is the anterior calcaneal angle normal?
   - Method: Measure Gissane's angle (normally 120-145°). Acute
     angulation suggests calcaneal fracture.

c) Talar tilt assessment:
   - Question: Is there subtle talar subluxation or joint incongruity?
   - Method: Assess the congruency of the talar dome with the tibial
     plafond. Any step-off >1-2 mm is suspicious.

d) Contrast enhancement / histogram equalization:
   - Question: Are there subtle trabecular density changes or early
     stress reactions?
   - Method: Apply adaptive histogram equalization (CLAHE) to enhance
     local contrast in the trabecular bone of the talus and calcaneus,
     where early stress fractures or contusions may alter bone density
     subtly.

e) Edge enhancement / unsharp masking:
   - Question: Are there subtle cortical disruptions?
   - Method: Apply edge enhancement to highlight cortical margins of
     the talus, calcaneus, and distal tibia. This could reveal hairline
     fractures or cortical step-offs invisible at normal viewing.

f) Soft tissue thickness mapping:
   - Question: Is there occult soft tissue swelling?
   - Method: Map posterior heel soft tissue thickness and compare
     dorsal vs. plantar soft tissue contours. Asymmetric swelling may
     indicate injury even without fracture.

g) Multi-planar reconstruction if DICOM available:
   - Question: Are there osteochondral lesions or subtle fractures
     hidden by superimposition?
   - Method: If original CT or MRI data exist, obtain multi-planar
     reconstructions. Even from a single radiograph, oblique digital
     reformatting cannot help, but this underscores the need for
     orthogonal views.

h) Phase-based / phase-contrast analysis (if source allows):
   - Question: Are there microcalcifications or early soft tissue
     calcifications?
   - Method: If the source data permits, phase-based analysis could
     enhance visualization of early enthesophytes or soft tissue
     calcifications.

i) Deep learning-based fracture detection:
   - Question: Is there a subtle fracture that a human reader might
     miss?
   - Method: Apply a validated AI model (e.g., Fracture Detection
     system) trained on ankle/foot radiographs to flag regions of
     interest.

j) Bone texture analysis:
   - Question: Is there early osteonecrosis of the talus?
   - Method: Analyze trabecular bone pattern density in the talar dome
     and body for areas of increased or decreased density suggestive
     of AVN.
