The question it attempts to answer: does a simple global linear contrast stretch, which preserves the relative intensity relationships across the entire image, provide adequate contrast for assessing both bone and soft tissue structures, or is adaptive enhancement (CLAHE) necessary?

RELEVANT FINDINGS:
1. The linear stretch preserves the full dynamic range (min 0, max 255) and distributes intensities across a wider range than either windowed image. This means it theoretically preserves more information, as no pixels are hard-clipped. However, the information is spread so thinly that local contrast is insufficient for confident anatomical assessment.
2. The maximum gradient of 80.96 is only 22.4% of the gradient maximum seen in the windowed images (360.62). This means that cortical edges, which are the most important features for fracture detection, are substantially less conspicuous in the linear stretch than in the bone window. The ratio of high-gradient pixels (>50) is 2,823 for linear stretch vs 13,918 for bone window — a 5x difference in edge conspicuity.
3. The U-shaped histogram (peaks at 0 and 255) indicates that the p2-p98 mapping effectively maps the lowest 2% of pixels to 0 and the highest 2% to 255, with the remaining 96% spread across the intermediate range. This preserves the relative relationships but does not enhance local features.
4. The vertical cortical edge at columns 191-194, visible with gradient 8-9 in the linear stretch, corresponds to the same edge visible with gradient 32 in the bone window. The bone window amplifies this edge by approximately 3.6x, making it substantially more visible to the eye.

The bottom-right quadrant's near-black appearance (mean 5.53) confirms that the distal forearm/wrist region in this projection contains very low-density content that maps to near-zero in the linear stretch. This is likely the distal ulna/radius shafts in the background, and their invisibility is a limitation shared by all processing methods for this particular projection.

POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS:
1. The linear stretch's uniform contrast scaling does not create artificial edges (unlike the windowed images' hard clipping), making it the most faithful representation of the underlying data. However, the low gradient magnitudes mean that real anatomical edges are not sufficiently enhanced for confident visual inspection.
2. The p2-p98 mapping clips the bottom 2% and top 2% of the histogram to 0 and 255 respectively. While this removes extreme outliers, it also hard-clips 27,415 pixels (4.9% of total) at each extreme, which is a form of information loss. The 192,054 pixels at bin 0 include both true black background and the lowest 2% of anatomical content.
3. The low gradient maximum (80.96) means that noise and signal are both relatively low, making it difficult to distinguish between genuine anatomical edges and noise artifacts. The gradient mean of 2.31 is barely above the noise floor for typical 8-bit quantization.
4. The bottom-right quadrant's near-total darkness (mean 5.53) may give a false impression that this region contains no anatomy, when in fact it likely contains low-density structures (distal forearm soft tissues, distal ulna/radius) that are mapped to near-zero by the linear stretch.
5. The linear stretch preserves relative intensities globally, which means that a bright pixel and a slightly less bright pixel maintain the same contrast difference regardless of their anatomical context. This is both a strength (faithful representation) and a weakness (no local optimization).

CONFIDENCE: moderate

The linear stretch provides a faithful, undistorted representation of the cropped image's intensity distribution, with the full dynamic range utilized and no hard-clipping artifacts. The cortical edges are visible but not conspicuously so (maximum gradient 80.96 vs 360.62 in bone window). The anatomical layout is clearly identifiable: humeral shaft extending from the upper-right, elbow joint at the mid-image region (rows 70-150, columns 200-320), and ulnar shaft extending distally. However, the low local contrast makes it difficult to confidently assess cortical continuity, trabecular patterns, or soft tissue details. The comparison with CLAHE shows that while the linear stretch preserves global relationships, CLAHE is likely superior for local detail enhancement. The moderate confidence reflects the faithful data representation but limited visual utility for detailed anatomical assessment.
