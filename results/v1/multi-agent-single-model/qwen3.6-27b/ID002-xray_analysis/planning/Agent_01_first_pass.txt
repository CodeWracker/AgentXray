6. ADDITIONAL IMAGE PROCESSING RECOMMENDATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  A. HISTOGRAM EQUALIZATION / CONTRAST ENHANCEMENT:
     Applying histogram equalization or adaptive contrast enhancement would
     spread the pixel intensities across the full 0-255 range, making low-
     contrast structures (soft tissues, fat pads, subtle trabecular changes)
     more visible. This is the single most impactful processing step.

  B. WINDOW/L LEVEL ADJUSTMENT:
     A narrow "bone window" (e.g., window width 400-600, window level
     adjusted to center on bone density) would optimize visualization of
     cortical bone and trabecular patterns. A "soft tissue window" would
     help evaluate the fat pad signs and surrounding soft tissues.

  C. CROP TO ANATOMICAL CONTENT:
     The image should be cropped to remove the empty black region on the
     left side (approximately the leftmost 50% of the image). This would
     effectively double the usable resolution for the anatomical content.

  D. EDGE ENHANCEMENT:
     Mild unsharp masking or edge enhancement could help delineate cortical
     margins and detect subtle step-offs or fracture lines.

  E. INVERSION (if not already inverted):
     Confirm that the image display convention is correct (bone = white,
     air = black). The data confirms this is the case, so no inversion is
     needed.

  F. LINE PROFILE ANALYSIS:
     Tracing line profiles along the expected cortical margins of the ulna
     and radius would help detect subtle cortical irregularities that might
     be missed by visual inspection.
