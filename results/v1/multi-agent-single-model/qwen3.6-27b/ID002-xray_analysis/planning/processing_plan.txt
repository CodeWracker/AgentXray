STEP 1 — CROP TO ANATOMICAL CONTENT

STEP 2 — CLAHE CONTRAST ENHANCEMENT
================================================================================
Output:          images/02_clahe_enhanced.png
Analysis:        CLAHE on cropped grayscale image. Clip limit=2.0, tile size=8x8
                 (in 9-pixel blocks). Also produce global linear contrast stretch
                 (p2 to p98).
Question:        Can we recover visibility of low-contrast structures (fat pads,
                 trabecular patterns, interosseous membrane)?
Reason:          78.8% of pixels are below intensity 30. Dynamic range is heavily
                 compressed. CLAHE adapts locally to each region.
Supporting:      Agents 01, 02, 03, 04, 05 (5/6)
Opposing:        None
Artifacts:       CLAHE may amplify noise if clip limit is too high. Tile size
                 too small creates visible tile boundaries. Over-enhancement
                 may create misleading structures.
Expected value:  HIGH — critical for soft tissue and fat pad assessment.

================================================================================
STEP 3 — DUAL WINDOW/LEVEL RENDERING
================================================================================
Output:          images/03_bone_window.png (bone detail)
                 images/03_soft_tissue_window.png (soft tissue detail)
Analysis:        Two independently windowed versions:
                 (a) BONE WINDOW: Map intensity [150, 254] linearly to [0, 255].
                 (b) SOFT TISSUE WINDOW: Map intensity [10, 130] linearly to [0, 255].
                 Both applied to cropped image.
Question:        Can we simultaneously assess cortical bone detail and soft tissue?
Reason:          Original image maps full 0-254 range, compressing both bone
                 and soft tissue. Separate windows optimize each tissue type.
Supporting:      Agents 01, 02, 03, 04, 06 (5/6)
Opposing:        None
Artifacts:       Hard clipping at window boundaries may create false uniformity.
                 Bone window loses all soft tissue information.
Expected value:  HIGH — standard radiographic practice.

================================================================================
STEP 4 — EDGE DETECTION / CORTICAL OUTLINE
================================================================================
Output:          images/04_edge_detection.png
Analysis:        Canny edge detection on bone-windowed image. Gaussian
                 pre-smoothing (sigma=1.5), Canny thresholds (low=20, high=80).
                 Semi-transparent edge overlay on original cropped image.
Question:        Are there cortical disruptions, step-offs, or fracture lines?
Reason:          Edge detection highlights intensity discontinuities. Cortical
                 bone edges are sharp; fracture lines create additional edges.
Supporting:      Agents 01, 02, 03, 04, 06 (5/6)
Opposing:        None
Artifacts:       Canny may detect trabecular patterns as edges. Noise may
                 create spurious edge fragments. Edge gaps may miss actual
                 cortical disruptions.
Expected value:  MODERATE-HIGH — supports fracture screening.

STEP 5 — FAT PAD ASSESSMENT (ENHANCED)
================================================================================
Output:          images/05_fat_pad_enhanced.png

                 (olecranon fossa) fat pad regions from soft-tissue window.

                 visibility.
Supporting:      Agents 01, 03, 04, 05 (4/6)
Opposing:        None

Expected value:  HIGH — critical for occult fracture detection.

================================================================================
STEP 6 — RADIAL HEAD DETAILED ANALYSIS
================================================================================
Output:          images/06_radial_head_roi.png
Analysis:        Tight ROI crop on radial head region. Mild unsharp masking
                 (kernel=5, amount=0.5). Edge tracing of cortical outline.
                 Smoothness analysis of radial head contour.
Question:        Is there a radial head fracture (most common elbow fracture)?
Reason:          Radial head fractures are frequently subtle on lateral view.
                 Dedicated ROI with sharpening may reveal occult cortical
                 step-off or lucency.
Supporting:      Agents 01, 02, 03, 05 (4/6)
Opposing:        None
Artifacts:       Unsharp masking creates halos at edges that may mimic
                 fracture lines. Tight ROI may miss adjacent pathology.
Expected value:  MODERATE-HIGH — targets most common elbow fracture.

================================================================================
STEP 7 — LINE PROFILE ANALYSIS
================================================================================
Output:          images/07_line_profiles.png
Analysis:        Intensity profiles along anterior humeral cortex, posterior
                 olecranon cortex, and radial head/neck. Compute raw profiles,

                 Visualize as overlaid plots on the cropped image.
Question:        Are there quantitative cortical irregularities along expected
                 smooth bony margins?

Opposing:        None

Expected value:  MODERATE — quantitative supplement to visual assessment.
