================================================================================
SEVERE limitations that constrain the reliability of any visual assessment:

(a) FORMAT DEGRADATION:
    - PNG format discards all DICOM metadata (patient info, acquisition
      parameters, window/level settings, modality LUT, etc.)
    - No way to verify the intended display LUT or inversion direction
    - Resolution metadata (pixel spacing) is lost, preventing measurement
      of anatomical dimensions in real-world units

(b) 8-BIT QUANTIZATION:
    - Original DICOM data is typically 12-16 bit
    - Converting to 8-bit PNG causes severe loss of dynamic range
    - Subtle density differences (critical for detecting subtle pathology)
      are collapsed by the 256-level quantization
    - The extreme histogram skew (P75 = 8, P99 = 240) shows that the
      windowing used for the 8-bit conversion is poorly matched to the
      data distribution

(c) RESOLUTION:
    - 1600x1280 is adequate for screen review but below the resolution of
      typical digital radiography detectors (often 3000x4000+)

      detail (small nodules, hairline fractures) may be lost

    - ~75% of the image area is empty background
    - The anatomical content occupies only ~47% of the width and appears
      to extend to the image boundary on the right
    - The content may be truncated at the right edge (peak brightness at

(f) ROTATION/ORIENTATION:

(g) ARTIFACT POTENTIAL:

    - No way to assess for motion artifact, scatter, or beam-hardening
