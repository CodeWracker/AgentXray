 2.2  CONTRAST ENHANCEMENT / CLAHE [UNANIMOUS — 6/6 agents]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Rating:  STRONGLY JUSTIFIED

  Rationale: The histogram is heavily skewed — 75-79% of all pixels have
  values 0-8 (background), and the clinically relevant anatomical content
  (soft tissue through cortical bone) is compressed into approximately
  values 20-254. Contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalization (CLAHE)
  would redistribute intensity locally, revealing soft-tissue detail
  (fat pads, muscle boundaries) and trabecular bone pattern.

  Implementation consensus:
  - Apply CLAHE with clip limit 2.0-4.0 (start conservative).
  - Tile size: 32x32 to 64x64 pixels (larger tiles reduce block artifacts;
    64x64 is preferred for this image scale).
  - Apply to grayscale channel only (all three RGB channels are identical).
  - Generate both a moderate and aggressive version for comparison.
  - Also produce a simple global linear contrast stretch (mapping 2nd to
    98th percentile of non-background pixels to 0-255) as a baseline.

  Caveats agreed upon:
  - CLAHE amplifies noise. In soft-tissue regions where signal is weak,
    noise amplification could create spurious structures that mimic fat
    pads or soft-tissue abnormalities.
  - Tile boundary artifacts ("seams") can appear if the tile size is too
    small. Visual inspection for seams is mandatory.
  - CLAHE is a hypothesis generator, NOT a diagnostic tool. Any finding
    on the CLAHE-enhanced image must be verified against the original.

  Resolution:  APPROVED as Step 2 of the processing pipeline.

 2.3  DUAL WINDOW/LEVEL ADJUSTMENT [5/6 agents — all except Agent_05,
        who proposed a general histogram-based approach instead]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Rating:  STRONGLY JUSTIFIED

  Rationale: Bone and soft tissue have fundamentally different density
  ranges. A single window cannot optimize both simultaneously. A "bone
  window" (narrow, centered on cortical/trabecular bone density,
  approximately 150-254 in the original scale) maximizes cortical margin
  visibility for fracture detection. A "soft tissue window" (wider,
  centered on soft tissue density, approximately 20-130) maximizes fat
  pad and muscle boundary visibility for occult fracture assessment.

  Implementation consensus:
  - Bone window: map [150, 254] to [0, 255], clip below 150 to black.
  - Soft tissue window: map [20, 130] to [0, 255], clip below 20 to
    black, above 130 to white.
  - Apply windows to the CROPPED, contrast-stretched image (not the raw
    original), after Steps 1-2.
  - Save both renderings as separate outputs.

  Agent_05's histogram-based approach (compute percentiles p1, p5, p95,
  p99 of the content-only histogram, then derive optimal windows) is
  a refinement of this proposal. The council adopts Agent_05's method
  for determining the exact window boundaries rather than the hardcoded
  ranges above.

  Caveats agreed upon:
  - Narrow windowing clips information outside the window range.
  - Soft tissue window will render bone as featureless white silhouettes.
  - The 8-bit quantization means some information was lost in the
    original DICOM-to-PNG conversion; windowing redistributes but does
    not recover information.

  Resolution:  APPROVED as Step 3 of the processing pipeline.

 2.4  EDGE ENHANCEMENT / CORTICAL ANALYSIS [5/6 agents]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Rating:  JUSTIFIED with CAVEATS

  Rationale: Cortical bone margins are the primary visual target for
  fracture detection. Edge enhancement (Canny, Sobel, or unsharp masking)
  can reveal subtle cortical step-offs, disruptions, or angular deviations
  that are below the threshold of reliable visual perception in the
  original image.

  Implementation consensus:
  - Canny edge detection on the bone-windowed image (from Step 3).
  - Gaussian pre-smoothing (sigma=1.5) before edge detection.
  - Canny thresholds: lower=20, upper=80 (on the bone-windowed,
    contrast-stretched image).
  - Unsharp masking as an alternative: sigma=1.5-2.0, amount=0.5-1.0.
  - Render result as semi-transparent overlay on the original grayscale.

  Caveats agreed upon:
  - Edge detection produces false positives from soft-tissue boundaries,
    noise, and normal anatomical landmarks (trochlear notch, olecranon
    fossa, coronoid fossa).
  - Unsharp masking creates "halo" artifacts (dark rings around bright
    edges) that can mimic cortical step-offs — the most common source
    of false-positive fracture detection.
  - The olecranon fossa, coronoid fossa, and trochlear notch have NORMAL
    cortical discontinuities that will be flagged as "breaks" but are
    anatomical, not pathological.
  - Every flagged discontinuity must be manually verified against the
    original unprocessed image.

  Resolution:  APPROVED as Step 4, with mandatory validation against the
               original image.

 2.5  FAT PAD ASSESSMENT [4/6 agents — Agents 01, 03, 04, 05; Agent_02
        addressed it implicitly through soft tissue windowing; Agent_06
        did not explicitly propose it]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Rating:  HIGHLY JUSTIFIED — CRITICAL FOR ELBOW IMAGING

  Rationale: The fat pad signs are the most sensitive indirect
  radiographic markers of occult elbow fracture. On a lateral view:

  - ANTERIOR FAT PAD: Normally barely visible or invisible. Elevation into
    a triangular shape ("sail sign") indicates joint effusion, which in a
    trauma setting is highly predictive of an occult fracture (especially
    radial head or supracondylar).

  - POSTERIOR FAT PAD: NEVER normally visible. Its presence is pathognomonic
    for intra-articular pathology and, in the trauma setting, virtually
    always indicates an occult fracture.

  The lateral view is the OPTIMAL projection for fat pad assessment, making
  this analysis particularly valuable for this image.

  Implementation consensus:
  - Use the soft-tissue windowed image (Step 3) as the primary view.
  - Define anterior ROI in the coronoid fossa region.
  - Define posterior ROI in the olecranon fossa region.
  - Apply targeted CLAHE (smaller tile, moderate clip) to each ROI for
    enhanced visibility.
  - Assess anterior fat pad for sail sign (elevation above normal plane).
  - Assess posterior fat pad for any visibility at all.
  - Measure soft tissue thickness anterior vs. posterior for swelling.

  Caveats agreed upon:
  - A negative posterior fat pad sign does NOT rule out fracture.
  - The anterior fat pad may be visible normally; the distinction between
    "visible" and "elevated" is subtle and requires careful judgment.
  - Contrast enhancement in these small ROIs may amplify noise to create
    false lucent areas that resemble fat pads.
  - The olecranon fossa itself is a radiolucent depression that can be
    confused with a posterior fat pad.

  Resolution:  APPROVED as Step 5, treated as a high-priority diagnostic
               analysis given its clinical significance.

 2.6  RADIAL HEAD DETAILED ANALYSIS [4/6 agents]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Rating:  HIGHLY JUSTIFIED

  Rationale: Radial head fractures are the most common adult elbow
  fracture (5-15% of all adult fractures) and are notoriously difficult
  to detect on a single lateral view. A nondisplaced (Mason type I)
  fracture may show only a subtle cortical step-off or hairline lucency.

  Implementation consensus:
  - Crop a tight ROI around the radial head and neck region.
  - Apply unsharp masking (mild: sigma=1.0-1.5, amount=0.5-0.8) for
    cortical sharpening.
  - Trace the expected circular/oval outline of the radial head cortex
    using edge detection (Canny).
  - Compute smoothness of the traced outline; flag deviations from a
    smooth curve as potential step-offs.
  - Assess radial head to capitellum alignment.

  Caveats agreed upon:
  - This is the WEAK point of lateral-only imaging. A nondisplaced
    fracture with the fracture line perpendicular to the beam will be
    invisible. Absence of findings does NOT rule out radial head fracture.
  - Edge enhancement creates false step-offs at the radial head-neck
    junction (a normal anatomical transition).
  - Superimposition of the radial head over other structures in the
    lateral view complicates isolated cortical tracing.

  Resolution:  APPROVED as Step 6, understood as supplementary evidence
               to be interpreted in conjunction with fat pad findings.

SECTION 3 — ARTIFACT RISKS FROM PROCESSING
================================================================================

The council identified the following specific artifact risks that must be
mitigated during processing:

  RISK 1: CLAHE NOISE AMPLIFICATION
  Severity: HIGH
  Mechanism: CLAHE with too-small tile size or too-high clip limit will
    amplify background noise, creating a grainy "salt-and-pepper" appearance
    that can be mistaken for cortical irregularity or trabecular disruption.
  Mitigation: Use tile size >= 64x64 pixels. Clip limit <= 4.0. Always
    compare CLAHE findings against the original.

  RISK 2: EDGE ENHANCEMENT FALSE FRACTURE LINES
  Severity: HIGH
  Mechanism: Unsharp masking creates "halo" artifacts — dark rings on one
    side and bright rings on the other of every edge. These halos can mimic
    cortical step-offs or hairline fracture lines.
  Mitigation: Use mild unsharp masking parameters (sigma 1.5-2.0, amount
    0.5-1.0). Any apparent fracture visible only on the sharpened image
    and not on the original is a false positive.

  RISK 3: UPSCALING DOES NOT ADD REAL RESOLUTION
  Severity: MODERATE
  Mechanism: Interpolation (bilinear, bicubic, Lanczos) creates new pixel
    values by mathematical averaging of existing pixels. No new information
    is added. Sub-pixel detail inferred from an upscaled image is illusory.
  Mitigation: Perform all quantitative analysis on the cropped image at its
    native resolution. Use upscaling only for display.

  RISK 4: THRESHOLDING MAY MISS SUBTLE STRUCTURES
  Severity: MODERATE
  Mechanism: Binary thresholding (e.g., for bone mask extraction) will
    exclude pixels at intermediate intensity values. Thin cortical bone,
    trabecular bone, and early lytic lesions may fall below the threshold
    and be excluded from analysis.
  Mitigation: Use multiple thresholds and compare results. Never rely on a
    single binary mask for diagnostic conclusions.

  RISK 5: WINDOWING ARTIFACTS AT INTENSITY BOUNDARIES
  Severity: MODERATE
  Mechanism: Narrow windowing clips values outside the window range to
    black or white. At the clipping boundary, small intensity differences
    that were present in the original are lost, potentially creating
    artificial "steps" or "bands" in the displayed image.
  Mitigation: Use multiple window settings and cross-validate findings.

  RISK 6: ROTATION INTERPOLATION ARTIFACTS
  Severity: LOW
  Mechanism: Image rotation requires resampling, which introduces
    interpolation artifacts. These are cosmetically visible but generally
    do not create misleading diagnostic features at mild rotation angles.
  Mitigation: Restrict rotation to display purposes. Do not perform
    quantitative analysis on rotated images.

  RISK 7: LINE PROFILE PATH MISPLACEMENT
  Severity: MODERATE
  Mechanism: If a line profile does not perfectly follow the cortical
    margin (even a few pixels off), the intensity trace will show artificial
    dips where the line crosses the cortex at an oblique angle.
  Mitigation: Verify line placement visually on the original image. Use
    edge detection results to guide placement. Smooth profiles with a
    moving average (3-5 pixel window) before analysis.

SECTION 5 — PRESERVED DISAGREEMENTS
================================================================================

The council deliberately preserves the following disagreements rather than
forcing artificial consensus, as they represent legitimate areas of
uncertainty:

  DISAGREEMENT 1: Pediatric vs. Adult Patient
  Status: UNRESOLVED
  Details: Agent_05 observed what appears to be an open physis at the
    distal humerus, suggesting a pediatric patient. No other agent made
    this observation explicitly. If this is a pediatric patient, the
    anterior humeral line analysis (Step 8) and physis assessment take on
    much higher clinical priority, as supracondylar fractures and
    Salter-Harris injuries are common in children.
  Implication: The processing plan includes both analyses. Their relative
    priority should be adjusted if the patient is confirmed to be pediatric.

  DISAGREEMENT 2: True Lateral vs. Oblique Projection
  Status: UNRESOLVED
  Details: Agents 01, 03, and 05 described the image as a "true lateral"
    view. Agent_04 noted structural drift and non-standard orientation.
    Agent_06 noted a 142.6-degree principal axis, suggesting possible
    rotation. If the view is not a true lateral (i.e., the forearm is
    rotated even slightly from the lateral plane), the anterior humeral
    line analysis (Step 8) is invalid, and the interosseous space
    measurement (Step 12) is unreliable.
  Implication: The superimposition of the humeral condyles should be
    checked as the criterion for a true lateral. If they are not
    perfectly superimposed, the view is oblique, and analyses depending
    on true lateral geometry should be flagged as approximate.

  DISAGREEMENT 3: Value of FFT/Frequency-Domain Analysis
  Status: MAINTAINED
  Details: The council rated FFT analysis as "low yield" (Section 2.9),
    but Agents 01, 02, 03, and 04 argued for its inclusion as a quality-
    assurance check. The council preserves this as a legitimate minority
    position: FFT analysis could detect periodic artifacts that are
    invisible in the spatial domain, and its absence leaves a small gap
    in the quality assurance pipeline.

  DISAGREEMENT 4: Laterality (Right vs. Left Elbow)
  Status: UNRESOLVED
  Details: Agent_01 and Agent_03 suggested "right elbow" based on the

    laterality marker (R/L) is present in the image. Without DICOM

  Implication: All findings should be reported without assumed laterality,

SECTION 6 — UNIFIED PROCESSING PLAN
================================================================================

Based on the deliberation above, the council adopts the following ordered
processing pipeline:

  STEP 0  [PRELIMINARY — runs in parallel with Step 1]
          Histogram and density analysis of the content region.
          Determines optimal window parameters for Step 3.
          Agents responsible: 01, 02, 04, 05, 06 (all proposed this)

  STEP 1  [FOUNDATIONAL]
          Crop to anatomical content, removing black background margin.
          Preserve small margin to avoid edge truncation.
          Convert to single-channel grayscale (lossless, since R=G=B).
          Agents responsible: 01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 06 (all proposed this)

  STEP 2  [FOUNDATIONAL]
          CLAHE contrast enhancement on the cropped image.
          Parameters: clip limit 2.0, tile size 64x64.
          Also produce a global linear contrast stretch (p2 to p98).
          Agents responsible: 01, 02, 03, 04, 05 (all proposed this)

  STEP 3  [DISPLAY/ANALYSIS]
          Dual window/level rendering:
            (a) Bone window — derived from Step 0 histogram analysis.
            (b) Soft tissue window — derived from Step 0 histogram analysis.
          Agents responsible: 01, 02, 03, 04, 06 (proposed this);
                              Agent 05's percentile method for boundaries

  STEP 4  [FRACTURE SCREENING]
          Edge detection (Canny) on the bone-windowed image.
          Gaussian pre-smoothing (sigma=1.5), then Canny (low=20, high=80).
          Morphological filtering to isolate cortical edges.
          Semi-transparent overlay on original for visual review.
          Agents responsible: 01, 02, 03, 04, 06 (proposed this)

  STEP 5  [OCCLT FRACTURE DETECTION — HIGH PRIORITY]
          Fat pad assessment on the soft-tissue windowed image.
          Anterior ROI (coronoid fossa): assess for sail sign.
          Posterior ROI (olecranon fossa): assess for any visibility.
          Targeted CLAHE on each ROI for enhanced visibility.
          Agents responsible: 01, 03, 04, 05 (proposed this)

  STEP 6  [TARGETED FRACTURE DETECTION]
          Radial head dedicated analysis.
          Tight ROI crop, mild unsharp masking, edge tracing.
          Smoothness analysis of cortical outline.
          Alignment assessment (radial head to capitellum).
          Agents responsible: 01, 02, 03, 05 (proposed this)

  STEP 7  [QUANTITATIVE CORTICAL ASSESSMENT]
          Line profile analysis along cortical margins.
          Use Step 4 edges to guide line placement.
          Compute raw profiles, first and second derivatives.
          Flag anomalies (second derivative > 2 SD from mean).
          Agents responsible: 01, 02, 04 (proposed line profiles);
                              Agent 03 (gradient analysis);
                              Agent 05 (transect profiles)

  STEP 8  [ALIGNMENT ASSESSMENT]
          Anterior humeral line analysis.
          Trace anterior distal humeral cortex, project through capitellum.
          Assess which third of capitellum is intersected.
          Agents responsible: 03, 05 (proposed this)

SECTION 7 — VALIDATION PRINCIPLES
================================================================================

The council establishes the following validation principles that apply to
ALL processing steps:

  PRINCIPLE 1: ORIGINAL AS GROUND TRUTH
  No finding on any processed, enhanced, or windowed image is considered
  credible until it can be verified on the original unmodified image.
  Processed images are hypothesis generators; the original is the arbiter.

  PRINCIPLE 2: CROSS-VALIDATION ACROSS METHODS
  When multiple independent methods (e.g., edge detection, line profiles,
  and visual inspection) converge on the same finding at the same
  anatomical location, confidence in that finding is substantially
  increased. Disagreement between methods flags the finding for careful
  review.

  PRINCIPLE 3: ARTIFACT AWARENESS
  Every processing step has known artifact modes (Section 3 above). Any
  finding that matches a known artifact pattern (halo from unsharp masking,
  noise amplification from CLAHE, edge discontinuity from Canny) must be
  treated as suspicious until proven otherwise.

  PRINCIPLE 4: 8-BIT LIMITATION ACKNOWLEDGMENT
  The image is an 8-bit PNG converted from an unknown original (likely
  12-16 bit DICOM). Some information has been irreversibly lost.
  Quantitative density measurements are approximate at best. The council
  recommends obtaining the original DICOM file if available.

  PRINCIPLE 5: SINGLE-VIEW LIMITATION
  This is a single lateral view without an accompanying AP view. Many
  pathologies (especially radial head fractures with the fracture line
  perpendicular to the beam, and medial/lateral epicondyle fractures)
  are invisible on a lateral-only study. A negative finding does NOT
  rule out fracture. Clinical correlation is essential.

================================================================================
SECTION 8 — RECOMMENDATIONS BEYOND IMAGE PROCESSING
================================================================================

The council notes the following recommendations that extend beyond the
scope of image processing but are relevant to the diagnostic assessment:

  1. Obtain the original DICOM file. The 8-bit PNG conversion has
     irreversibly lost dynamic range and all metadata (patient ID,
     laterality, acquisition parameters, kVp, mAs).

  2. Obtain an AP view of the elbow for orthogonal assessment. Many
     fractures (especially radial head and medial epicondyle fractures)
     are invisible on lateral-only imaging.

  3. Obtain an oblique view (or Y-view) for radial head assessment.
     The radial head is optimally visualized on an oblique projection
     that separates it from the superimposed capitellum.

  4. Include clinical context: patient age (to determine if physeal
     analysis is relevant), mechanism of injury, and location of
     tenderness.

  5. If clinical suspicion remains high despite negative imaging,
     consider CT or MRI for occult fracture detection.
