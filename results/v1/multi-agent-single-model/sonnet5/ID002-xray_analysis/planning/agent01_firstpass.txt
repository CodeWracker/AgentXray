SECTION B — INDEPENDENT PROCESSING PROPOSALS

PROPOSAL 1 — Soft-tissue/bone dual-range windowing (adaptive contrast
re-mapping split into two intensity bands)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question it tries to answer:
  Are the elbow fat pads (soft tissue immediately adjacent to the distal
  humerus) normal or displaced/effaced, and is there a true joint effusion
  sign that isn't visible at the image's current single global contrast
  setting?

Why the method may help:
  Standard radiographic display uses one contrast curve for the whole
  image, but bone (very high signal) and the fat pad/soft tissue planes
  (much lower signal, narrow dynamic range) compete for the same 0–255
  display range. Splitting the intensity histogram and applying a
  separate, more aggressive contrast stretch to the low/mid-gray soft-
  tissue band (while compressing the already-saturated bone band) should
  make faint fat-pad stripes and any effusion-related displacement far
  easier to see without altering the underlying pixel data's diagnostic
  meaning.

What I expect it to reveal:
  Either confirmation that the fat pads sit in their normal position
  (supporting the "unremarkable" impression from Section A), or a
  posteriorly displaced/elevated fat pad that would raise suspicion for an
  occult fracture not visible as a bone line.

Artifacts / misleading effects:
  Aggressive local contrast stretching can create false "halos" or banding
  at tissue-bone boundaries that look like discrete lines but are just
  processing artifacts (should not be mistaken for periosteal reaction or
  fracture lines). Over-stretching may also amplify noise in the
  soft-tissue band, mimicking a fat-pad stripe where none exists.

How to compare with the original:
  View side-by-side at matched crop/zoom on the elbow region; any
  soft-tissue finding flagged in the processed image should be
  cross-checked against the original at native contrast to confirm it
  isn't purely a stretching artifact, and ideally checked for anatomic
  plausibility (expected fat-pad location relative to the distal humerus).

PROPOSAL 2 — Unsharp-mask / high-frequency edge enhancement restricted to
the cortical bone outline
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question it tries to answer:
  Is the cortex of the radius, ulna, and distal humerus truly continuous
  along its entire visible length, or is there a subtle step-off, buckle,
  or hairline lucency that is being smoothed over by the image's apparent
  moderate resolution/softening noted in Section A?

Why the method may help:
  Unsharp masking amplifies local intensity gradients (edges) while
  leaving broad, low-frequency regions largely unchanged. A fracture,
  cortical buckle, or subtle displacement typically manifests as a
  small, local discontinuity in the otherwise smooth, continuous bright
  cortical line — exactly the kind of high-frequency feature this
  technique is designed to accentuate — so it should make a real break
  easier to distinguish from normal bone-edge blur.

What I expect it to reveal:
  A confirmation that the cortical outlines of both forearm bones and the
  humeral segment are unbroken (supporting the unremarkable read), or the
  emergence of a discrete step/lucent gap that was too subtle to register
  at normal contrast — particularly relevant to the "soft-looking" humeral
  segment flagged in Section A, point 4.

Artifacts / misleading effects:
  Edge enhancement can create ringing/overshoot artifacts along strong
  edges (a thin dark or bright fringe running parallel to the true cortex)
  that could be misread as a periosteal reaction, a second cortical line,
  or a hairline fracture. It can also exaggerate normal anatomic edges
  (nutrient canals, nutrient foramina, vascular grooves, nutrient artery
  markings) into looking like discontinuities.

How to compare with the original:
  Overlay or toggle between original and enhanced versions at the same
  zoom level along the full length of each bone; any suspected
  discontinuity found in the enhanced image must be checked for
  correspondence with an actual signal change (not just an enhancement
  fringe) in the unprocessed original before being treated as a real
  finding.

PROPOSAL 3 — Targeted magnified crop of the elbow joint complex (with
mild adaptive contrast, no other filtering)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question it tries to answer:
  Does closer, larger-scale inspection of the elbow articulation itself
  (distal humerus / radial head / coronoid / olecranon) change my
  impression from Section A of an unremarkable joint, given that this is
  both the highest clinical-yield region and the most spatially compressed
  part of the current whole-limb framing?

Why the method may help:
  This is the simplest possible intervention — the current image frames a
  large amount of the limb, so the elbow joint itself occupies a
  relatively small number of pixels. A straightforward crop-and-upscale
  (rather than a complex filter) removes the distraction of the rest of
  the limb and lets fine joint-space and articular-surface detail be
  evaluated at effectively higher apparent resolution, which is a
  low-risk way to double-check a region rather than assume the whole-image
  view was sufficient.

What I expect it to reveal:
  Better visibility of the radiocapitellar and ulnohumeral joint space
  width/congruency, the radial neck contour, and whether any subtle
  irregularity exists at the coronoid or olecranon that was not clearly
  resolved in the full-frame view.

Artifacts / misleading effects:
  Simple pixel upscaling (without true higher-resolution source data) can
  introduce interpolation blur or blockiness that might be mistaken for
  texture/trabecular changes; it creates the appearance of more detail
  without actually adding new diagnostic information beyond what's in the
  original pixels, so conclusions should stay conservative.

How to compare with the original:
  Confirm that every feature seen in the magnified crop can be located and
  is consistent with the same region in the unmagnified original — the
  crop is an inspection aid, not a new data source, so it should never be
  used alone to assert a finding that isn't at least plausible in the
  original framing.
