PURPOSE: This is a side-by-side comparison (LEFT: original elbow-joint crop; RIGHT: same crop after CLAHE local adaptive contrast enhancement) intended to test whether an anterior or posterior elbow fat-pad / joint-effusion sign (e.g. a "sail sign" — an elevated triangular soft-tissue lucency adjacent to the coronoid or olecranon fossa) becomes visible or more conspicuous with enhanced local contrast, which would suggest occult intra-articular effusion/fracture.

OBSERVATIONS: Both panels show a flexed lateral elbow with the distal humerus (capitellum/trochlea) at upper left, radial head/neck and coronoid process of the ulna below, and radius/ulna shafts descending to the lower left. The soft tissue anterior/inferior to the joint (the large gray field to the right of the bones) is homogeneous and gradually darkens toward the axilla/antecubital region in both panels; there is no discrete triangular or curvilinear lucent structure standing off the anterior or posterior humeral cortex in either panel. The CLAHE panel (right) markedly increases visible trabecular texture in the humerus, radius, and ulna, sharpens the cortical margins, and adds fine mottled/granular texture to the soft-tissue field and skin surface, but does not reveal any new discrete soft-tissue structure that isn't present as a smooth gradient in the original. A faint curvilinear skin-fold line is visible near the top of the frame (arm contour) in both panels, unchanged in course between them.

RELEVANT FINDINGS: None seen. No anterior or posterior fat-pad elevation, no "sail sign," and no focal soft-tissue lucency/density asymmetry is identified in either the original or CLAHE-enhanced panel.

RELATION TO ORIGINAL IMAGE: Directly consistent — this crop is derived from the same source (matches 01_elbow_joint_crop.png / the elbow region of 00_original.png). The absence of a fat-pad sign is the same in the unprocessed left half as in the enhanced right half, satisfying the requirement that any reported fat-pad finding be at least faintly visible on the original; here there is no finding to report on either side.

POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: CLAHE introduces amplified graininess/noise texture throughout the soft tissue and increases local contrast around normal trabecular bone pattern and skin folds, which could, under lower scrutiny, be mistaken for an abnormal soft-tissue interface — but no such candidate line rises to the level of a discrete, elevated fat-pad-type structure here. The over-sharpened trabecular pattern in bone is a typical CLAHE contrast artifact and should not be over-read as trabecular pathology.
