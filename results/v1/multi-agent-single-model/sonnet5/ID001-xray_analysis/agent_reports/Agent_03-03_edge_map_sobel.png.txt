PURPOSE: A full-image Sobel gradient-magnitude edge map (inverted so edges are dark on a
light background), generated because this agent (among others) proposed an edge-enhancement
analysis to help trace cortical continuity through the overlapping bone margins near the
ankle mortise, where it is hard to visually follow a single cortex through superimposed
structures on the plain lateral view.

OBSERVATIONS: The full leg/ankle/foot is rendered as a network of thin dark lines on a
near-white background. The tibial shaft is clearly represented by two roughly parallel
long curved lines (its medial and lateral cortical margins), continuous from the top of
the frame down to the ankle mortise. A thinner, less continuous pair of lines lateral to
the tibia corresponds to the fibula; it becomes harder to trace as a distinct line once it
approaches and overlaps the talus/calcaneus region. At the ankle, multiple curved lines
converge and cross, corresponding to the tibial plafond, talar dome, and subtalar joint
facets. The calcaneus and talus interior show a dense mesh of fine crossing lines,
consistent with trabecular bone edges (and likely also image noise) rather than a single
discrete line. The forefoot/metatarsal bases show parallel paired lines per bone, again
consistent with normal cortical margins. Faint, very-low-contrast vertical striping is
visible across the light background, corresponding to the banding artifact also seen in the
original and in image 04.

RELEVANT FINDINGS (descriptive, not diagnostic): No single line in the tibial, fibular,
talar, or calcaneal cortex shows an abrupt discontinuity, sharp angular deviation, or a
short line segment disconnected from the rest of the cortical contour that would suggest a
fracture line distinct from normal joint margins or bone-on-bone overlap. The lines at the
ankle mortise and subtalar joint are dense and crossing due to genuine anatomic
superimposition on a lateral view, which limits how confidently any single joint surface
can be isolated. The fine internal mesh within the calcaneus (corresponding to the
lower-density rounded area noted in image 04) does not stand out here as a distinctly
bounded loop separate from normal trabecular lines — i.e., this edge map does not add
strong independent support for, nor against, that area being a true finding.

RELATION TO ORIGINAL IMAGE: Checked directly against
/home/ralph/projects/ufsc/pablo-xray-tests-claude/ID001-xray_analysis/images/ID001-xray_original.png.
Every major line structure in the edge map (tibial and fibular shaft cortices, ankle
mortise contour, calcaneus and talus outlines, metatarsal bases) corresponds to a real
gray-value boundary visible in the original grayscale image. No line pattern in the edge
map appears to introduce a structure that is absent from the source image; the edge map is
consistent with, and traceable to, the original.

POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: Sobel gradient edge maps respond to any sharp gray-value transition,
including image noise, quantum mottle, and the pre-existing vertical banding artifact — so
some of the fine crossing lines inside the calcaneus/talus almost certainly reflect noise
rather than true trabecular structure, and this cannot be reliably separated from real
anatomy using this image alone. The edge map also equally emphasizes normal anatomic
overlap lines (e.g., where fibula crosses talus) and true cortical margins, so a dense
cluster of lines at the mortise should not by itself be read as abnormal — it is expected
overlap for this projection. Thin or subtle true fracture lines could, in principle, be
under-represented if their gradient magnitude is lower than surrounding noise/texture
edges.
