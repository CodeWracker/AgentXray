intended to test whether local/adaptive contrast enhancement reveals subtle cortical
breaks, joint-space irregularities, or trabecular detail that were not clearly

OBSERVATIONS: Same overall anatomy and framing as the original (lateral distal
tibia/fibula, ankle, hindfoot/midfoot, same crop). Contrast is locally redistributed:
the calcaneus and talus show more visible internal texture/trabecular gradation than

streak/banding texture across the entire dark region, well beyond what was easily
seen before.
RELEVANT FINDINGS: No new focal lucency, sclerotic lesion, or discontinuous cortical
line is convincingly revealed — the ankle mortise, talus, and calcaneus trabecular
pattern still appear grossly continuous, now just more textured/visible. The main
"finding" from this image is about image quality rather than anatomy: the
background streaking is confirmed to be strong, structured (vertical banding),
and present across the whole non-anatomic region, which supports it being a
scan/digitization artifact rather than an anatomic signal.
RELATION TO ORIGINAL IMAGE: The overall bone shapes, alignment, and absence of an
obvious fracture are consistent with and traceable back to the original image —
CLAHE has not surfaced any discontinuity that isn't at least faintly present in the
source. The dramatically increased visibility of background banding is a direct,
expected consequence of amplifying local contrast in a low-contrast noisy region,
not a new anatomic observation.
POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: CLAHE is known to amplify existing noise, and here it clearly
amplified the background grain into obvious vertical banding — this should not be
mistaken for anatomic striation since it also fills the non-anatomic background.
Within the bone/soft-tissue regions, locally boosted texture could exaggerate minor
trabecular variation into something that looks more "notable" than it truly is, and
edge/tile boundaries can introduce mild blotchy or halo-like contrast shifts near
strong bone/background transitions. Any candidate finding from this image should be
cross-checked against the original before being taken seriously.
