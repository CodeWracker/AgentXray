PROCESSING PLAN — ID003-xray.png
Synthesized by orchestrator from independent proposals of Agent_01–Agent_06 (all sonnet 5, single-model council).
Council method: 6 agents independently reviewed the original image (no cross-talk), then independently proposed
processing analyses. This plan groups their 27 raw proposals into non-redundant implementation items, ranked by
how many agents independently converged on the same underlying question. No agent explicitly opposed any
proposal; every agent flagged generic artifact-risk caveats for contrast/sharpening operations, which are carried
into "possible artifacts" below and must be respected during visual inspection (any finding visible only in a
processed image and not traceable, even faintly, in the original should be treated with skepticism).

================================================================================
1. OUTPUT: 01_fracture_zone_zoom_contrast.png
TRANSFORM: Crop forearm shaft around the candidate lucent line, ~4x upscale (LANCZOS), local min-max contrast
           stretch on the crop only.
PARAMETERS: crop box ~ (560,560)-(820,720) in original 1280x1600 px; scale factor 4x; percentile clip 1-99 for
            contrast stretch.
QUESTION: Is the transverse lucent line crossing the forearm shaft a true cortical fracture, or a nutrient
          foramen / projection artifact? What is its margin sharpness, cortex involvement, and any step-off?
REASON: This was the single most consistent finding across all six independent first-pass reviews.
AGENTS SUPPORTING: Agent_01, Agent_02, Agent_03, Agent_04, Agent_05, Agent_06 (unanimous)
AGENTS OPPOSING/QUESTIONING: None opposed; all cautioned that aggressive contrast stretching can exaggerate a
          normal nutrient canal or introduce false halos/ringing at high zoom.
POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: Contrast-stretch halos, interpolation ringing from upscaling, exaggeration of normal
          trabecular texture into a line-like pattern.
EXPECTED INFORMATIONAL VALUE: High — directly targets the top diagnostic question.

================================================================================
2. OUTPUT: 02_fracture_zone_edge_enhancement.png
TRANSFORM: Same crop as #1, unsharp-mask edge enhancement (high-pass emphasis) instead of plain contrast stretch.
PARAMETERS: unsharp radius ~3px, amount ~150%, threshold 3; applied to the same crop box as #1.
QUESTION: Are the margins of the candidate fracture line sharp/well-corticated (favors true fracture) or soft/
          gradual (favors motion blur, summation, or nutrient canal)?
REASON: Proposed independently by five of six agents as a complement to plain contrast stretching, specifically
        to judge margin character rather than just visibility.
AGENTS SUPPORTING: Agent_01, Agent_02, Agent_03, Agent_04, Agent_06
AGENTS OPPOSING/QUESTIONING: None opposed; all noted unsharp masking can create ringing/halo artifacts that
          mimic additional lines, and that normal cortex elsewhere should be checked as a sanity control.
POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: Ringing/halo along any high-contrast edge, including normal cortex, which could be
          mistaken for a second fracture line.
EXPECTED INFORMATIONAL VALUE: High — complements #1 for margin-sharpness judgment.

3. OUTPUT: 03_hand_region_deblur_contrast.png
TRANSFORM: Crop the hand/digit cluster, ~3x upscale, unsharp-mask sharpening + local contrast stretch.
PARAMETERS: crop box ~ (350,1000)-(850,1600); scale 3x; unsharp radius 2px amount 120%; percentile clip 1-99.
QUESTION: Does the blurred/overlapping digit cluster reflect motion blur over a normal clenched fist, or does it
          obscure a true phalangeal/metacarpal abnormality? Does it represent one hand or two superimposed hands?
REASON: Motion blur in the hand was independently flagged by all six agents as the second major image-quality
        limitation and source of diagnostic uncertainty.
AGENTS SUPPORTING: Agent_01, Agent_02, Agent_03, Agent_04, Agent_05, Agent_06 (unanimous)
AGENTS OPPOSING/QUESTIONING: None opposed; all cautioned that sharpening a genuinely motion-blurred region can
          hallucinate false edges/ringing that look like extra bone margins, and that true 2D deblurring cannot
          "unfold" genuinely superimposed structures.
POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: Hallucinated edges from deconvolution/sharpening of blurred, low-information data.
EXPECTED INFORMATIONAL VALUE: Moderate-high — best-effort clarification only, explicitly not a substitute for
          a repeat radiograph with an extended hand.

4. OUTPUT: 04_hand_region_edge_detection.png
TRANSFORM: Sobel/Canny-style gradient-magnitude map of the same hand crop as #3.
PARAMETERS: Canny thresholds auto-tuned (Otsu-based) on the crop; crop box same as #3.
QUESTION: How many genuinely distinct digit outlines can be traced, and does the hand-wrist junction represent
          one flexed hand or two overlapping hands (patient's own hand vs. a possible assisting/support hand)?
REASON: Two agents specifically flagged the "one hand vs. two hands" interpretive question as materially
        affecting how hand findings should be read, and edge/gradient mapping was proposed as the objective
        way to trace digit boundaries independent of blur-smoothed grayscale.
AGENTS SUPPORTING: Agent_03, Agent_06 (edge-detection specifically); Agent_01, Agent_04, Agent_05 independently
          proposed general hand sharpening covered partly by #3
AGENTS OPPOSING/QUESTIONING: None opposed; all edge-detection proposals warned that gradient filters are noise-
          sensitive and can invent spurious digit boundaries from soft-tissue folds.
POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: Spurious edges from noise/soft-tissue folds; doubled edges from real motion blur
          misread as two separate real structures.
EXPECTED INFORMATIONAL VALUE: Moderate — targets a specific, materially important interpretive ambiguity.

5. OUTPUT: 05_elbow_fatpad_zoom_contrast.png
TRANSFORM: Crop elbow joint region, upscale, soft-tissue-oriented windowing (gamma/levels tuned toward the
           lower-mid intensity range rather than bone-optimized).
PARAMETERS: crop box ~ (600,150)-(1050,430); scale 3x; gamma ~0.7 on normalized crop to lift mid/low tones.
QUESTION: Is there a fat-pad sign (anterior "sail sign" or visible posterior fat pad) suggesting an elbow joint
          effusion / occult intra-articular injury associated with the forearm finding?
REASON: Proposed independently by three agents given the flexed limb and the nearby forearm fracture candidate;
        pediatric forearm fractures are frequently associated with occult elbow injury.
AGENTS SUPPORTING: Agent_02, Agent_05, Agent_06
AGENTS OPPOSING/QUESTIONING: All three supporting agents themselves flagged that the elbow's atypical ~90-100°
          flexion (not a standard true lateral) can itself produce a false-positive fat-pad-like lucency from
          projection/rotation — this is a built-in caveat, not opposition, but it materially lowers confidence
          in any positive finding here.
POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: Projection-induced pseudo fat-pad appearance from non-standard flexion angle; over/under-
          brightening washing out or fabricating the sign.
EXPECTED INFORMATIONAL VALUE: Moderate — useful but low-confidence given non-standard positioning.

6. OUTPUT: 06_whole_image_clahe.png
TRANSFORM: Contrast-Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization over the whole image, with the near-black background
           capped/masked before equalization to avoid amplifying background noise.
PARAMETERS: CLAHE clipLimit=2.0, tileGridSize=8x8; background mask = pixels below ~5th percentile intensity
           held constant.
QUESTION: Is there low-contrast detail (soft-tissue swelling, subtle periosteal reaction, secondary lucent/
          sclerotic lines) anywhere in the image compressed out by the native exposure/windowing?
REASON: Proposed by three agents as a general-purpose visibility aid, given the wide dynamic range needed to
        cover both the well-penetrated shaft and the comparatively overexposed distal hand.
AGENTS SUPPORTING: Agent_01, Agent_03, Agent_04 (regional variant)
AGENTS OPPOSING/QUESTIONING: None opposed; all warned CLAHE can amplify quantum noise/compression artifacts
          into false texture or tile-boundary grid lines, and should be a visibility aid only, never sole
          evidence for a finding.
POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: Amplified noise/speckle in background and low-signal regions; tile-boundary artifacts;
          normal trabecular bone appearing falsely heterogeneous/mottled.
EXPECTED INFORMATIONAL VALUE: Moderate — general-purpose companion view, not targeted at one question.

7. OUTPUT: 07_full_forearm_edge_trace.png
TRANSFORM: Sobel gradient-magnitude edge map computed over the full limb (humerus through wrist), overlaid
           semi-transparently on the original grayscale.
PARAMETERS: Sobel kernel 3x3, magnitude normalized, overlay opacity ~40% on top of original.
QUESTION: Is the cortical margin continuous along the entire length of the humerus, radius, and ulna, or is
          there a break/step-off anywhere beyond the already-identified mid-shaft line?
REASON: Proposed by three agents as a full-length screening complement to the targeted fracture-zone crop, to
        avoid missing a second, less obvious injury elsewhere along the bone.
AGENTS SUPPORTING: Agent_01, Agent_04, Agent_05
AGENTS OPPOSING/QUESTIONING: None opposed; all warned edge filters respond to any strong gradient (soft-tissue
          overlap, skin folds, bone-on-bone crossing at the flexed joint and hand), producing false "breaks."
POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: False cortical-break appearance at genuine anatomic overlap zones (flexed elbow, hand-wrist
          junction), noise-driven spurious edges.
EXPECTED INFORMATIONAL VALUE: Moderate — screening value, overlay format makes artifacts easier to sanity-check
          against the underlying grayscale in the same view.
