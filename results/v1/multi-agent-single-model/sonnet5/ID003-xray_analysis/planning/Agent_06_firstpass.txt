4) IMAGE QUALITY LIMITATIONS
- Non-standard, non-orthogonal single view: the limb is bent within one exposure, combining elbow and wrist/hand regions at oblique angles to each other, which distorts normal anatomic relationships and complicates side-by-side comparison to standard AP/lateral references.
- Overlap: the hand overlaps the distal forearm/wrist, obscuring the wrist joint and carpal alignment; forearm bones (radius/ulna) overlap each other in places depending on true rotation, complicating fracture characterization.
- Possible motion blur: the hand's outline (fingertips, finger margins) looks slightly less sharp than the forearm shaft, suggesting patient motion during exposure — common in pediatric imaging without adequate immobilization.
- Exposure/contrast is not uniform: the proximal forearm/elbow area is well penetrated with good bone-soft tissue contrast, while the hand region appears comparatively denser/whiter with less internal trabecular detail visible, possibly due to differential thickness, exposure technique, or grasping/compression of tissues.
- No visible markers (L/R, technique, patient ID) in the visible crop, limiting confirmation of laterality and exam context.
- Only a single projection is available; standard orthopedic trauma workup requires at least two orthogonal views (e.g., AP and lateral) to confirm or exclude a fracture and assess displacement/angulation — this cannot be done from one image alone.
- Field of view cuts off part of the shoulder/torso on the right, and the top of the image includes a thin bright horizontal band (possible film/collimation edge or label strip) that is not diagnostic tissue.

5) REGIONS THAT DESERVE CLOSER INSPECTION
- The suspected fracture line in the forearm shaft (mid-to-distal ulna region) — highest priority for cropping/zooming and contrast enhancement.
- The wrist/carpal region under the overlapping hand, to check for a second injury site given that pediatric forearm trauma often involves both the fracture site and possible torus/buckle fracture near the wrist.
- The elbow physes and metaphyses, to exclude a subtle supracondylar or physeal injury given this is a pediatric patient (common in this age group).

Global filters applied to the whole image are less likely to help than localized enhancement of the fracture zone and the hand/wrist overlap zone. See proposals file for specifics.
