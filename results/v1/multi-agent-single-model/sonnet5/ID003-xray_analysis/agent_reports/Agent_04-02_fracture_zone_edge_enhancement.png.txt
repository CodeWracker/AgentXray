OBSERVATIONS: Two parallel long-bone shafts (ulna medially/left, radius laterally/right) fill the crop. On the ulna, a thin, mildly oblique lucent line crosses the full width of the shaft roughly at mid-height of the crop; after unsharp masking it appears crisp and dark against the surrounding cortex/medulla, with no visible gap or displacement of the two fragments. On the radius, at a slightly more proximal level, there is a distinct step in the lateral cortical contour with associated angulation of the shaft axis and a zone of jagged, irregular density just above the step (small radiating lucent/dense lines suggestive of a comminuted or oblique fracture apex). Both findings sit at approximately the same craniocaudal level, consistent with a paired both-bone forearm injury.
RELEVANT FINDINGS: (1) Ulna: sharply defined, thin oblique lucent line, non-displaced — margins are crisp, not blurred, after enhancement. (2) Radius: cortical discontinuity with a clear step-off/angulation and irregular jagged edge, also sharply defined. Both lines have high local edge contrast rather than the smooth, gradually-fading margins typical of blur or simple projectional summation.
RELATION TO ORIGINAL IMAGE: Directly compared against the corresponding region of images/00_original.png (cropped to the same anatomic level). Both the ulna hairline and the radius step-off/angulation are clearly visible in the original at the same location and relative morphology, confirming these are real underlying structures and not artifacts introduced solely by this processing step.
POSSIBLE ARTIFACTS: Unsharp masking can produce overshoot/ringing (thin bright or dark halos) at high-contrast boundaries, which may slightly exaggerate the apparent crispness or width of both lines and could contribute to some of the fine radiating marks seen at the radius fracture apex — these should not be over-read as definite comminution without checking the plain-contrast version. Enhancement can also amplify pre-existing image noise/grain as small spurious "cracks," so short, isolated hairline segments elsewhere in the crop not corroborated by the original should be treated with caution.
